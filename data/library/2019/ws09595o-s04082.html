The Riggs Report: Synagogue shooting prompts talk of more security spending

Gov. Newsom’s revised budget to contain significant increase

Updated: 9:01 AM PDT May 3, 2019Editorial Standards ⓘSACRAMENTO, Calif. (KCRA) —

Last weekend’s shooting at a San Diego County synagogue is prompting Gov. Gavin Newsom to look at a substantial increase in state money that’s allocated to provide security at places of worship across California.

Newsom said this week he’s proposing to set aside $15 million when his newly-revised budget proposal comes out later this month; money that would take the form of grants awarded to mosques, synagogues, private schools, health clinics and other nonprofit organizations.

Assembly Bill 1548 is the vehicle. It’s authored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-San Fernando Valley, a freshman legislator who is a chair of the Legislative Jewish Caucus. The bill would establish the California State Nonprofit Security Grant Program, which would require the California Office of Emergency Services to issue grants on a competitive basis to applicants, up to a maximum of $200,000 for each grant.

“The increase in hate-motivated violence and the shocking increase in the number of hate groups has made improving security at Jewish institutions, and institutions serving other communities, an urgent priority,” Gabriel said in comments accompanying the bill. “The Anti-Defamation League identified 1, 986 anti-Semitic incidents perpetrated throughout the United States in 2017. This is an increase of 57 percent over the preceding year. California had the second highest number of incidents (268) after New York.”

California has had a grant program in place since 2015 that is meant to supplement a federal grant program. AB 1548 would change the existing program by increasing the amount of funds an individual applicant could receive, and would also authorize the money to be used for the hiring of security guards.

Funding under the revised grant program could also be used to reinforce doors and gates, install better lighting and alarm systems, and other unspecified security improvements.

The measure has passed the Assembly Governmental Organization Committee and is now awaiting a vote in the Assembly Appropriations Committee.

Author Kevin Riggs is an Emmy winning former political reporter for KCRA-TV. He is now the station’s political analyst and is Senior Vice President at Randle Communications.
