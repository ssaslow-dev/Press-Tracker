BERNIE unveiling CLIMATE plan in CHICO — DNC gathers in SF — TRUMP rages at CA auto pact — KAMALA staffs up

By Jeremy B White; Carla Marinucci; Alexander Nieves; Maya Parthasarathy

THE BUZZ: With Democratic National Committee members gathering in San Francisco, Sen. Bernie Sanders is making his environmental pitch in a part of California with ample reason to worry about climate change.

There’s no more urgent manifestation of the spiraling climate crisis than devastating wildfires — a recent Public Policy Institute of California poll found unprecedented bipartisan concern about worsening blazes — and today Sanders is using the region charred by the Camp Fire as a backdrop to roll out his climate plan, one day after Gov. Gavin Newsom was in Paradise touring a school. POLITICO's Gavin Bade has the details.

California pols love national relevance, and Sanders’ trip underscores how the presidential primary is providing that — not just with candidates vying for votes and cash here, but also by spotlighting how California’s ambitious climate policies offer both a template and some cautionary tales nationally.

Newsom is fond of saying that California has long been pursuing its own Green New Deal. As California transitions toward the 100 percent renewable energy generation that Sanders’ plan similarly seeks and leads the way on tougher air standards, the state is also offering a taste of how that shift can fuel conflict with labor, spotlight the weaknesses of capand-trade and fan local government and big business opposition — witness cities pushing Newsom to slow a shift away from fossil fuels.

Enviros and DNC members will be using the SF gathering to push the party to hold a climate-focused debate — resistance to which is angering a green-minded bloc of the party base. California candidate and climate crusader Tom Steyer amplified that push yesterday, calling the DNC’s reluctance “frankly disturbing” in a statement yesterday.

After his town hall in Chico, Sanders will be rallying the faithful in Sacramento — part of a crush of Democratic candidates rolling through California this week. The Vermont senator retains plenty of support here (though not everyone is pleased with his choice of venue), and a new POLITICO/Morning Consult poll shows that he and former Veep Joe Biden — who’s skipping SF this week — are the best head-to-head bets against Trump, countering Sen. Kamala Harris’s case that she’s best equipped to take the fight to Trump on a debate stage.

STATES GUNS SUMMIT AHEAD? In the wake of the Gilroy mass shooting, Assemblymembers Buffy Wicks and Jesse Gabriel are reaching out to members of the Nevada State Assembly to urge a joint legislative summit “to discuss avenues for interstate cooperation on gun safety’’ — an effort they say could be “a groundbreaking, state level coordination that could serve as a model for other states across the United States.’’

Noting that the Gilroy shooter obtained a gun in Nevada “that would have been illegal to purchase in California,’’ the CA Assemblymembers have invited Nevada Assembly Speaker Jason Frierson and his colleagues to a summit aimed at allowing the neighboring states to work together toward tougher gun rules. “We have a responsibility to act decisively to prevent gun violence, especially as Congressional Republicans continue to block common sense gun safety legislation that enjoys broad bipartisan support,’’ a letter signed by two dozen California Assemblymembers says.
