California Attorney General Rob and other officials on Tuesday unveiled a bill aimed at increasing fines on corporations convicted of crimes.

The penalties would be used to fund a new California Crime Victims Fund that supports crime victims with things like housing and counseling.

Bonta argued that money would be a necessary supplement to other underfunded sources for crime victims like the federal Victims of Crime Act and California Victims Compensation Fund.

The new bill, AB 2432, would increase penalties for corporations convicted of crimes in two ways. It would raise the possible maximum criminal fines that corporations could face and increase the minimum fine for a separate restitution penalty.

For the first example, the bill would raise the maximum criminal fines that could be imposed on a corporation to twice the amount taken from victims damages caused by the corporation’s actions, up to a $25 million cap. One hundred percent of the money raised by this fine would go to the new California Crime Victims Fund. This would be a big jump from the current $10,000 per felony maximum fine corporations face.

“This provides more tools in the toolbox for judges,” Bonta said. “It is not mandatory. It is discretionary.”

For the second example, restitution fines would be increased to a minimum of $100,000 for a corporation convicted of a felony, up from $10,000 now. Seventy-five percent of this money that's raised would go to the California Crime Victims Fund, while 25% would go to the prosecuting agency that brought the criminal prosecution.

AB 2432, authored by Assemblymembers Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, and Eloise Gómez Reyes, D-Colton, is aimed at corporations that are convicted of things like environmental crimes and Ponzi schemes.

An example the AG’s office cited was the 2015 Refugio Oil Spill in Santa Barbara County. In that case, Plains All American Pipeline, L.P., paid more than $3.3 million in total fines and penalties after the spill caused more than $200 million in damages.

It’s unclear how much money would be raised for crime victims should the bill become law.

Bonta said the Department of Finance and Legislative Analyst’s Office would provide more guidance on how much could be recovered, though it could be tens of millions or hundreds of millions of dollars over time.
