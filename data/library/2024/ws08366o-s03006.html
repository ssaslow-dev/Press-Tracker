California lawmakers move forward with budget bargaining chip

SACRAMENTO, Calif. —

Less than 24 hours after releasing a proposed state spending plan crafted by Democratic state lawmakers, the Assembly's Budget Committee voted to move forward with it on a party-line vote Thursday.

The spending plan represents a deal between Democrats in the Senate and Assembly, but for now serves as a bargaining chip to take to the negotiating table with Gov. Newsom to nail down the state's budget.

The legislative plan attempts to restore some of the proposed cuts Newsom proposed earlier this month related to healthcare, childcare slots, and housing development. The proposal also attempts to further cut spending on the state's prison system.

"We also understand such an effort must go hand and glove with a clear, unwavering commitment of smarter spending and real accountability," said Democratic Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, who leads the committee. "We recognize that in these times the people of California expect the government to tighten its belt and balance its budget just like families do every month. That's what the legislative budget does."

It was unclear through discussion in the hearing how exactly legislative Democrats plan to balance out the restored cuts.

Two of the state's key budget watchers, Legislative Analyst Gabe Petek, and Erika Li with Gov. Newsom's Department of Finance, told the committee they were in the process of reviewing the plan and could not fully weigh in on the proposal.

"We are still reviewing the details of the Legislative plan, including both the new reductions as well as new spending proposals," Li told the panel. "It is not yet clear how the overall plan impacts fiscal years beyond 2025-26, and it will be important to the Administration to know that there are not significant increases in projected deficits in 2026-27 and 2027-28."

Following the panel's vote, some Republicans said they were still reviewing the plan.

"I'm looking forward to reviewing it more and I'm sure we have our share of draconian cuts in the budget as well," said Assemblyman Joe Patterson, R-Rocklin.

Lawmakers face a deadline to pass a spending plan by June 15. Typically, what's passed by the deadline is a placeholder for the real plan that is to be negotiated between the governor and legislative leaders.
