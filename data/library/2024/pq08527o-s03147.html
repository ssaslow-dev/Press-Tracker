CALIFORNIA BILL TARGETS CREDITS FOR GHG OFFSET PROJECTS UNLIKELY TO BE 'REAL'

By Curt Barry

Posted February 8, 2024 A California lawmaker is floating legislation that would prohibit the issuance of a voluntary carbon offset (VCO) if the issuer "knows or should know" that the emission-reduction benefits of the credit "are unlikely to be quantifiable, real, and additional."

The bill, SB 1036 by Sen. Monique Limon (D-Santa Barbara), was introduced Feb. 6. It is the latest example of legislators' skepticism of the veracity of carbon offsets often used to satisfy corporate climate goals, with critics arguing such credits are backed by projects with dubious claims to GHG cuts.

Last year, state lawmakers enacted a controversial bill -- AB 1305 -- requiring companies that buy and sell VCOs to disclose information showing the credits are valid, though the author of that measure recently clarified that initial disclosure reports are not due until 2025.

The new bill, SB 1036, "would make it unlawful for a person to certify or issue a voluntary carbon offset, to maintain on a registry a voluntary carbon offset, or to market, make available or offer for sale, or sell a voluntary carbon offset if the person knows or should know that the greenhouse gas reductions or greenhouse gas removal enhancements of the offset project related to the voluntary carbon offset are unlikely to be quantifiable, real, and additional," according to the bill text. The measure would also make it unlawful for a person to "verify an offset project for the purposes of issuing a voluntary carbon offset if the person knows or should know that the greenhouse gas reductions or greenhouse gas removal enhancements of the offset project are unlikely to be quantifiable, real, and additional."

Further, SB 1036 would prohibit the issuance or marketing of a VCO "if the person knows or should know that the durability of the voluntary carbon offset's greenhouse gas reductions or greenhouse gas removal enhancements is less than the atmospheric lifetime of carbon dioxide emissions," with some exceptions.

The prohibition would also apply if a seller or marketer "knows or should know that the atmospheric lifetime of the greenhouse gases associated with the voluntary carbon offset's greenhouse gas reductions or greenhouse gas removal enhancements is less than the atmospheric lifetime of carbon dioxide emissions," with some exceptions.

A violation of the bill's provisions would not be a crime, but it would be "subject to enforcement by any available civil remedies," the text states.

Curbing 'Greenwashing' The enactment of last year's AB 1305 and the introduction of SB 1036 reflect California lawmakers' interest in rooting out false advertising and public claims by companies that they are taking significant steps to reduce GHG emissions when their efforts are not backed by true emissions cuts.

The author of last year's VCO disclosure law -- Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) -- has cited reports finding that the global VCO market is worth more than $2 billion and it is projected to grow to between $10 billion and $40 billion by 2030. However, "researchers have grown increasingly concerned with the impact of credits sold on the voluntary market," he said. "Indeed, a recent investigation found that greater than 90% of rainforest offset projects listed on one of the largest voluntary offset standards bodies did not represent genuine carbon reductions. Given the absence of regulations for this market, there is a clear need for additional transparency to ensure that consumers are not misled by corporate 'greenwashing.'"

But the California Chamber of Commerce and other industry groups opposed AB 1305, arguing offsets are already subject to rigorous reporting requirements by voluntary GHG registries. "Duplicating that process [through AB 1305] is wholly unnecessary and could potentially diminish the value of the registry by imposing this requirement on staff that have not been trained in offset protocols by" the California Air Resources Board, they argued. -- Curt Barry (cbarry@iwpnews.com)
