In Focus SoCal: Honoring our veterans

BY In Focus Staff El Segundo

PUBLISHED 11:00 AM PT Nov. 09, 2024 PUBLISHED 11:00 AM PST Nov. 09, 2024

EL SEGUNDO, Calif. — A handful of new laws supporting California’s nearly two million veterans will soon go into effect, affecting affordable housing, education and financial assistance.

State legislators found several areas to strengthen and streamline procedures to help veterans and their families.

On this week’s “In Focus SoCal,” host Tanya McRae sits down with Assemblywoman Pilar Schiavo, chair of the Military and Veterans Affairs Committee, who authored several bills.

Schiavo discusses AB 535, which removes barriers to housing for veterans by changing a policy that currently counts veterans’ disability income against them.

“This really is a way to exclude that disability income from being counted when determining veterans’ needs for state-funded, affordable, transitional, or supportive housing,” said Schiavo.

Schiavo also shares about her father’s service in the Air Force during the Vietnam War and how he is fighting cancer for the third time because of his exposure to Agent Orange.

The U.S. military used the chemical herbicide as part of its herbicidal warfare program during the war. Schiavo credited the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs for supporting her dad.

“The first time he got cancer, we were a struggling, you know, working-class family, and my dad was the breadwinner. He couldn’t work because of the treatment,” said Schiavo. “We really struggled during that time but because of the VA, at least we didn’t have to make that choice of whether or not we have a roof over our head or food on the table or pay for my dad’s treatment.”

McRae visits The Veterans Art Project, a community-based arts organization in Vista, California, that serves veterans through arts programming.

“I believe that arts change lives, and I believe that the arts have an opportunity to have our participants be involved with process,” said Steve Dilley, founder and Executive Director of the organization.

Dilley, whose parents are both veterans, started the organization in 2009 after he saw the devastating affects of the Afghanistan War on service members who came back home.

Reginald Green is a Navy veteran who is now a staff art instructor with VETART.

He left the Navy after he received a mandatory anthrax vaccine and developed chronic headaches and migraines.

“I think it gave me a sense of purpose, and I kind of lose track of my pain and stuff while I’m like engaging in art and creating,” said Green.

Also in this week’s episode is Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, Chair of the Assembly Budget Committee.

He discusses the California School Food Safety Act, the first-in-the-nation legislation to ban harmful chemicals in school foods.

“We started to hear from parents all across the state of California and really all across the United States who asked us to take a look at these issues of food dyes and how they could negatively impact our children,” said Gabriel.

The new law will go into effect in 2027 and prohibits public schools from serving foods containing six synthetic food dyes.

A report from the California EPA found the dyes were linked to neurobehavioral issues in children.

“It really, really upset me and, frankly, kind of made me angry when I was approached by advocates and I learned that so many of the chemicals that are allowed in our food here in the United States of America are actually banned in other countries, that they are not allowed to be used in food sold in other countries, in all 27 nations in the European Union, in many countries in Asia and Latin America,” said Gabriel.
