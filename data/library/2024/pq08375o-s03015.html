EDF Joins Industry Groups Against California Bill Targeting Carbon Offsets

By Curt Barry

May 22, 2024 The Environmental Defense Fund (EDF) and a conservation group are joining industry in opposing a California bill to prohibit the issuance of carbon offsets if the issuer "knows or should know" the benefits of the credits "are unlikely to be quantifiable, real, and additional," arguing the measure would block worthwhile emission-reduction projects. The bill "would stifle private investment in nature-based climate solutions, preventing California from achieving its natural and working lands goals," states a Senate "floor alert" issued this week by EDF and Conservation International in opposition to the measure, SB 1036 by Sen. Monique Limon (D-Santa Barbara), ahead of a scheduled Senate floor vote. The legislation was approved by the Senate May 21 on a 31-5 vote, with four senators not voting. But sources say that opponents will be seeking to kill or significantly scale back the measure as it is considered in the state Assembly in the coming weeks.

SB 1036 proposes to add "certain claims about voluntary carbon offsets (VCOs) to the False Advertising Law, related to VCOs that are known or should be known to not be quantifiable, real, and additional, as defined," explains a Senate floor analysis.

Specifically, the bill declares several activities unlawful if the person knows or should know that the VCO's greenhouse gas reductions or GHG removal enhancements are unlikely to be quantifiable, real, and additional. Those activities are: verifying a VCO project; certifying or issuing a VCO; maintaining a VCO on a registry; or marketing, offering for sale, or selling a VCO, the analysis says.

Certain specific offset credits that relate to California's compliance offset protocol for the state's GHG cap-and-trade program would be exempted from the provisions of the bill.

Further, SB 1036 declares it to be unlawful for a person to market, offer for sale, or sell a VCO -- "without explicitly marketing the VCO as not being physically equivalent to the climate impact of carbon dioxide emissions" -- if either of two conditions applies: it is reasonably expected, based on appropriate technical evidence, that the durability commitment related to the VCO is subject to no more than a negligible risk of reversal of at least 1,000 years; or the person knows or should know that the "atmospheric lifetime of the VCO's non-CO2 GHG reductions (or removal enhancements) are less than 1,000 years."

In 2023, state lawmakers enacted a controversial bill -- AB 1305 -- requiring companies that buy and sell VCOs to disclose information showing the credits are valid, though the author of that measure recently clarified that initial disclosure reports are not due until 2025.

The enactment of that law and this year's SB 1036 reflect California lawmakers' interest in rooting out companies' false advertising and public claims that they are taking significant steps to reduce GHG emissions, when the lawmakers say their efforts are often not backed by true emissions cuts.

The author of 2023's VCO disclosure law -- Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) -- cited reports finding that the global VCO market is worth more than $2 billion and it is projected to grow to between $10 billion and $40 billion by 2030. Growing Opposition The California Chamber of Commerce and other industry groups opposed AB 1305, arguing offsets are already subject to rigorous reporting requirements by voluntary GHG registries. "Duplicating that process [through AB 1305] is wholly unnecessary and could potentially diminish the value of the registry by imposing this requirement on staff that have not been trained in offset protocols by" the California Air Resources Board, they argued.

The Chamber, carbon trading businesses and affected industry groups are now strongly opposing SB 1036 for similar reasons.

But the addition of EDF and Conservation International to the list of opponents could presage a more difficult fight for SB 1036 in the Assembly.

"California's Nature-Based Climate Solutions Targets explicitly require 'high-levels of investment from many partners, including . . . private landowners and managers . . . development companies . . . investors and more,'" the two groups emphasize in their floor alert.

"Governments cannot provide sufficient funding for natural and working lands without private sector help. No government -- including California's -- can fully finance activities to meet their natural and working lands and climate goals. In fact, Governor Newsom's May Revise proposes cutting more than half a billion dollars from nature-based climate solutions," they continue.

SB 1036 also would conflict with ongoing federal and international efforts to ensure integrity in the voluntary carbon market (VCM)," they further argue. "The Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market (ICVCM) is setting an international regulatory standard for the VCM, with aligned guidance from the Biden administration forthcoming." SB 1036 will also increase "the cost of climate action with its $1.7 million annual price tag, in addition to 'unknown but potentially significant ongoing costs (General Fund) for the Attorney General,'" they add, citing the Senate Appropriations Committee analysis.

"California cannot afford to cut off private investment in climate while also slashing public expenditures, let alone incur new ongoing costs."

The voluntary carbon registry ACR, formerly known as American Carbon Registry, argues SB 1036 "will have serious negative implications for market stakeholders' willingness to support climate action and verified mitigation outcomes through carbon markets for fear of lawsuits under rules that are subjective and difficult to enforce or prove on matters that lack scientific and policy consensus," the Senate floor analysis states.

"The policy precedent California sets, particularly on climate change mitigation through markets, has far reaching effects both through extra-territorial impacts and policy replication and can either positively or negatively impact a range of other policy making efforts currently underway, including interagency work at the federal level as well as international cooperation under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement," ACR adds. -- Curt Barry (cbarry@iwpnews.com)
