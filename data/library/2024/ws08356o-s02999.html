FAIRFIELD — Legislation was introduced Thursday that the author said will "enhance consumer protection" for restaurant patrons.

“Restaurant customers shouldn’t be surprised when they get their checks by a slew of extra charges they were not expecting,” state Sen. Bill Dodd, D-Napa, said in a statement. “Many restaurants are up-front with their business practices, but too many aren’t, necessitating action. This proposal will level the playing field for all restaurants and address confusion and disagreement about what is permissible under state law.”

Gov. Gavin Newsom, last year, signed Dodd’s Senate Bill 478, "which bars businesses of all types from adding undisclosed fees at the end of transactions."

"In response to questions about how the new law applies to restaurant charges, Sen. Dodd is introducing an urgency measure, Senate Bill 1524, which could be adopted within the next month. The new proposal makes clear that any mandatory gratuity, service charge or other fee charged by restaurants must be displayed conspicuously on restaurant menus. For example, if a restaurant adds a 20% security fee to all bills, that 20% charge must be clearly stated up front on the menu. It cannot be a surprise that only shows up when the bill arrives," the statement said.

SB 1524 is co-authored by Sen. Scott Wiener, D-San Francisco, and Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino. It is supported by the California Restaurant Association and the Unite Here labor union.
