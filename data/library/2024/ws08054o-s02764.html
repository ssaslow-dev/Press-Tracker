California lawmakers race to 'Trump-proof' state laws

California Governor Gavin Newsom is proposing a $25 million legal defense fund to "Trump-proof" his state before President-elect Donald Trump takes office.

The Democrat-led state legislature is convening an emergency special session to discuss legislation that would provide for up to $25 million in additional funding for the California Department of Justice and other state agencies to challenge any unconstitutional or unlawful federal actions the Trump administration may take.

Newsom says the fund would help protect the state's rights and freedoms, regardless of the White House occupant. California has previously led 122 lawsuits against Trump administration policies, costing $42 million in litigation.

"He wants to have the money ready to go the minute that Trump gets in there. Otherwise, these things would be taking up months to go through," said KCBS Insider Phil Matier. "Also, he wanted to make a political statement. He called for this just as soon as the votes were counted. Gavin Newsom has always been at the forefront of what he calls the resistance."

The governor's office said the action is needed to safeguard "California values," including fundamental civil rights, reproductive freedom, climate action, immigrant families and more.

"California is a tent pole of the country — from the economy to innovation to protecting and investing in rights and freedoms for all people. We will work with the incoming administration and we want President Trump to succeed in serving all Americans," Newsom said in a statement, per the LA Times. "But when there is overreach, when lives are threatened, when rights and freedoms are targeted, we will take action. And that is exactly what this special session is about — setting this state up for success, regardless of who is in the White House."

The session coincides with the swearing-in of newly elected state legislators, signaling a proactive approach to anticipated conflicts with the federal government.

"While we always hope to collaborate with our federal partners, California will be ready to vigorously defend our interests and values from any unlawful action by the incoming Trump Administration," Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel said in a statement. "We know from President-elect Trump’s statements – and from the more than 120 lawsuits that California filed during the first Trump Administration – that we must be prepared to defend ourselves. We're not going to be caught flat-footed."

The goal is to pass the legislation and have it signed into law prior to Trump's inauguration on January 20, 2025.
