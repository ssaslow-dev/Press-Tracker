A Young Airman Takes A Final "Flight" in Protest; D12 Assemblyman Damon Connolly's Valentine's Week Trip To Israel Remains Undisclosed to the Public A Full Ten Days Later

Editor's Note: I deeply regret to inform you that at the time of this writing, a second self-immolation in protest of the ongoing Israeli assault on Palestinians in Gaza has occurred, this time in front of the Israeli embassy in Washington, DC. Aaron Bushnell, a 25-year-old, active-duty member of the U.S. Air Force, stated that he could no longer be "complicit in genocide" and then calmly set himself on fire. Bushnell succumbed to his injuries Sunday night. You can admire the courage required for his protest, but you are also permitted to feel outraged that this decent and beautiful soul is gone.

Meanwhile, State Assemblyman Damon Connolly, whose job it is to represent Marin and Sonoma counties, has declined to issue a single statement in support of ceasefire. But Connolly's anti-Palestinian bias was made even more clear in mid-February when we learned from The Sacramento Bee that Connolly had taken leave of his office to visit Israeli President Isaac Herzog along with six other California legislators. Given that the case against Israel for genocide is currently being heard by the International Court of Justice, this was at best an inauspicious time for Connolly and the delegation (led by State Senators Henry Stern and Scott Wiener) to make such a trip, let alone to meet with Herzog. And, of course, neither the corporate-owned Marin Independent Journal nor the "alternative" Pacific Sun appear to have reported that the trip even occurred.

No Answers From Connolly's Office

Seeking answers about Connolly's trip, I made calls over the last week to Connolly's offices in Sacramento, San Rafael, and Petaluma. On Tuesday, I spoke directly with Megan Myre, Connnolly's apparently fresh-out-of-college Communications Director. Myre told me that she could not answer any of my basic questions about the trip, including: what date Connolly left/ returned; which officials Connolly had met; who paid for the trip; which areas in Israel Connolly had visited, et cetera. Myre repeatedly claimed that a public statement about the trip would be issued soon. When I asked her to define "soon", she declined to specify, but merely kept repeating "soon".

But "soon" has come and gone, and there is still no statement from Connolly or his staff.

Messages for Connolly's Chief of Staff, Mill Valley native Aaron Vad, were not returned, although Connolly felt it somehow appropriate to tweet a photo of Vad with his new service dog.

Was Connolly's staff trolling us with this tweet? The photo of the oddly- plaided Vad and the pricy looking dog were ill-timed. Reports from Gaza are that, amid the IDF's slaughter and the lack of food, cats and dogs have survived by feeding from the corpses of the dead. The bodies have been left in the streets, as Israel has prevented Palestinian family members from retrieving their dead for burial. Those who do try to retrieve their dead are often killed by IDF snipers.

Though there was no official public statement about the Israel trip, Connolly's office put out a steady stream of supposedly feel-good tweets about salmon, beavers, and even whales over the past week. I reached out to Melissa Apuya, who is Connolly's District Director, a holdover from Marc Levine's tenure as the D12 Assemblymember, and she too declined to answer any questions. (Levine termed out and found a job now as a Director at the ADL Northern California.)

Connolly's silence reveals an obvious vulnerability: If this trip were acceptable to the majority of his constituents, there would already have been a statement. Both Scott Wiener and Henry Stern issued public statements about the delegations' trip to Israel upon their return.

But look closer at Connolly's tweet featuring the photo of Vad’s new service dog. If you click on the link, it brings you to a politico article that very briefly acknowledges the trip to Israel, but provides no useful information about it, such as who paid for the trip, which “communities” the lawmakers were scheduled to meet with, etc.

My First LORA Request:

In hope of gathering additional information about Connolly's trip to Israel during the ongoing genocide, I filed a Legislative Open Records Act (LORA) request with the state. (Many decades ago, California's legislators decided that they would be exempt from CPRA requests, and thus you must file a LORA, rather than a CPRA, request. This adds another layer of opacity for members of the public, who then have to figure out how to file a LORA.)

In this process, I called the Chief Counsel for the State Assembly's Special Committee on Legislative Ethics, Adam Silver, with questions about how the LORA differs from the CPRA. Silver was gracious and able to answer some, but not all, of my questions about the LORA process. Silver stressed that, like the CPRA requests, documents may be redacted or withheld.

In the meantime, let's look at two of the media articles about the trip:

The February 14, 2024 Sacramento Bee article stated that the three-day trip included State Senators Scott Wiener (San Francisco) and Henry Stern (Los Angeles); and the following Democratic Assemblymembers: Damon Connolly (San Rafael); Esmeralda Soria (Fresno); Jesse Gabriel (Woodland Hills); Al Muratsuchi (Torrance), and Josh Lowenthal (Long Beach).

The SacBee reported the lawmakers stated that they would "meet with impacted communities and bear witness to the devastation caused by the October 7th Hamas attack and resulting Israel-Hamas war, both of which have had significant impacts on communities across California."

But reporting from the Jewish Public Affairs Committee of California (JPAC) Executive Director David Bocarsly (in the publication San Diego Jewish World) seems to indicate that the California lawmakers met with only one Palestinian during their trip. And every site they visited was focused on damage from the October 7 Hamas attack, with apparently no visits to areas in Gaza destroyed by Israel. Thus, the trip appears to have been a junket designed mostly to solidify loyalty to Israel rather than to learn anything about what is currently happening in Israel/Palestine. Is such bias appropriate if this trip was paid for in part by tax dollars? A request for documents related to who paid for the trip is included in my LORA request.

Solidarity for Some

JPAC's Bocarsly further contradicted the SacBee in his report, stating that it was not a three-day trip, but a four-day trip, but more importantly that it was specifically a "Solidarity Mission to Israel and the Gaza Border". Take note: "Solidarity" apparently ended at the border, and was only extended to Israeli victims of Hamas, and not to Palestinian victims of the IDF or of any Israeli settlers. In fact, the term "settler" does not appear anywhere in Bocarsly's report.

Bocarsly indicates that the trip was a collaboration of JPAC, JCRC Bay Area, and the Jewish Federation Los Angeles. You might recognize that last group as having been involved in partnering with "Birthright Israel", a program that has long been controversial, not least because younger Jewish people sent on its missions have continued to defect to an anti-apartheid position, sometimes while still in Israel during the Birthright program.

Herzog

Bocarsly's article notes that the legislators and tour leaders "visited impacted communities on the Gaza border and surrounding areas, cried with families of hostages, and met with Israeli government officials, including President Isaac Herzog." What message did Damon Connolly intend to convey when he went all the way to Israel to shake hands with a man credibly accused of an ongoing genocide?

And why did these "lawmakers" fear offending hosts' sensibilities by asking to see what was happening in Gaza? "Lawmakers" should have some concern about the facts on the ground related to the genocide casse, particularly since our support for their actions could result in criminal charges against the US, economic sanctions (or boycotts), and possibly even more severe forms of blowback.

Bocarsly's Selective Memories of Kfar Aza and Nova

Bocarsly states that the sites visited by the delegation were Kibbutz Kfar Aza "where 63 people were murdered and 19 residents were taken hostage." He includes the fact that Kfar Aza is part of the Sha'ar Hanegev municipality, the partnership region of the Jewish Federation of San Diego."

But Bocarsly does not indicate that two of the Israeli hostages shot dead by the IDF in December were from Kfar Aza. By failing to mention that fact, Bocarsly attempts to quash the question of why Israeli hostages were shot by the IDF. Nor does Bocarsly include reporting from The New York Times which details how much of the devastation at Kfar Aza was based on Israel's massive intelligence failures. The best-trained army in the world, with the most sophisticated surveillance systems, all of which the American taxpayer is funding, "didn't realize until 11 a.m. that Hamas fighters had stormed Kfar Aza."

Bocarsly then indicates the the delegation then visited the site of the Nova music festival. Again, no details provided by Bocarsly of Haaretz’ reporting that survivors of the Nova festival are now suing Israeli security forces, arguing that "were it not for the failures and negligence of the defendants, jointly and severally, the disaster could have been prevented." Haaretz also reported that the IDF approved the new Nova festival location despite a senior officer's concerns about needless security risk. Shocking images of the Israeli victims who tried to flee in their cars have raised obvious questions about whether that level of intense fire damage could ever have been effected by lightly armed Hamas insurgents.

Per Bocarsly, the delegation met with a small group of Bedouin leaders, leaders apparently approved by the Israeli government. But the only Palestinian with whom the delegation met was a reporter from a very prominent publication who, per Bocarsly, stressed not freedom for the Palestinians, or even ceasefire, but merely "co-existence." I reached out to the reporter in an attempt to confirm Bocarsly's claim and almost immediately received a response that the meeting had been "off the record". But at the point when Bocarsly reported it for San Diego Jewish World, it was, for better or worse, already on the record. Was it appropriate for a reporter to meet with a legislative delegation led by JPAC in the first place? (In the last week, even a publication as prominent as The New York Times was forced to sever its relationship with a journalist who had written an article about "mass rape" by Hamas that continues to fall apart upon closer inspection.)

There's obviously more to write about the delegation, and the peculiarly tight relationship between these particular lawmakers and law enforcement agencies that have long received training from Israeli police and/or the IDF. I hope to investigate how that's played out in Marin County and the North Bay in upcoming articles.
