California Lawmaker Sets 2025 Effective Date For Carbon Offset Disclosures

By Curt Barry

January 24, 2024 A California lawmaker is clarifying that a deadline for initial reports under his controversial law requiring companies that buy and sell voluntary carbon offsets (VCOs) to disclose information showing the credits are valid was intended to be Jan. 1, 2025, amid industry uncertainty and previous assumptions that reports were due this month.

"While the bill does not specify the date on which the first set of disclosures must be posted to a company's Internet website, it was my intent that the first annual disclosure be posted by January 1, 2025. This deadline provides reporting entities with sufficient time to align their business practices with the stated objectives of AB 1305 prior to being subject to potential civil fines," Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) states in a Nov. 30 letter to the Chief Clerk of the Assembly. Gabriel in the letter adds that he intended to submit a formal letter to the Assembly Daily Journal when the Assembly reconvened on Jan. 3; however, it was unclear at press time whether he did. A spokesman for the lawmaker did not respond to requests for comment by press time.

The move is easing some concerns from industry groups affected by the law. "We're really appreciative of Assemblymember Gabriel submitting this letter to the journal. We'll keep working with that office to get clarity as needed, or, if needed," one industry source says.

Signed into law by Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) on Oct. 7, AB 1305 requires a company that is marketing or selling VCOs within the state to disclose on its website details regarding applicable carbon offset projects. These include the specific protocol used to estimate emissions reductions or removal benefits; the location of the offset project site; the project timeline; the date when the project started or will start; and the dates and quantities "when a specified quantity of emissions reductions or removals started or will start, or was modified or reversed," according to the text of the bill.

Other information the seller must disclose includes: the type of project, including whether the offsets are derived from a carbon removal, an avoided emission, or the breakdown of each category; whether the project meets any standards established by law or by a nonprofit entity; the "durability period" for any project that the seller knows or should know that the durability of the project's greenhouse gas reductions or GHG removal enhancements is less than the atmospheric lifetime of carbon dioxide emissions; and whether there is independent expert or third-party validation or verification of the project attributes.

In addition, companies must report the "emissions reduced or carbon removed on an annual basis," including details "regarding accountability measures if a project is not completed or does not meet the projected emissions reductions or removal benefits."

AB 1305 also requires purchasers of VCOs to disclose much of the same data about the credits on their websites. An Assembly floor analysis notes that the Senate amended the bill to reduce the civil penalty cap from $5,000 to $2,500 per violation, and to clarify that the disclosure requirements apply to entities marketing or selling VCOs within the state, and they do not apply to entities that do not operate within the state, do not purchase or use VCOs sold within the state, or do not make claims within the state.

Companies Scrambling To Comply Maureen Gorsen, a partner with the law firm Sidley Austin, said during a Jan. 23 webinar about new California laws and regulations that AB 1305 was a "sleeper" bill during last year's session. Because of that -- and because bills enacted in 2023 generally take effect in January 2024 -- the law's compliance obligations "caught a lot of people unaware." In response to this uncertainty, Gabriel wrote the letter to the Assembly clerk clarifying that he intended Jan. 1, 2025, to be the deadline for companies to file their first disclosure reports, she noted.

Gorsen said that while public prosecutors, including district attorneys and the state attorney general, will "probably" honor Gabriel's intent and not enforce AB 1305's provisions until 2025, she and her firm are currently working with many companies that are scrambling to prepare to comply.

Gabriel has cited reports stating that the global VCO market is worth more than $2 billion and it is projected to grow to between $10 billion and $40 billion by 2030. However, "researchers have grown increasingly concerned with the impact of credits sold on the voluntary market. Indeed, a recent investigation found that greater than 90% of rainforest offset projects listed on one of the largest voluntary offset standards bodies did not represent genuine carbon reductions," he said last year. "Given the absence of regulations for this market, there is a clear need for additional transparency to ensure that consumers are not misled by corporate 'greenwashing.'"

The California Chamber of Commerce and other industry groups opposed AB 1305, arguing offsets are already subject to rigorous reporting requirements by voluntary GHG registries. "Duplicating that process [through AB 1305] is wholly unnecessary and could potentially diminish the value of the registry by imposing this requirement on staff that have not been trained in offset protocols by" the California Air Resources Board, they argued. -- Curt Barry (cbarry@iwpnews.com)
