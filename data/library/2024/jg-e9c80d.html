Doritos, Hot Cheetos could be banned from California schools under Dem-backed bill

by JACKSON WALKER | The National Desk

Fri, March 15th 2024 at 3:34 PM

Updated Fri, March 15th 2024 at 5:20 PM

FILE- This Sept. 26, 2014 file photo shows Nacho Cheese flavored Doritos in Philadelphia. (AP Photo/Matt Rourke, File)

SACRAMENTO, Calif. (TND) — Popular snacks like Doritos and Flamin’ Hot Cheetos could soon be banned in California public schools.

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino,announced AB 2316 Tuesday, a measure that would prevent schools from serving foods with six synthetic food dyes. These foods, the assemblymember said in a press release, have been linked to “cancer, harm to the immune system and neurobehavioral issues.”

The dyes listed include Red Dye 40, which is commonly found in snacks like Doritos and Flamin’ Hot Cheetos. Also mentioned was Yellow 5, an additive in Mountain Dew and M&Ms.

“California has a responsibility to protect our students from chemicals that harm children and that can interfere with their ability to learn,” Assemblymember Gabriel said. “As a lawmaker, a parent and someone who struggled with ADHA, I find it unacceptable that we allow foods with additives that are linked to cancer, hyperactivity, and neurobehavioral harms to be served to our children."

Noah Marty, a senior aide for Assemblymember Gabriel,teased the attention the measure has received online, reposting a meme of Robert Oppenheimer’s famous quote “I am become death, the destroyer of worlds” with the word “death” replaced with “hot Cheetos.”

Frito Lays, which makes a number of products that could be affected by the bill, did not immediately respond to a request for comment from The National Desk (TND) Friday.

In a statement, a spokesperson for trade organization the National Confectioners Association told TND "activists" are "dismantling our national food safety system state by state in an emotionally-driven campaign that lacks scientific backing."

"FDA is the only institution in America that can stop this sensationalistic agenda which is not based on facts and science," the spokesperson said.

In 2023, Californiabanned four food additives, including brominated vegetable oil, potassium bromate, propylparaben and Red Dye 3. Though the measure received attention for being a “Skittles ban,” none of those ingredients appear in the candy. An earlier version of the bill had mentioned titanium dioxide, which is used in Skittles.

The law gives manufacturers until 2027 to change their recipes and avoid being pulled from store shelves.
