KERRY BACKERS TAKE ON EAST VALLEY'S CHALLENGE

By Art Thomason

East Valley Democrats are so pumped up over the party's recent national convention that they've opened an office in Mesa. That's good news for anyone who believes a healthy, two-party political system should be restored in the East Valley. Adrian Minster and Jesse Gabriel are managing the office at 1616 E. Main St., Suite 102.

Delegate Beverly Fox-Miller of Mesa is a veteran of five Democratic National Conventions. She said her enthusiasm over her party's recent production in Bean Town is unprecedented.

"We are united more than ever as a party," Fox-Miller said. The excitement on the floor was just, I don't know how to explain it. It was unlike any others that I have seen. People are willing and so ready to take the message back home with them." Yet she acknowledged that swinging undecided voters and moderate Republicans to support Democrat John Kerry in the East Valley, a bunker of Republican strength, will be a challenge.

I'd give the Sun Angel Foundation better odds selling Arizona State University rally T-shirts on the University of Arizona campus in Tucson.

Recent polls, including an Arizona Republic poll published Wednesday, give President Bush a slim lead. But the Democratic convention created an esprit de corps that hasn't been demonstrated among party faithful felt since 1996, said Fox-Miller and fellow delegate John Chiazza of Gilbert.

It was 1996 when incumbent President Clinton became the first Democratic presidential candidate to carry Arizona since 1948.

"Yes, it is going to be hard," Chiazza said. "But we are going to be working hard. And we've been getting a lot of volunteers. More people are moving into the East Valley who want to work for the Democratic Party."

Chiazza and Fox-Miller realize that invigorating Democrats puts a different spin on traditional political perceptions in these parts. Normally, political junkies are telling us how much Democrats infuriate the right wing of the Republican Party and encourage its vote production.

So adding any significant strength to the Democratic workforce and base in the East Valley would be a watershed event. "When the Clinton-Gore campaign opened an office in Gilbert in 1992, it made national news," Chiazza recalled. The Mesa office will coordinate the Kerry-Edwards get-the-vote-out activities for the southeast Valley. And Democrats such as Chiazza are delighted for personal reasons.

He took his 18-year-old son, Daniel, to the convention.

"When I was a kid, I got to attend the Democratic convention in Los Angeles that nominated John F. Kennedy," he said. "This was the chance of a lifetime for Daniel. I gave him my credentials a couple of times so he could get on the floor. I didn't know what to expect."

Not until father and son were en route home from the convention.

Chiazza said, "On the way back he said, 'Dad, I'm really going to get involved now.' "
