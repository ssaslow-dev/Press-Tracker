DEMOCRATS EMPHASIZE GRASS-ROOTS EFFORT

By Mike Walbert

Democrats must develop an energized grass-roots voter base for their candidates to have any chance of victory, according to several speakers at Wednesday night's District 22 Democratic Committee meeting.

Maricopa County attorney candidate Jonathan Warshaw, members of John Kerry's presidential campaign and the Arizona Advocacy Network Foundation made presentations at the Southeast Regional Library, 775 N. Greenfield Road in Gilbert before about 25 people, the committee's largest turnout this year, said Karen Walter, committee chairwoman. Jesse Gabriel, field organizer for the Arizona Democratic Coordinated Campaign, emphasized the need to dispel ideas that Democrats can't win in Arizona.

"People just don't believe that they're competitive in this state," Gabriel said.

Gabriel, who is working for Kerry's campaign, said the Democratic Party is changing that perception by pouring money and time into the state and local political scene.

"The people in this community are ready to go. People are very excited about this (presidential) election," Gabriel said. "People say there are no Democrats in the East Valley, but I can tell you that is absolutely false." The Democrats failed to field any candidates for this year's legislative race for District 22, which covers most of Gilbert. Warshaw, a Gilbert resident, also addressed primary opponent Don Lewis and Republican candidate Andrew Thomas during his speech. He questioned Lewis' ability to relate to Democrats and called Thomas' politics "divisive."
