Scott Wiener leaving Jewish Caucus role after Gaza ‘genocide’ remarks

The state senator said he’s stepping down as co-chair of the group.

By Gabriel Lorenzo GreschlerPolitics Reporter

Published Jan. 22, 2026at2:21pmUpdated Jan. 22, 2026at3:11pm

Published Jan. 22, 2026at2:21pmUpdated Jan. 22, 2026at3:11pm

Weeks after describing the conflict in Gaza as a “genocide,” state Sen. Scott Wiener is resigning as co-chair of the state’s Jewish Caucus.

The resignation comes after Wiener — who is running in the heated race to succeed Rep. Nancy Pelosi in Congress as a moderate candidate — refused to use the term “genocide” during a candidates’ forum when discussing Israel’s conduct in the war in Gaza. Following an uproar, he changed his tune Jan. 11.

Wiener, a Democrat, has been a supporter of Israel, a position that has sparked criticism among the state’s progressive Democrats.

“Since I stated my view that the Netanyahu government committed a genocide in Gaza, I have had many in-depth conversations with members of the Jewish community with a range of perspectives,” Wiener said in a statement. “While many in the community strongly disagree with my view, I am grateful for their willingness to engage with me and hear my perspective, showing once again the deep respect for difference in our community.”

Wiener will leave the caucus Feb. 15. He has been a co-chair of the group since 2023.

“My campaign is accelerating, and my recent statements on Israel and Gaza have led to significant controversy in the Jewish community,” he said. “The time to transition has arrived.”

The caucus is co-chaired by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel. Serving as vice chairs are Assemblymember Dawn Addis and state Sen. Josh Becker.

Controversy surrounding Wiener’s position on the Middle Eastern conflict heated up Jan. 7 after he and his progressive opponents in the congressional race — Supervisor Connie Chan and former congressional staffer Saikat Chakrabarti — were asked whether Israel was committing genocide in Gaza. Chan and Chakrabarti answered in the affirmative, while Wiener declined to answer.

Wiener changed his position four days later.

“We all have eyes, and we see the absolute devastation and catastrophic death toll in Gaza,” he said in a 90-second clip posted to X. “The Israeli government has tried to destroy Gaza and to push Palestinians out, and that qualifies as genocide.”

The fracas marks a low point for Wiener’s bid to replace Pelosi and shows how foreign policy has become a key issue in the race to represent San Francisco in Congress. While Wiener’s comments angered some of his moderate supporters, it also did not appear to do him any favors among progressives: Both Chan and Chakrabarti painted Wiener’s remarks as a political move to distance himself from the criticism surrounding his support of Israel.

Even Israel’s consul general to the Pacific Northwest, Ambassador Marco Sermoneta, was not happy with the state senator.

“I am disappointed and saddened that Sen. Wiener, co-chair of the California Legislative Caucus, disgracefully chose to abandon facts and amplify Hamas propaganda to score political points,” Sermoneta wrote. Others, including the Israeli-American Council, had demanded that Wiener step down from the Jewish Caucus.
