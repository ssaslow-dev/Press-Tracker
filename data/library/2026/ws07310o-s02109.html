Was there fraud in the LA 2026 mayoral race?

By mid-June, Los Angeles’s contentious top-two mayoral primary finally drew to a close. Incumbent mayor Karen Bass and progressive candidate Nithya Raman both moved on to the general election this fall defeating the trailing conservative candidate Spencer Pratt, though not without controversy.

On election night, Raman trailed Pratt for second place before overtaking him as late mail was counted over the ensuing week with the final tally finishing Bass 34.3% / Raman 29.0% / Pratt 25.5%.

Conservatives and critics have cried election foul and fraud. On the All-In podcast, David Friedberg looked at the Los Angeles mayor’s race and said “there had been no election, just an appointment dressed up as one” and that this was a “one-in-a-trillion” statistical anomaly. And the numbers Friedberg put on screen do look dramatic:

  • In-person, election day: Pratt 35%, Bass 29%, Raman 26%

  • Mail-in received before election day: Bass 38%, Pratt 28%, Raman 20%

  • Ballots arriving after election day: Raman 37%, Bass 35%, Pratt 19%

Friedberg’s claim is that no demographic lean moves Raman from last to first with Bass sliding at the same time. While the standard counter claim is that late mail simply skews towards more progressive voters. And while California does seem to have specific vulnerabilities that could allow a late-mail surge shade into legal fraud, there’s still a difference between having a statistically impossible outcome and fraud being the by product of those vulnerabilities. The first is a clear math question we can solve.

Hypothesis 1: Did late mail skew toward progressives in other LA races this primary?

If late ballots manufactured Raman’s surge, then the mayoral race should be an anomaly. But if late mail just skews progressive in general, we’d see the same thing in races that have nothing to do with the mayor’s contest.

So I measured the late-mail shift for every contest on the LA County ballot using the same method: take each candidate’s share in the early count, subtract it from their share in the late-counted batch, and see who gained.

Early-vs-late vote shares were reconstructed for every contest by capturing the LA County results feed at its first post-election canvass and differencing it against the near-final count.*

Rank everyone by raw late gain and the top is full of Democrats beating Republicans. Progressive Caroline Menjivar and moderate Jesse Gabriel both gained +13.7, identically, because both are Democrats facing a Republican.

Strip out the Dem-vs-Republican races and keep only the progressive-vs-moderate matchups, and 11 of 12 LA progressives gained ground in late mail with a median +10.4 advantage*. Raman's +16.2 late mail gain is the top of a tight pack.

Pratt was collapsing in the late mail. His voters went to Raman. So part of her +16.2 isn’t Raman beating Bass but rather taking share from Pratt. The fair question is simpler: did Raman pull away from Bass by more than other progressives pulled away from their moderates? Line up all twelve of these races and the answer is no. Raman beat Bass by 18 points in the late count. She's the middle of the pack against a median of +17.8 across the twelve races.

The “Bass fell too” point is weaker than it sounds, too. Bass lost about two points in late mail. In most of these races the moderate lost ten or more. If LA is unusual at all, it’s for having a moderate who held up, not a progressive who surged.

Hypothesis 2: Could harvesting for Raman have produced this result and do other states show the same skew?

If someone were harvesting ballots and marking the full progressive slate, you’d see every LA progressive rising together in late mail. To tell them apart, we can look at similar elections in different counties and states over the past few years.

I rebuilt the same measurement for 23 races outside LA’s reach: prior all-mail elections in Washington, Oregon, Colorado, and Arizona; other California counties in this same June 2026 primary; and Los Angeles’s own races in prior years.* To keep it apples-to-apples, I sorted them by similar matchup.

In the cleanest comparison where both viable candidates were Democrats, the late progressive swing is everywhere. If you measure Raman's share of just the two-way Bass-vs-Raman vote and the same swing turns up everywhere late ballots are counted. Seattle's 2021 mayor's race: +12. San Francisco's supervisor race this June: +11. LA's +14.6 is the top of that group, but it sits in a tight band of +11 to +13.

What about the biggest claim of Raman overtaking Spencer Pratt? Raman up 80% but Pratt down a third is actually just Raman going from 20% to 37% of late mail while Pratt’s loss is squarely in the average of all conservatives across the same ballot.

The typical Republican on this same June 2026 ballot lost about 10 points as late mail came in; eighteen of the forty-one fell harder than Pratt. A conservative leading early and losing the final count is the most predictable pattern in American mail voting.

Conclusion

So the one-in-a-trillion chance is not factually correct. Raman’s gain is still within the realms of statistical normality. The actual second institutional argument that California allows unlimited ballot harvesting, mails a ballot to every registrant, requires no ID, and keeps messy voter rolls is not something I’m trying to refute.

But “the laws make fraud possible” is not the same as “fraud absolutely happened because of the swing change”. The one-in-a-trillion math only works if you assume the people who mail their ballots late are a random sample of everyone else where a 17 point swing could only come from tampering. But late voters aren’t a random sample. They’re younger, more progressive, and they break left everywhere late ballots are counted, including in the Seattle and San Francisco races.

Given the unpopularity of the current Republican president and the overwhelming blue city demographics and it stops looking like a one in a trillion event and starts looking like a more normal election. If it were called fraudulent, it would be because it’s the highest point differential of late mail in ballot gains in all races over the past few years. Is that actually fraud? Could be. But is it statistically an outlier? Not compared to every other race.

Could this be a sign of progressive campaign ballot harvesting fraud across every single state and county? Seems far-fetched.

The second piece of actual genuinely testable local claim, that her gains clustered around Skid Row, needs precinct-level data LA County won’t release until certification in July. Until then, the claim that the result itself was mathematically impossible, does not survive contact with the other elections.
