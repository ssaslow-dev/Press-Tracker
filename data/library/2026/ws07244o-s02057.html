Trump DOJ sues California over new gun conversion device law

Supporters say the measure will help keep convertible machine guns off the streets, while opponents call it unconstitutional.

By Jeannie Nguyen
Published 5:19 PM PDT July 2, 2026

CALIFORNIA, USA — California's new law restricting the sale of firearms that can be converted into makeshift machine guns is now in effect, and the Trump administration has already filed a lawsuit seeking to block it.

Supporters say Assembly Bill 1127 will help keep convertible machine guns off the streets, while opponents argue it violates the Second Amendment.

The lawsuit, filed by the U.S. Department of Justice, challenges the new law, which prohibits gun manufacturers from selling firearms that can be converted into makeshift machine guns through the use of a "switch."

"We're excited to see that the DOJ is taking the same stance as our organization and fighting back against California's unconstitutional gun laws," Alex Wilson of Gun Owners of California said.

Wilson argued the law targets one of the country's most popular handguns based on the possibility it could be converted into a machine gun.

"The author used, you know, potential for misuse to justify banning one of the most popular handguns circulated in America," Wilson said. "Based on the fact that they said it was machine gun convertible."

AB 1127, authored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), prevents gun manufacturers from selling firearms that can be converted into makeshift machine guns with a switch. The law targets Glock-style handguns that can be modified with the device.

Gabriel was not available for an interview with ABC10 but has previously spoken about the dangers posed by the conversion devices.

"They're small devices. They're cheap devices. They can be bought for $25 or less. They can be 3D printed at home. They can be installed with a screwdriver," Gabriel said in August 2025. "And this tiny device, this switch, can turn something that is a pistol into a deadly lethal machine gun capable of firing hundreds of rounds in a mere matter of mere minutes."

The Justice Department's lawsuit argues California's new law is unlawful under the Second Amendment.

"The court said that Americans have the right to use arms that are in common use for self-defense and other lawful purposes," Barry Arrington, Acting Chief of the Justice Department's Second Amendment Section, said.

In response to the lawsuit, Gabriel said California would not back down in the face of threats from President Donald Trump.

Gavin Newsom also responded on social media, saying in part that the Trump administration is trying to dismantle California's "common-sense gun safety laws." He added that the laws save lives and that the state will continue defending them.

"Common sense gun safety laws work. That is something that California has proved," Gabriel said.

A spokesperson for Rob Bonta said the Attorney General's Office is reviewing the lawsuit and will defend what it called California's constitutional gun safety laws in court.

The law does not require people who already legally own Glock-style handguns to surrender them.
