Covid-19

Pandemic Recovery Continues: Teen Mental Health Is Improving, And Some Americans Are Drinking Less

The New York Times: Sadness And Suicidal Behavior Among Teenagers Is Declining, C.D.C. Finds

A national survey has found that key mental health measures for teenagers have significantly improved from their worst levels during the pandemic. (Ghorayshi, 9/21)

NBC News: Drinking Finally Slows After Pandemic Spike — Especially For Millennials And Gen Z

Americans are drinking less for the first time since the pandemic, but alcohol use remains higher than it was before 2020, according to a study published Monday in the journal Annals of Internal Medicine. The decline in drinking rates, though modest at roughly 1%, is welcome news after heavy alcohol use spiked sharply during the pandemic, said senior study author Dr. Brian P. Healthcare workers and hospitals are still recovering from the pandemic —

Ohio State News: Public Health Leaders Report Widespread Harassment, Threats After COVID-19

Backlash against public health officials, including intimidation and threats of physical harm, has been widespread in the years since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic and they face ongoing risk in a divisive nation, according to a new study. “The backlash we’re studying is different than people disagreeing about wearing masks or getting a COVID vaccine,” said lead author Marian Moser Jones, associate professor of health services management and policy in The Ohio State University College of Public Health. “This is true hostility. Threats of violence. Undermining these people in ways that harm their ability to do their jobs.” (Crane, 9/21)

At the height of the pandemic, hospitals in Maine spent $560 million on contract staffing. This year, that number dropped to $214 million. (Forbes, 9/21)

Administration News

Physician Groups Join Lawsuit Against CMS Over Medicaid Work Requirements

Modern Healthcare: CMS Faces Lawsuit Over Medicaid Work Requirements, Frailty Rule

The American College of Physicians and the American Academy of Pediatrics have signed onto a lawsuit seeking to repeal key provisions of a Medicaid work requirements regulation. The plaintiffs allege that the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services’ work requirements rule impermissibly restricts verification and exemption policies such as the parameters for the “medical frailty” exemption. The National Health Law Program and Democracy Forward lead the lawsuit, and the plaintiffs include six physician groups, five Medicaid enrollees and the city of Columbus, Ohio. (Early, 9/21)

The Wall Street Journal: Pharmaceutical Companies Push Trump Administration To Protect China Deals

Western pharmaceutical companies are urging the administration to allow them to keep doing deals with Chinese companies for promising drug candidates, despite a 2025 law restricting U.S. investment in Chinese technology. The group that represents pharmaceutical companies such as Pfizer, Merck and Eli Lilly has been lobbying to make sure that the new rules the Treasury Department is writing, as part of that 2025 law, maintain the drugmakers’ ability to do deals in China. (Martinez and Lawrence, 9/21)

The Guardian: Brendan Carr Was A Member Of A Rogue Alcoholics Anonymous Group Some Labeled A ‘Cult’

Brendan Carr, the chair of the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), was for about a decade a member of a rogue Alcoholics Anonymous (AA) group when he was in his late teens and 20s that some former members compared to a “cult”, according to a Guardian investigation. The Midtown group, which was based in the Washington DC area, attracted young people who were seeking help with addiction, sometimes at the urging of their parents. But while AA groups are traditionally a fellowship of people who come together to solve their drinking problem, former Midtown members say its one-time leader Michael Quinones – who died of cancer in 2007 – came to control nearly every aspect of their lives, including where they lived, worked and went to school. (Kirchgaessner, 9/22)

Modern Healthcare: CVS Health Subsidiary Omnicare Wins Approval For Bankruptcy Plan

CVS Health subsidiary Omnicare won approval of its bankruptcy plan last week. More partnerships with health systems are on the drawing board for InnovAge, a large operator of Programs of All-Inclusive Care for the Elderly. The health systems made financial investments in co-branded PACE centers. KFF Health News: As Health Insurance Costs Soar, Healthcare Workers Also Feel The Pinch

Joshua and Ashley Durham run a family medicine practice, and for the first time in their lives, they have no health insurance. But they said their monthly premiums for a similar health plan for this year rose several hundred dollars to nearly $1,600. (Norimine, 9/22)

AP: ICE Hides Location Of Thousands Of Detainees With Removal Orders

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement has stopped publicly revealing where it is holding thousands of detainees who are subject to final deportation orders, an unannounced move that has made it far more difficult for lawyers and relatives to find them, according to current and former officials familiar with the practice. KFF Health News: Insurance Coverage Lags As Cancer Science, Treatment Move Forward

Eighteen months after his initial diagnosis, chemotherapy hadn’t slowed 21-year-old Mason Henderson’s rare brain tumor, which had spread to his spinal fluid. So he left his home in southeastern Texas to spend three weeks in a clinical trial in New York City. But that failed, too, leaving a murky path for Henderson, whose cancer was so rare the World Health Organization had only given it a name in 2021. So early this year, Henderson’s doctors, evaluating his tumor’s deep genetic language, turned to a drug made by Merck and AstraZeneca called Lynparza. (Allen, 9/22)

Stat: Cancer Symptoms Can Be Treated With Integrative Oncology

For much of my career in cancer care, I have seen a contradiction play out in the exam rooms of my colleagues and across the country. Clinicians often prescribe only medication, even though they know other evidence-based treatments could help: acupuncture for chemotherapy-induced nausea; exercise and nutrition counseling to improve clinical outcomes; and mindfulness-based programs to ease distress, sleep problems, and even immune dysfunction. (Lorenzo Cohen, 9/22)
