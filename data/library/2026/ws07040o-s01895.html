Gov. Newsom signs bill to ban use of shock gloves and other legislation aimed at immigration enforcement

SACRAMENTO, Calif. —

California Gov. Gavin Newsom on Tuesday signed multiple bills that his office said would expand oversight of federal immigration enforcement in California.

The bills cover law enforcement practices, detention centers that operate in the state and are aimed at strengthening legal protections for immigrants.

Among the provisions in the package of legislation:

  • A ban on shock gloves by law enforcement.
  • Restrictions on the use of flash-bang grenades and explosive breaching charges during immigration enforcement.
  • A ban on ICE officers who have committed serious misconduct from becoming peace officers or public employees in California.
  • A new 25% tax on private detention centers where the revenue will go toward immigration-related services.
  • Expanded health oversight requirements for civil detention facilities.
  • Expanded public access to records related to private detention facilities, and a mandate that the attorney general develop and report a proposed oversight plan for private civil detention facilities.
  • More public input on potential facilities through more planning and zoning requirements.
  • A restriction on the use of state-owned facilities for immigration-enforcement staging.
  • Clear identification via a temporary decal on rental vehicles used by law enforcement for immigration operations, with some exceptions.
  • Individuals given the ability to sue federal government officials who have violated their constitutional rights.
  • Protections for people traveling to or from courthouses from civil arrest.
  • Strengthened protections for immigrant victims.
  • Keeping eligibility for CalWORKS assistance when a child or household member is detained by federal immigration authorities, in certain cases.
  • Protections for immigrant victims seeking assistance under California law.

“Trump has put his political interests above the health, safety and livelihood of American families,” Newsom said in a statement. “California is taking action to strengthen transparency, accountability, and oversight around immigration enforcement in our state. This is about stepping up where the federal government has failed our communities.”
