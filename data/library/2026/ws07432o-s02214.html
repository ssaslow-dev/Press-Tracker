Assemblywoman accuses Newsom's top economic official of no showing budget hearing

SACRAMENTO, Calif. —

Gov. Gavin Newsom's top economic official apparently no-showed a budget hearing Tuesday that was expected to include a discussion on a controversial, $19 million ad campaign meant to improve California's image.

A smaller Assembly budget panel that focuses on the Newsom administration's spending was scheduled to hear from the governor's office of business and economic development (also known as GoBiz). A spokesperson for GoBiz Director Dee Dee Myers said she was not specifically requested to attend.

(Previous coverage in the video above.)

The committee's leader, Democratic Assemblywoman Sharon Quirk-Silva, told KCRA 3 last week the panel planned to discuss the national ad campaign. Democrats last Monday would not say if the $19 million on ads was a good use of taxpayer dollars. Republicans criticized it stating it's a way for the governor to promote himself and paint the state's situation in a better light ahead of a presidential run.

Quirk-Silva began Tuesday's hearing by noting the committee would not be discussing GoBiz because the director would not show up. She did not mention Myers by name.

"We expect our directors to come and if our directors are not going to make it, then we're not going to hear the item," Quirk-Silva said, stating it was the second year in a row a director no-showed a hearing.

"We will be holding GoBiz until we can get the director in front of us," Quirk-Silva said.

Willie Rudman, a spokesman for GoBiz, told KCRA 3 that the committee did not specifically request Myers be there ahead of the hearing.

GoBiz submitted a list of speakers in advance which was approved by the committee, Rudman said.

Rudman also pointed to the committee's agenda and noted the ad campaign was not specifically on it.

Newsom's Office of Business and Economic Development is prepared to spend up to $19 million on an ad campaign to promote how the state is "an economic powerhouse and unmatched tourism destination and world's epicenter of innovation," according to a state Request for Proposal (RFP) posted last month.

According to the 19-page document, the campaign is meant to fight "negative narratives amplified online and in partisan media."

The contract is expected to be awarded to a business or businesses by March 31 with a contract that would run from April 6 to Dec. 31 of this year. State certified small businesses will be considered first for the job, according to the RFP.

Newsom's Office of Business and Economic Development is working with a total budget this year of $373 million and the $19 million would come out of that budget.

Earlier in the day, GoBiz sent out a lengthy memo to reporters stating in part "the campaign will tell the California story, not the Gavin Newsom story."

"The effort is about California's success, not about politics," the memo read.
