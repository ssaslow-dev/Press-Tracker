California moves to create non-ultra processed food certification

SACRAMENTO — A new California bill seeks to establish a state certification program for non-ultra processed foods (UPFs).

The bill, introduced on March 24, would create a system where the California Department of Public Health would oversee independent agents certify products as non-UPF in accordance with the state’s definition as established in California law AB 1264, which was enacted last September. Under the law, UPFs are defined as any food or beverage that contains specific additives, including flavors, emulsifiers, stabilizers, thickening agents and colors. Products that contain high amounts of saturated fat, added sugar or sodium also qualify as UPFs under AB 1264.

The proposed bill would have California maintain a publicly available list of non-UPF certified products, audit certification records at any time and enforce standards for a new “California Certified” seal for non-UPF products.

“Parents shouldn’t need a PhD in chemistry to understand what they’re feeding their kids,” said Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, who introduced the bill. “AB 2244 will empower consumers with clear, trustworthy information and make it easier for them to locate healthier foods that are free from harmful additives. This new seal doesn’t limit consumer choice. It just makes informed choice possible.”

Retailers that generate more than $10 million in annual sales and sell more than 25 non-UPF-certified products would additionally be required to display California Certified products more prominently, including at checkout or store entrances, according to the bill.

“This is about setting a higher bar for what we consider healthy food,” said Bernadette Del Chiaro, senior vice president of the Environmental Working Group for California. “Consumers deserve labels they can trust. And families deserve a simple way to tell which foods are closer to what comes from a kitchen rather than a factory.

“If enacted, the bill would establish a state-verified seal for foods free from the additives, emulsifiers, food dyes and flavors that define the modern American diet.”
