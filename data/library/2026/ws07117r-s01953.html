EWG: Newsom signs non-ultraprocessed food labeling bill

NewsNews | Sep 23, 2026

Share this story

California Gov. Gavin Newsom, a Democrat, has signed a bill to create the nation’s first non-ultraprocessed foods label, the Environmental Working Group said in a news release.

The California Legislature passed the bill in August and sent it to Newsom for his signature on September 8, with a September 30 deadline for action. The bill, numbered Assembly Bill 2244, was introduced by state Assembly member Jesse Gabriel, a Democrat from Encino. Newsom’s lists of bills signed in the last few days have not included that bill. “The California Department of Public Health will oversee certification of products that meet the state’s non-UPF standard, approving and accrediting third-party certifiers to conduct product certifications. The products must be recertified at least every three years. The law directs the department to accredit these third-party certifiers no later than June 1, 2029,” EWG said in a news release.

The Trump administration is working on a definition of ultraprocessed foods, but the White House Office of Management and Budget has not released the definition written by the Food and Drug Administration.

–The Hagstrom Report
