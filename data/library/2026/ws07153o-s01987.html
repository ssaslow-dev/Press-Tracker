Proposition 2: INCREASES STATE’S RAINY DAY FUND

What it is: The legislature is asking voters to approve a constitutional amendment to change rules for building state budget reserves and paying down extra state debts. It would increase the constitutional Rainy Day Fund cap from 10% to 20% of general fund taxes, allowing for backup funding when fiscal challenges arise and preventing overspending in years with higher revenues.

What it will cost: By requiring the state to make extra debt payments through 2040, the state might make more debt payments than it would without Proposition 2.

Official Supporters: CA Professional Firefighters; LA Chamber of Commerce; Gavin Newsom; Xavier Becerra; Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel

Website: yesonprop2.com

Endorsements
California Democratic Party

Official Opponents: None Submitted
Carl DeMaio’s Reform California reports spending $7500 to oppose Prop 2

Via CalMatters: Opponents say the state’s reserves can be used by politicians as a “slush fund,” and argue the measure would allow lawmakers to withhold more tax revenue without triggering the spending limit so as to avoid refunds to taxpayers.

My Comment: Going against DeMaio’s choices is the right thing to do.

Proposition 4 REPEALS PROHIBITION AGAINST PUBLIC FUNDING OF ELECTION CAMPAIGNS

What it is: This would undo a (1988) prohibition against state and local governments establishing public campaign funds for candidates if they choose to. It would reverse a 1988 ban on such systems for the state and most local governments. Charter cities in California were exempted from the prohibition.

What it will cost: The cost of these programs could be significant for governments that choose to create them. The total cost would depend on which governments created such programs and how those programs were designed. The Fair Political Practices Commission would probably have to hire more legal staff to help answer questions, costing the state a few hundred thousand dollars each year.

Official Supporters: Placed on the ballot by a vote of the State legislature

Website: yesfairelections.org

Endorsements
California Common Cause
California Clean Money Campaign
League of Women Voters
California Democratic Party

Official Opponents:
California Taxpayers Association
Carl DeMaio’s Reform California reports spending $7500 to oppose Prop 4

Eliminating this safeguard would pave the way for politicians to spend our tax dollars on campaign junk mail, including negative mailers, endless television ads, and other political communications. Californians’ tax dollars should be used to fund essential public services – not political campaigns.

Opposition Website: caltax.org/prop4

My Comment: The fastest way to cut back on the garbage associated with campaigns would be to support legislation undoing the Supreme Court’s Citizens United decision. It will be a cold day in hell before any of these “anti-tax” groups sign on to such laws. Why? Because what/who they really represent are the corporations and ultra-wealthy who benefit from an economy that favors the wealthy few.

Proposition 5: CHANGES RECALL ELECTION PROCESS FOR STATEWIDE OFFICERS.

What it is: Changes the recall process in California by making the selection of a replacement official should the voters opt to give an elected official the boot to be the subject of a separate election or appointment.

What it will cost: In the event of a recall, savings or costs of millions of dollars to administer the election depending on the office recalled.

Proposition 44: REQUIRES COMMUNITY HEALTH CLINICS SPEND 90% OF REVENUE ON PROGRAM SERVICES

What it is: Placed on the ballot by petition signers, Prop 44 would require community clinics serving low-income patients to spend 90% of their annual revenue on patient care. Clinics that don’t follow the Attorney General’s guidance on what qualifies could be fined by the state.

What it will cost: Proponents expect fees to cover the costs, projected to be in the low tens of millions of dollars annually.

Yes, there’s a good reason to be concerned.

Workers accuse health clinic of reprisals- San Diego UT Sept 9, 2026

  • A growing number of doctors and other staff at a sprawling San Diego County health clinic network for underserved patients are saying their patients’ care may be compromised by understaffing — and that clinic leaders are retaliating against them for saying so.

  • The former employees of Family Health Centers of San Diego have made retaliation claims in at least a dozen lawsuits, including one just weeks ago, as well as in federal labor complaints, with the latest filed Friday.

  • The nonprofit is one of the county’s largest networks of federally qualified health centers, which get federal funding to provide health services to underserved populations and cannot turn away patients who can’t pay. The nonprofit says it serves more than 227,000 patients each year in over 90 locations with more than 3,300 staff.

  • At least 12 former employees have sued in the past three years, many of them making similar allegations. Five of those lawsuits have settled, while at least seven are still pending.

Family Health Centers of San Diego has contributed $1,500,000 toward defeating this measure

  • More from UT…Family Health Centers’ net assets have grown every year for more than a decade, and the nonprofit regularly takes in more revenue than it spends. It has recorded net incomes of $58 million to $94 million in every year since 2020, according to its tax filings. Last year it took in $488 million in revenue and spent $409 million.

  • Meanwhile, its cash stores have ballooned from $177 million in 2021 to $269 million last year, its audits show. Last year its unrestricted cash was equivalent to roughly two-thirds of the nonprofit’s spending

Official Supporters: SEIU-UHW largely paid the cost of soliciting signatures

Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs), also known as community clinics, were created to address the fact that many low-income communities struggle to get access to quality healthcare. That’s why, more than any other healthcare provider, they’re publicly funded. But clinic CEOs are now betraying that mission and misusing our tax dollars, spending millions on executive pay and other non-essentials. Some clinics spend less than 50% of their funding on providing care.

The measure is terribly drafted and rife with legal flaws that are going to wind up saddling clinics with enormous financial penalties — just for spending resources on the important staff and patient services that keep clinics running and Californians connected to care. Under Prop. 44, clinics would be financially penalized for investments like capital projects, new equipment and technology upgrades, disaster prep or even if they put revenue in its budget reserves.
