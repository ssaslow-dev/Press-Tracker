California State Senate District 24

John M. Erickson and Brian Goldsmith are the candidates for California State Senate District 24 on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

The State Senate is one of the two houses of the California Legislature, which holds the state's lawmaking power. The Senate has 40 members elected to four-year terms, with half of the seats up every two years; even-numbered districts are on the 2026 ballot. Senators write and vote on state laws and, with the Assembly, pass the state budget, which the Constitution requires by June 15 each year.

What it means for the City of LA

State laws bind the City, and the state budget funds programs the City runs. Example: state law requires the City to plan housing for all income levels and update that plan on a set schedule (Government Code sections 65580 and 65588). The district includes Westside and Hollywood-area City neighborhoods, from Bel Air and Pacific Palisades to Venice and Westchester.

The candidates

John M. Erickson

Erickson grew up in Ripon, Wisconsin, and holds a bachelor's degree from the University of Wisconsin Oshkosh and a doctorate from Claremont Graduate University, WeHo Times reported (WeHo Times). He was a council deputy and city staffer in West Hollywood, a legislative representative for LAX and a vice president at Planned Parenthood Los Angeles. He is now chief of staff at Alliance for a Better Community (same article). He was elected to the West Hollywood City Council in 2020, served as mayor in 2024 and was re-elected that year (Beverly Press). Gov. Jerry Brown appointed him to the California Commission on the Status of Women and Girls in 2017 (WeHo Times).

Brian Goldsmith

Goldsmith grew up in Brentwood and lives in Beverly Hills (Beverly Press; SCNG questionnaire). He went to Harvard and holds a law degree from Stanford, and he worked on Hillary Clinton's 2000 Senate campaign (Daily News; Beverly Press). He produced news for Katie Couric at CBS and Yahoo and worked on Pete Buttigieg's 2020 presidential campaign (Beverly Press). CalMatters describes him as a former senior adviser to Democratic Majority for Israel (CalMatters). He runs Kona Media & Message, a Democratic media consulting firm, and has never held elected office (SCNG).

The job

The district. The 24th Senate District runs along the coast from the Santa Monica Mountains to the Palos Verdes Peninsula.

  • Cities. Agoura Hills, Beverly Hills, Calabasas, El Segundo, Gardena, Hermosa Beach, Hidden Hills, Lomita, Malibu, Manhattan Beach, Palos Verdes Estates, Rancho Palos Verdes, Redondo Beach, Rolling Hills, Rolling Hills Estates, Santa Monica, Torrance, West Hollywood and Westlake Village. It also takes in the unincorporated Santa Monica Mountains and Marina del Rey (Senate District 24 page).
  • City of LA. The same page lists these Los Angeles neighborhoods: Bel Air, Beverly Grove, Brentwood, Fairfax, Holmby Hills, Hollywood, Hollywood Hills, Little Holmby, Mid City West, Pacific Palisades, Playa del Rey, Sunset Hills, Venice, Westwood and Westwood Village. An overlay of the County’s district map on City Neighborhood Council boundaries also shows parts of Westchester/Playa and West LA-Sawtelle (County district layer).
  • People. The Census Bureau’s 2020-2024 survey estimates 1,003,720 residents. About 55% are white (not Latino), 17% Asian, 16% Latino and 4% Black (Census Reporter).
  • Fires. CalMatters describes the district as “home to hundreds of thousands of people displaced by the Los Angeles wildfires in 2025” (CalMatters).
  • Voters. On May 18, 2026 the district had 702,277 registered voters: 49.3% Democratic, 20.8% Republican and 23.1% no party preference (Secretary of State). In the June primary 304,378 votes were cast in this race (Statement of Vote).

What a senator does.

  • Laws. The Senate and Assembly write and pass state laws. A bill becomes law when both houses pass it and the Governor signs it (Constitution, art. IV).
  • Budget. The Legislature must pass the budget by midnight on June 15, and a simple majority of each house is enough (art. IV, sec. 12). CalMatters reported the 2026-27 budget deal at $352 billion (CalMatters).
  • Taxes. Any state tax increase needs two thirds of each house (art. XIII A, sec. 3).
  • Appointments. Only the Senate votes to confirm many of the Governor’s appointees. Its Rules Committee holds those hearings (Senate Rules Committee).
  • What it means for the City. The same budget deal gave counties $900 million for homelessness, CalMatters reported (CalMatters). State law also sets the City’s housing planning duties (Gov. Code 65588).

Records

John M. Erickson

Office. Erickson has been on the West Hollywood City Council since 2020. He was mayor in 2024 and was re-elected that year (Beverly Press). He has been vice president of the California Contract Cities Association. He has served with the National League of Cities, the Clean Power Alliance and the Santa Monica Bay Restoration Commission (WeHo Times).

Council record, as reported. West Hollywood’s council records could not be opened, so these items come from news reports:

  • WeHo Times reported that on the council he supported expanded tenant protections, helped launch the WeHo Cares homelessness program, and backed an anti-gun violence ordinance and a drone program (WeHo Times).
  • He told the Santa Monica Daily Press that he co-authored what he called “the highest minimum wage in the county.” He also cited West Hollywood’s cap on yearly rent increases at 3% (SMDP).
  • He told the Beverly Press that he wrote a rule letting seniors and disabled tenants move from a second-floor to a first-floor unit and keep their rent-controlled rent. He also said the city had a $31 million budget surplus while he served (Beverly Press).

Travel. On April 1, 2024 the West Hollywood council approved sending Erickson, then mayor, to the Paris Olympics Pride House handoff. Councilmember Lauren Meister cast the only no vote (Beverly Press).

Other work. He was vice president of Planned Parenthood Los Angeles. He has served on the national board of the National Organization for Women, and WeHo Times reported that he “previously led the ACLU of Southern California” (WeHo Times). The Daily News editorial board described that role as president (Daily News).

Ethics and court records. The FPPC lists no enforcement case or penalty against him. No City Ethics Commission orders, FEC enforcement cases (searched Oct. 1, 2026), State Bar license or court case naming him as a party (CourtListener) were found (searched Sept. 30, 2026).

Brian Goldsmith

Career.

  • Goldsmith is a Democratic media consultant and principal of Kona Media & Message (SCNG).
  • The Daily News reported that he founded two technology startups (Daily News).
  • He was a news producer for Katie Couric at CBS and Yahoo, including the 2008 interview with Sarah Palin, and joined Pete Buttigieg’s 2020 presidential campaign (Beverly Press).
  • The Daily News editorial board wrote that he has advised campaigns going back to Hillary Clinton’s 2000 Senate race (Daily News).
  • CalMatters describes him as a former senior adviser to Democratic Majority for Israel (CalMatters).

Campaign work on Prop 36. He wrote that he “made ads in support of” Proposition 36, the 2024 measure that raised penalties for some theft and drug crimes. He also supported Nathan Hochman for District Attorney (SCNG).

Public office. None. He lists no prior office (SCNG).

Ethics and court records. No enforcement actions found in FPPC, City Ethics Commission or FEC records (FEC searched Oct. 1, 2026), and no license in his name at the State Bar. No court case naming him as a party was found in CourtListener federal and appellate records (searched Sept. 30, 2026).
