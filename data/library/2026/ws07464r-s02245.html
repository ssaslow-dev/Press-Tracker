Mayor McCarty working on ICE accountability with lawmakers

By Brett Stover
Return to Homepage

(FOX40.COM) — Sacramento Mayor Kevin McCarty is pushing back against ICE, working with state lawmakers on legislation in the wake of another deadly Minnesota shooting.

McCarty, a former assemblymember, said Monday he is collaborating with state leaders on a bill that would give the California attorney general the authority to independently investigate incidents involving federal officers.

"What we are seeing unfold in Minneapolis — where multiple shootings involving federal immigration agents have resulted in the deaths of local residents — underscores a serious gap in accountability when federal law enforcement uses deadly force without robust state oversight," McCarty said in a statement.

On Saturday, 37-year-old nurse Alex Pretti was killed by a U.S. Border Patrol officer in Minneapolis. His death came just weeks after the Jan. 7 fatal shooting of 37-year-old Renee Good by a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer in the same city. The shootings came amid Operation Metro Surge, which ICE has called the agency's largest-ever enforcement action.

If a similar shooting happened in California, McCarty said the state must ensure state law gives the attorney general "clear authority" to pursue an independent investigation.

"Strengthening these powers would help guarantee transparency, build public trust and ensure that all uses of force are thoroughly reviewed, no matter the agency involved," he said.

To accomplish that, McCarty is working with a former colleague — Asm. Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino. Gabriel, who chairs the powerful Assembly Budget Committee, said Monday he will introduce two bills to protect residents' rights.

One bill would require the attorney general to conduct an "independent, transparent and thorough" investigation into any incident involving a shooting by federal immigration enforcement agents, according to a news release.

The legislation builds on Assembly Bill 1506, a 2020 law authored by McCarty that requires the attorney general to review all police shootings involving unarmed people. McCarty said he applauds the proposed expansion of his landmark legislation.

A second bill, from Gabriel and Asm. Juan Carillo, D-Palmdale, would prohibit ICE from using state properties as staging areas for federal operations.

"California cannot stand idly by while ICE agents terrorize our communities and murder American citizens in broad daylight," Gabriel said in a statement. "We must use our power and authority to protect our immigrant communities, push back on federal overreach, and safeguard our constitutional rights."
