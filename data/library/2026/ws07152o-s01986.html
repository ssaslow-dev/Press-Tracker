California’s legislature unanimously passed a bill establishing the nation’s first “Non-Ultraprocessed Certified” seal for food manufacturers lable products that meet associated standards.

Assembly Bill 2244, modeled after the “USDA Organic” label, aims to help consumers quickly identify healthier food options and would require grocery stores in California to display products bearing the label.

The bill is authored by Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), who last year also penned a bill signed by Gov. Gavin Newsom that established the nation’s first statutory definition of ultra-processed foods and a process to ban products from California schools.

The bill, which passed 104-0 combining the state assembly and state senate votes, was delivered to Newsom’s desk Tuesday. He has until Sept. 30 to veto or sign it into law.
