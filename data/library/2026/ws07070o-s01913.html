LA City Council District 3

Timothy K. Gaspar and Barri Worth Girvan are the candidates for LA City Council District 3 on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

The City Council is the lawmaking body of the City of Los Angeles. It has 15 members, and each one is elected by the voters of one district. The Council passes City laws, called ordinances, which the Mayor can approve or veto. It also approves or changes the budget the Mayor proposes each year.

The candidates

Timothy K. Gaspar

Gaspar, 44 (LA Times), grew up in Canoga Park and went to El Camino Real High School, Pierce College and Cal State Northridge (campaign bio; LAist). He founded Gaspar Insurance in 2008 and sold it in 2021, the LA Times reported (LA Times). He has served on the boards of Discovery Cube Los Angeles, the Metropolitan YMCA, Viewpoint School and the Woodland Hills/Warner Center Chamber of Commerce, and lives in Woodland Hills (campaign bio). He lists no prior political office (Daily News questionnaire).

Barri Worth Girvan

Worth Girvan, 43 (LA Times), was born and raised in the West Valley, graduated from UC San Diego, has a master's degree from USC and lives in Woodland Hills (campaign bio). She worked for Assemblymember Lloyd Levine, was West Valley area director for Mayor Antonio Villaraigosa and district director for state Senate Majority Leader Bob Hertzberg, and directed government affairs for the Jewish Federation of Greater Los Angeles (LAist). She is director of community affairs for County Supervisor Lindsey Horvath (LA Times). She has not held public office; she is an elected member of the LA County Democratic Party (Daily News questionnaire).

The job

What a Councilmember does. The 15-member City Council passes City laws, adopts the budget the Mayor proposes, and approves or rejects the Mayor’s picks to run most departments and sit on commissions, LAist explains (LAist). Ordinances go to the Mayor, who can approve or veto them, and the Council can override a veto by a two-thirds vote (City Charter Sec. 250, as reproduced in Council File 26-0489). Under current Charter text the Council can change the Mayor’s budget by June 1, and a Mayor’s veto of those changes takes a two-thirds vote to override (Charter Secs. 313 and 315, as printed in the November 2026 Voter Information Pamphlet). The FY 2026-27 City budget is $14.85 billion (Mayor’s office).

Each member also runs a district office that works with more than 40 City departments on local services and plays “a vital role in shaping development” in the district (LAist). The anti-camping law, LAMC 41.18, lets Council members designate areas near schools, libraries, senior centers and other sensitive places as no-camping zones (LA Times). Each member represents about 260,000 people (LAist).

The district. Council District 3 covers the southwest San Fernando Valley: Woodland Hills, Tarzana, Canoga Park, Winnetka and Reseda (LAist; Daily News). South of Ventura Boulevard are hillside ranch homes. Flatter areas to the north mix single-family homes with apartments and commercial strips (LAist). Bob Blumenfield has held the seat since 2013 and is termed out (Daily News). LAist says the district’s voters “historically have sent centrist (for Los Angeles) Democrats and Republicans” to the Council.

Local issues in the news.

  • Rams Village and Warner Center. The Rams’ owner is building a 52-acre complex in Woodland Hills with the team headquarters, more than 3,000 homes, venues, a hotel and shops (LA Times, Aug 18, 2026). The LA Times put its cost at more than $10 billion (LA Times, April 2025). A separate $2 billion plan, Viva L.A., would add four senior-housing towers of 34 to 42 stories with 3,192 units under the Mayor’s Executive Directive 1 (LA Times, Aug 18, 2026). LAist notes that nearby former rocket-engine land in Canoga Park is still awaiting environmental cleanup.
  • Woodland Hills Country Club. A developer, Arrimus Capital, proposed 398 homes on the golf course under a state fast-track law, AB 2011. In August 2026 the Council voted 11-1 to start a zoning change and then approved it unanimously, and City Planning found the site is mountain lion habitat (Daily News, Aug 21; Daily News, Aug 26; LA Times, Aug 22). Abundant Housing LA and other housing groups opposed Blumenfield’s move (Daily News, Aug 26).
  • Fire risk. Parts of the district are in “very high” fire hazard severity zones near the Santa Monica Mountains (Daily News, Aug 21).
  • Other issues. LA Public Press lists housing and homelessness, public safety, transportation and closing gaps in the LA River bike path as issues the next member will face (LA Public Press).

Records

Neither candidate has held City office, so neither has a Council voting record.

Timothy K. Gaspar

Business. Gaspar founded Gaspar Insurance in 2008. His campaign says it grew to more than 60 employees and 11,000 clients (campaign site). He sold it in 2021 and now campaigns full time, his spokesperson told the LA Times (LA Times).

Civic roles. His campaign lists leadership roles with Discovery Cube Los Angeles, the Metropolitan YMCA of Los Angeles, Viewpoint School, the Woodland Hills/Warner Center Chamber of Commerce and the CSUN Nazarian School of Business (campaign site). LA Public Press also lists the Viewpoint School board of trustees (LA Public Press).

Public positions before the race. In a June 2025 campaign essay he opposed two proposed UNITE HERE Local 11 initiatives: a $30 citywide minimum wage and voter approval for large new hotels (campaign essay, June 26, 2025). Neither is on the November ballot (County Registrar’s list of measures). In December 2025 he wrote that the Woodland Hills Country Club project “cannot proceed as planned” (campaign essay, Dec. 15, 2025).

Ethics and lawsuits. No public enforcement actions found in FPPC, City Ethics Commission or FEC records (FPPC case search; City Ethics enforcement orders; FEC legal search). He is not a State Bar licensee (State Bar search). Searched Sept. 30, 2026.

Barri Worth Girvan

Brad Sherman, worked in Assemblymember Lloyd Levine’s field office, was West Valley area director for Mayor Antonio Villaraigosa and district director for Senate Majority Leader Bob Hertzberg, and directed government affairs for the Jewish Federation of Greater Los Angeles (LAist).
