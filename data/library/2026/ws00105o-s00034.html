What you need to know: The bills signed into law yesterday by Governor Newsom address several points across California’s gun-violence prevention system — from protective-order enforcement and firearm storage to research, reporting, training, youth services and emerging firearm technologies.

September 27, 2026 - SACRAMENTO – Yesterday, Governor Gavin Newsom announced his signature on a comprehensive package of bills to further prevent gun violence and strengthen California’s gun safety leadership.

Taken together, these bills address multiple stages of firearm-violence prevention: identifying and understanding crime guns, preventing prohibited possession, intervening when someone poses a risk, safely storing firearms, improving training and accountability, supporting young survivors and responding to emerging technologies.

Here’s what leaders are saying about the Governor’s latest action to protect California communities:

Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda): “Thank you to Governor Newsom for signing the California Firearm Printing Prevention Act, a bill that will make California safer. A 3D printed gun doesn’t have a serial number or a paper trail and should never have been produced. AB 2047 stops these weapons at the source, before they can harm our communities.”

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), co-founder of California’s Gun Violence Working Group: “California has become a national leader in reducing gun violence because we’ve made a sustained commitment to investing in smart, proven strategies to stop violence before it occurs. AB 2378 builds on that leadership by creating a dedicated Office of Community Violence Intervention to strengthen and coordinate this lifesaving work across our state. I’m grateful to Governor Newsom for signing this important legislation and for his continued leadership in protecting our communities.”

Assemblymember Mike A. Gipson (D-Carson): “AB 2339 strengthens California’s commitment to keeping firearms out of the hands of individuals who are prohibited from possessing them and ensuring that these safeguards work as intended. If someone is prohibited from possessing a firearm, the information needed to enforce that prohibition must be accurate, complete, and timely. This bill improves the reporting process when an individual has been involuntarily admitted to a mental health facility because they pose a threat to themselves or others. Closing gaps in the information available to the Department of Justice will help ensure that firearm background checks have the information they need to be effective.I am proud to partner with the Attorney General on this important legislation. AB 2339 reinforces existing safeguards, gives law enforcement an important tool, and helps protect Californians from preventable firearm-related tragedies.”

Assemblymember Blanca Pacheco (D-Downey): “A loaded firearm in the hands of a young person is a warning sign we cannot ignore. AB 2636 ensures that judges consider all facts when determining whether rehabilitation and deferred entry of judgment are appropriate. This balanced approach holds young people accountable, addresses the circumstances that led them into court, and helps prevent future violence. I thank Governor Newsom for strengthening public safety while preserving opportunities for rehabilitation.”

Assemblymember Catherine Stefani (D-San Francisco): “Gun violence is preventable, and we must act before it is too late. AB 1753 helps ensure firearms and ammunition are surrendered when someone poses a danger, while AB 1974 gives people a safe, voluntary way to temporarily store firearms outside the home during moments of crisis. Together, these laws give Californians practical tools to prevent tragedy and save lives. I am grateful to Governor Newsom for signing these important measures and for his continued commitment to making California a leader in preventing gun violence.”

Assemblymember Buffy Wicks (D-Oakland): “The State has invested significant resources into a robust database of information about crime guns that have caused harm in our communities. That data is powerful, and allowing our trusted partners in state agencies, local governments, and higher education to request it gives them one more tool to keep our communities safe. I’m grateful to Governor Newsom for signing this bill and helping us put this critical information to work.”

Senator Melissa Hurtado (D-Bakersfield): “Every parent should be able to send their child to school, to a friend’s house, or out into their community without worrying about a ghost gun ending up in the wrong hands. We’ve seen the damage ghost guns can do. When someone who has already been convicted of a serious firearm offense can turn around and get access to another gun, that puts our children, our families, our law enforcement officers, and our entire community at risk. That’s why I authored SB 1220, the Peace Officer Protection Act. This is about protecting families, supporting our peace officers, and respecting the rights of responsible gun owners while making it harder for illegal firearms to end up in the wrong hands. I’m proud to have authored this measure, and grateful to Governor Newsom for signing it into law. Our communities deserve to be safe.”

Brady California Legislation and Policy Chair Ruth Borenstein: “California continues to make inspiring, proactive, and intentional action to prevent gun violence before it happens. Gun laws have saved countless lives in California. We have seen our gun death rates drop year after year, and California ranks amongst the top ten states with the lowest rates of gun deaths. Today with the signing of these bills, including a bill to require prospective gun owners to receive education and training on gun laws and gun safety, that progress will continue. I am so thankful for the Brady California grassroots activists who worked tirelessly to support these bills, the leaders in our legislature for their relentless work in moving them through the legislature, and Governor Newsom for his unwavering commitment to gun violence prevention and signing them into law. Brady will never stop working to make California safer.”

GIFFORDS Executive Director Emma Brown: “California leads the nation in gun safety because its leaders work tirelessly to prevent violence and keep communities safe. The policies enacted today will save lives by closing loopholes and investing real resources in community violence intervention. We’re grateful for the hard work of our partners in the legislature and on the ground who helped push these over the finish line.”

Everytown for Gun Safety President John Feinblatt: “California has once again shown the country what it looks like to get ahead of an emerging gun violence threat, rather than waiting for it to become a crisis. In establishing safeguards against 3D-printed guns, California is setting a standard that the 3D-printing industry and states across the country should take seriously. We thank Governor Newsom and Assemblymember Bauer-Kahan for making sure California remains at the vanguard of the gun safety movement.”

Moms Demand Action Executive Director Angela Ferrell-Zabala: “Ghost guns have evolved, and today California made clear that our gun safety laws will evolve with them. For years, survivors, students and volunteers across California have fought to close the loopholes that allow people to make untraceable guns outside the law. AB 2047 builds on that progress by confronting the next generation of this threat before it becomes even more widespread. We’re grateful to Governor Newsom, Assemblymembers Bauer-Kahan and Gipson, and the gun sense champions in Sacramento for continuing to move this work forward.”
