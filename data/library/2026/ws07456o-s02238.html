California Democrats pushing wave of bills aimed at limiting ICE activity in state

At least a dozen bills have been proposed just this week, with many more anticipated

By Jenny Huh
Published 5:19 PM PST January 30, 2026

SACRAMENTO, Calif. — California Democrats continue to push forward a sweeping slate of immigration-related legislation this week, escalating the state’s resistance to federal immigration enforcement and the Trump administration as lawmakers seek to curb the reach of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement across daily life.

Democratic legislators have proposed at least a dozen bills so far targeting ICE operations, ranging from restrictions on the use of private property and public facilities to expanded oversight and new financial penalties for private detention contractors.

The legislative push follows legal battles between California and the federal government. Gov. Gavin Newsom and Attorney General Rob Bonta have filed more than 50 lawsuits against the Trump administration, while lawmakers say the Legislature must also act.

“It’s causing fear and disruption to people’s daily lives, and so people are expecting us to really fight back,” said Assemblymember Rick Chavez Zbur, a Democrat from Hollywood.

The week began with a rally of Democratic senators and assembly members denouncing federal immigration enforcement actions. On the visibly-packed stage Monday, Senate President Pro Tem Monique Limón said lawmakers refuse to “normalize what is happening in this country,” while Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas accused ICE of engaging in “out-of-control illegal violence,” vowing legislative action to protect Californians.

Thursday morning, a dozen Assembly Democrats briefed reporters on proposals designed to limit ICE’s access to everyday infrastructure. One measure would restrict the use of hotels, warehouses and parking lots as temporary detention or staging areas. Another would prevent rental car companies from providing vehicles for immigration enforcement operations.

Assemblymember Jessica Caloza, a Democrat representing the Los Angeles area, said her bill would bar rental car companies from renting vehicles for immigration enforcement activities. Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, a Democrat from Encino, said other his proposal would prohibit ICE from using state-owned parking facilities to stage raids or otherwise force the state into cooperation.

Lawmakers are also proposing new oversight mechanisms. Gabriel said one bill would require independent investigations whenever a civilian is shot by a federal immigration agent.

A key piece of legislation already advancing is Senate Bill 747, authored by Sen. Scott Wiener, a Democrat from San Francisco. Known as the “No Kings Act,” the bill would allow Californians to sue federal officers for constitutional violations, closing what supporters describe as a loophole that shields federal agents from accountability. The measure passed the Senate largely along party lines and now moves to the Assembly.

“Whether you’re local, state or federal, it doesn’t matter,” Wiener said of the bill's purpose. “If you violate the Constitution, you need to be accountable.”

Other bills target the private companies that operate immigration detention facilities under federal contracts. Assemblymember Matt Haney, a Democrat also from San Francisco, has proposed a steep tax on profits earned from detention operations, while Assemblymember Alex Lee, a Democrat from Milpitas, is pushing legislation that would strip state tax breaks from companies that do business with ICE.

“If we can’t ban them, the very least we should do is tax the heck out of them,” Haney said.

Lee said companies should be forced to choose between state tax incentives and federal detention profits.

Assembly Speaker Rivas noted the proposals unveiled this week do not represent a final legislative package, signaling more immigration-related bills are likely to follow.

Political analyst Steve Swatt said the surge of legislation reflects pressure on Democratic leaders to respond forcefully, but he warned they must balance immigration with other pressing priorities.

“They’re going to have to walk and chew gum at the same time,” Swatt said. “California legislators need to do something about this issue — not at the expense of other issues, perhaps — but they need to be very vocal about this and do something.”

Political reporter Jenny Huh asked legislators about other state matters.

Assemblymember Caloza responded that the fear of personal safety comes first and that the caucus is asking with the urgency requested by their constituents.

"Ultimately, if families are in fear for their lives and don't to come out of their homes or go to work... they cannot think about paying the bills, they cannot think about housing and homelessness and their environment," she said.

Additional proposals under development would prohibit individuals who have participated in immigration enforcement — such as former ICE agents — from serving as law enforcement officers or educators in California.

Together, the measures underscore California Democrats’ determination to confront federal immigration policy through state law, even as legal challenges and political backlash are expected to follow.
