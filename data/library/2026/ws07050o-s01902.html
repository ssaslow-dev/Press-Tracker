California first to create 'Non-Ultraprocessed Certified' seal

National Hog Farmer Staff

The bill also requires grocery stores to display products bearing the label.

Governor Gavin Newsom has signed into law a first-in-the-nation bill to increase transparency around ultraprocessed foods. Authored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), AB 2244 establishes the “Non-Ultraprocessed Certified” seal that food manufacturers can place on products that meet clear standards for not being ultra-processed.

Modeled after the “USDA Organic” label, the "seal would provide consumers with a simple, trustworthy way to identify healthier options with a quick glance," Gabriel said. The bill also requires grocery stores in California to display products bearing the label.

“While Washington D.C. is paralyzed by inaction, Republicans and Democrats in California are joining forces to empower consumers to avoid harmful ultra-processed foods,” said Gabriel. “Like the USDA Organic label, this new seal will provide consumers with clear, trustworthy information and make it easier for them to locate healthier foods that are free from harmful additives. Parents shouldn't need a Ph.D. in chemistry to understand what they’re feeding their kids. California began this movement three years ago by banning the most dangerous food additives, and with Governor Newsom’s continued leadership we are taking another big step forward today.”

"We should be about healthcare, not sick-care, recognizing that preventing illnesses is far preferable to finding — and affording — treatments. It’s why California is focused on making whole, healthy foods as accessible as possible, why we’re making healthier choices easier. California has never been afraid to challenge the status quo, and we’ll keep innovating, trying new things, and clearing the way for the rest of the country to follow," said Gov. Newsom.

Last year, Gabriel authored and Newsom signed into law The Real Food, Healthy Kids Act (AB 1264), legislation which established the nation’s first statutory definition of ultra-processed foods and set up a process to ban products from California schools. Assemblymember Gabriel also passed into law two other bills with bipartisan support: the California Food Safety Act (AB 418, 2023) and the California School Food Safety Act (AB 2316, 2024), which he says have "helped change the national conversation about food additives and ultra-processed foods, shaped federal policy, and inspired similar legislation across more than 30 other states."

"California is committed to giving children the best start in life — and that starts with fueling them with fresh, nutritious food free from harmful chemicals. That’s why California provides two free school meals every day, invests in a Farm to School program that brings local, organic foods into our schools and teaches students where their food comes from, and prioritizes making it easier for families to identify nutrient-dense, healthy options. From schools to farms to the grocery store, California is building healthier communities that put kids first," said First Partner Jennifer Siebel Newsom at the signing of AB 2244 on Monday.
