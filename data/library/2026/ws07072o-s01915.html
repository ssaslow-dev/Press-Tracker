Election 2026 · State of California

California Assembly District 40

Rickey Tracy Hayes II and Pilar Schiavo (incumbent) are the candidates for California Assembly District 40 on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

The State Assembly is one of the two houses of the California Legislature, which holds the state's lawmaking power. The Assembly has 80 members. Its members write and vote on state laws and, with the Senate, pass the state budget, which the Constitution requires by June 15 each year.

What it means for the City of LA

State laws bind the City, and the state budget funds programs the City runs. Example: state law requires the City to plan housing for all income levels and update that plan on a set schedule (Government Code sections 65580 and 65588). About 227,700 of the district's 517,000 residents live in the City of Los Angeles, in Chatsworth, Porter Ranch, Granada Hills, Northridge and part of North Hills.

The candidates

Rickey Tracy Hayes II

Hayes describes himself as a Marine Corps veteran, a former custody officer and police officer, and an IBEW Local 47 journeyman lineman who became an energy infrastructure executive (campaign site). He gave his job as director of North America field operations and listed no prior elected office in a 2026 questionnaire (SCNG questionnaire). His campaign says he has lived in the Santa Clarita Valley for more than a decade and has roots in Compton and Long Beach (campaign site).

Pilar Schiavo Incumbent

Schiavo was elected to the Assembly in November 2022 and re-elected in 2024 (Assembly bio). The Daily News's election-night count in 2024 had her at 50.7% to 49.3% for Republican Patrick Gipson (Daily News). Before that she was a nurse advocate and small business owner who worked in the labor movement for more than 20 years (Assembly bio). She chairs the Assembly Committee on Military and Veterans Affairs and lives in Chatsworth (Assembly bio). The Assembly is her first elected office (SCNG questionnaire).

The job

The district. The 40th Assembly District covers the Santa Clarita Valley, Castaic, Val Verde and the northwest San Fernando Valley (Assembly bio).

  • City of LA. The Assembly’s district page counts 227,698 City of LA residents in the district, 44% of its 517,084 people (2020 Census) (district map page). The rest live in Santa Clarita (229,156), Stevenson Ranch, Castaic, Val Verde and other unincorporated areas (district map page). The City neighborhoods are Chatsworth, Porter Ranch, Granada Hills, Northridge and the western part of North Hills, based on an overlay of the County’s district map on the City’s Neighborhood Council map (County district layer; City Neighborhood Councils).
  • People. The Census Bureau’s 2020-2024 survey estimates 515,952 residents, about 39% white (not Latino), 34% Latino, 16% Asian and 5% Black, with a median household income of $121,328 (Census Reporter).
  • Voters. On May 18, 2026 the district had 345,107 registered voters: 40.3% Democratic, 30.2% Republican and 22.4% no party preference (Secretary of State). In the June primary 133,924 votes were cast in this race (Statement of Vote).
  • Local issue. The Chiquita Canyon Landfill near Castaic and Val Verde has had an underground “elevated temperature” event that led to several bills from this seat (AB 28; AB 985).

What an Assemblymember does.

  • Laws. The Assembly and Senate write and pass state laws. A bill becomes law when both houses pass it and the Governor signs it (Constitution, art. IV, sec. 10).
  • Budget. The Legislature must pass the budget by midnight on June 15, and a simple majority of each house is enough (art. IV, sec. 12). CalMatters reported the 2026-27 budget deal at $352 billion (CalMatters).
  • Taxes. Any state tax increase needs two thirds of each house (art. XIII A, sec. 3).
  • What it means for the City. The same budget deal gave counties $900 million for homelessness, CalMatters reported (CalMatters). State law also sets the City’s housing planning duties (Gov. Code 65588).
