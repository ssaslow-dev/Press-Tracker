A number of bills related to LGBTQ health issues and youth passed out of the California Legislature this session and are headed to the desk of Governor Gavin Newsom. He has until September 30 to either sign them into law or veto the legislation.

Senate Bill 1023 authored by gay state Senator John Laird (D-Santa Cruz) aims to ensure nonprofit service providers that furnish an injectable form of the HIV prevention medication PrEP via their own pharmacies can recoup their costs for doing so. As the Bay Area Reporter has previously reported, some insurance policies restrict injectable PrEP coverage to their medical benefit for policyholders, which can cause problems for agencies like the San Francisco AIDS Foundation in being reimbursed.

Laird’s bill, which passed out of the Legislature August 25, is among the Legislative LGBTQ Caucus’ priority bills this session. Co-sponsors of it include gay outgoing Insurance Commissioner Ricardo Lara and the Department of Insurance, statewide LGBTQ advocacy organization Equality California, and nonprofit healthcare providers AIDS Project LA Health and the Los Angeles LGBT Center.

“PrEP works, and people should be able to access it regardless of where they receive care,” stated Laird. “SB 1023 removes a barrier that has made it harder for community clinics and smaller providers to offer this important HIV prevention medication. I’m pleased to see it pass the Legislature and look forward to the governor’s consideration.”

SB 1114 from gay Senator Christopher Cabaldon (D-Yolo) aims to strengthen privacy protections for sexual orientation, gender identity, and intersex data by limiting when state agencies can share the SOGI information. If signed into law by Newsom, sent the bill August 26, then LGBTQ data couldn’t be disclosed outside of California state government, except under limited circumstances.

Assembly Bill 1930 authored by gay Assemblymember Rick Chavez Zbur (D-Hollywood) aims to strengthen state privacy protections for confidential medical information. Sent to Newsom August 27, AB 1930 comes amid politically motivated investigations being initiated by the Trump administration and Republican officials in other states who are targeting providers of abortion and gender-affirming care in California and other states.

“California cannot allow hostile jurisdictions to use our institutions to target patients and providers for health care that is lawful in our state,” stated Zbur. “AB 1930 gives our attorney general an important opportunity to step in when private information about reproductive or gender-affirming healthcare is being sought. I’m proud this bill has passed the Legislature, and I urge Governor Newsom to sign it into law.”

If it is enacted, business entities in the Golden State would have to notify the California attorney general before responding to a subpoena or inquiry regarding healthcare they provide that is legally protected under state law. The attorney general would also be allowed to intervene in any civil action brought by a state business entity that faces a legal inquiry or summons related to the care they provide.

“California is a safe haven for reproductive rights and gender-affirming care. In the face of ongoing attacks by the Trump administration, I'm proud to sponsor legislation that strengthens our role as a safe haven,” stated California Attorney General Rob Bonta. “These medical decisions are deeply personal and should be made by patients with their providers, free from interference by politicians. California will not be intimidated into abandoning our values.”

AB 2164 authored by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda) would prohibit California’s governor from recognizing a request for extradition of a person related to their providing a legally protected healthcare activity in the Golden State such as gender-affirming care except under certain circumstances. It passed out of the Legislature August 25.

Also sent to Newsom that day was AB 1876 authored by Assemblymember Dawn Addis (D-Morro Bay) aimed at protecting intersex youth and adults. It would clarify that intersex traits, as well as gender identity and other characteristics, are included under state law prohibiting sex discrimination by health plans and insurers.

Youth bills sent to governor

One of the bills related to LGBTQ youth passed out of the Legislature this year is Zbur’s AB 1967 that would ensure young adults ages 18 to 21 are not blocked from extended foster care when they are no longer receiving support and are at risk of homelessness. Adopted August 27 by legislators, it would require county social workers to immediately review petitions filed by LGBTQ or straight youth seeking to enter the state’s foster care system due to being abused or neglected at home and determine whether to open a juvenile dependency case within 21 days.

SB 998 by Senators Lena Gonzalez (D-Long Beach) and Akilah Weber Pierson (D-San Diego), passed out of the Legislature August 26, would require the state’s Office of Civil Rights to appoint an LGBTQ+ discrimination prevention coordinator to develop educational materials for school teachers, staff, governing board and body members, administrators, and other local educational agency personnel so they can identify and proactively prevent LGBTQ+ discrimination at their school sites.

Lesbian state Senator Caroline Menjivar (D-San Fernando Valley) saw her SB 608 aimed at ensuring students in grades seven to 12 can get condoms from school-based health centers be sent to Newsom August 26. Former versions of her legislation were vetoed by the governor due to their costs to implement, so it remains to be seen if it will finally become law this year.

Another bill, AB 1578 authored by gay Assemblymember Corey A. Jackson, Ph.D., (D-Moreno Valley), would require elected school board members and other public office holders across California to complete at least one hour of antihate speech training and education within six months of taking office and subsequently every four years thereafter. Sent to Newsom August 30, the bill’s requirements would kick in on January 1, 2028.

Other bills await final enactment

Sent to Newsom August 27 was AB 1836 co-authored by gay Assemblymember Chris Ward (D-San Diego) and Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) that would allow nonprofit hosts of community events such as Pride celebrations and other LGBTQ public gatherings to seek funding upward of $500,000 for security measures via the state’s Nonprofit Security Grant Program. It currently covers nonprofit facilities but not off-site events put on by such entities.

“California is stepping up to protect our communities, so that Californians of all faiths and backgrounds can gather together and celebrate without fear,” stated Gabriel, who chairs the Legislature’s Jewish caucus. “This bill sends a powerful message that California will continue to stand with the LGBTQ+ community and all others targeted by hate. We will never let hate win.”

Sent to the inactive file August 30 was AB 1540 by gay Assemblymember Mark González (D-Los Angeles) that aims to have dedicated counselors for LGBTQ callers to California’s 988 suicide prevention hotline via state officials requesting the federal Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration allow it to have a “Press 3” option. The Trump administration eliminated the option at the national level last summer but is supposed to revive it by the end of the year after out congressmembers and their allies secured funding for the LGBTQ-dedicated counselors. González instead added his bill language and funding to implement it to a pair of budget bills that are now before Newsom.

Remarking on what has transpired over the last year, nonbinary Trevor Project CEO Jaymes Black noted, “LGBTQ+ youth suicide is a public health emergency that requires an urgent response. We need to integrate suicide prevention efforts for this high-risk group into our country’s public health infrastructure. It is no hyperbole to say that, right now, young people’s lives are on the line.”

As the B.A.R. reported online August 25, bills dealing with access to state services by transgender veterans ousted from the military by the Trump administration and when survivors of conversion therapy can file legal claims against practitioners of the discredited therapy practice made it out of the Legislature this month. So did bills allowing employees to take bereavement leave after the death of a chosen family member and requiring college satellite campuses to have staff dedicated to work with LGBTQ students.

The B.A.R. tracked 21 bills dealing with LGBTQ concerns this year. Four were shelved by legislators, while Newsom in July enacted one establishing for educators a permanent training program on how to address suicidal ideation among middle and high school students.

A fifth this month was gutted and amended a second time by bisexual Senator Sasha Renée Pérez (D-Pasadena), who changed the focus of her SB 957 from social media companies needing to notify users when they have received an administrative subpoena to now be aimed at protecting the health and safety of people housed in private civil immigration detention facilities.
