California Assembly District 46

Jesse Gabriel (incumbent) and Tracey Schroeder are the candidates for California Assembly District 46 on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

The State Assembly is one of the two houses of the California Legislature, which holds the state's lawmaking power. The Assembly has 80 members. Its members write and vote on state laws and, with the Senate, pass the state budget, which the Constitution requires by June 15 each year.

Summary by CityHallPass, not the official text or legal advice. The official text controls: LA County's official election information.

What it means for the City of LA

About 97% of the district's land lies inside the City of Los Angeles, across the west and central San Fernando Valley. State laws bind the City, and the state budget funds programs the City runs. Example: state law requires the City to plan housing for all income levels and update that plan on a set schedule (Government Code sections 65580 and 65588).

The candidates

Jesse Gabriel Incumbent

Gabriel is a graduate of UC Berkeley and Harvard Law School and worked as a constitutional rights attorney before entering public service (Assembly biography). He was first elected to the Assembly in 2018 (Daily News). Speaker Robert Rivas named him chair of the Budget Committee in November 2023 (LA Times), and he is also co-chair of the Legislative Jewish Caucus (Assembly biography). He lives in the San Fernando Valley (Assembly biography), and his office's press releases identify him as D-Encino (press release).

Tracey Schroeder

Schroeder told LA School Report in 2022 that she had spent 24 years teaching in the Los Angeles Unified School District, and she ran for the LAUSD Board of Education in District 4 that year (LA School Report). Her campaign site says she is a UCLA graduate with a master's degree from Phillips Graduate Institute and a third-generation Valley resident (campaign site). In a 2024 questionnaire, under other political positions held, she listed only union roles: National Education Association Representative Assembly and the United Teachers Los Angeles House of Representatives (Daily News). She ran against Gabriel in November 2024 (Daily News).

The job

Powers. The California Constitution gives the state’s lawmaking power to the Legislature, which is the Senate plus the 80-member Assembly (art. IV, sec. 1). A bill needs a majority of each house to pass (art. IV, sec. 8). The Legislature can override a Governor’s veto with two-thirds of each house (art. IV, sec. 10). Tax increases need two-thirds of each house (art. XIII A, sec. 3). The Legislature can also put constitutional amendments on the ballot by a two-thirds vote of each house (art. XVIII, sec. 1). The Legislature placed five of the 14 statewide measures on this ballot: Props 1, 2, 4, 5 and 43 (Secretary of State voter guide).

Budget. The Legislature must pass the budget bill by midnight on June 15, and a majority vote is enough (art. IV, sec. 12). CalMatters reported that the 2026-27 budget deal totaled $352 billion, added taxes, including a sales tax on everyday computer software, and gave counties $900 million for homelessness (CalMatters). Gabriel chairs the Assembly Budget Committee; his office says that in that role he works with legislative leaders and the Governor to write the state budget (Assembly biography).

The district.

  • About 502,000 people live in the 46th District, and the Census Bureau’s 2020-2024 survey estimates the population as 40% Latino, 39% white, 12% Asian and 4% Black, with a median household income of $91,381 (Census Reporter).
  • LAist describes it as straddling Los Angeles and Ventura counties along the 101 Freeway, from Van Nuys to Bell Canyon, with voter registration 48.4% Democratic, 20.8% Republican and 23.7% no party preference (LAist).
  • Almost all of it is in the City of LA. Of the district’s 1,425 LA County voting precincts, 1,414 are in the City (County Registrar precinct map). By land area, about 97% is in the City (City council district map).
  • City neighborhoods, by share of the district’s land (Neighborhood Council areas): Woodland Hills-Warner Center (21%), Encino (18%), West Hills (13%), Tarzana (11%), Reseda (8%), Lake Balboa (8%), Van Nuys (7%), Winnetka (6%) and Canoga Park (5%) (City Neighborhood Councils).
  • City Council districts, by share of land: CD 3 (Bob Blumenfield, 47%), CD 4 (Nithya Raman, 17%), CD 6 (Imelda Padilla, 17%), CD 12 (John Lee, 13%) and a sliver of CD 2 (Adrin Nazarian, 1%) (City council district map).
  • Land shares come from overlaying the County’s Assembly district map on the City’s Neighborhood Council and Council district maps. They measure area, not people.

Records

Jesse Gabriel

Office and roles. Gabriel has been in the Assembly since 2018 (Daily News). His office lists these assignments: chair of the Budget Committee, member of Governmental Organization, vice chair of the Joint Legislative Budget Committee, and alternate on all six budget subcommittees (committee page).

Bills, 2025-26 session. Counting only bills whose final version lists him as a lead author, the Legislature’s bill data shows Gabriel on 81 Assembly bills and one constitutional amendment in the regular session, 16 of them shared with other lead authors. Many are budget bills he introduced as Budget chair; dozens of others he introduced were later taken over by the Budget Committee and are not counted. As of the data’s Oct 1, 2026 update, after the Governor’s Sept 30 deadline to act on bills (art. IV, sec. 10), 26 of the 81 bills had become law, none was vetoed and 55 did not pass (bill data). Eight of the 26 were signed on Sept 29 and 30 (Governor’s office, Sept 29; Sept 29; Sept 30). In the 2025 special session he also carried budget bills with Sen. Scott Wiener; one, AB 4, became law (bill data). Laws include:

  • AB 102 and AB 109, the Budget Acts of 2025 and 2026 (bill data).
  • ACA 20, the “Save for California’s Future Act,” which is Prop 2 on this ballot. It enlarges the state’s rainy day fund. The Assembly passed it 59-8 on June 25, 2026 (votes). He shares lead authorship with Assemblymember Avelino Valencia and Senators John Laird and Melissa Hurtado (bill data).
  • AB 1264 (Chapter 467, 2025), which phases “ultraprocessed foods of concern” out of school meals (bill data). STAT profiled his food bills in June 2026 (STAT).
  • AB 1127 (Chapter 572, 2025), which bars dealers from selling pistols that can be easily converted to automatic fire (KQED).
  • AB 468 on looting and AB 299 on hotels and short-term lodging during disasters, both signed in October 2025 (bill data).
  • AB 1836 on the State Nonprofit Security Grant Program and AB 2378 on the CalVIP violence intervention grants, signed Sept 25 and Sept 26, 2026, and AB 2244, a state “Non-Ultraprocessed Certified” food standard, signed Sept 28 (bill data).
  • AB 1806 and AB 1807. The Governor signed both on Sept 29, 2026 (Governor’s office). AB 1806 requires the Attorney General to investigate fatal shootings of unarmed civilians by immigration officers. AB 1807 bars federal immigration agents from using state property for immigration enforcement (Gabriel’s office).
  • Signed Sept 29, 2026: AB 1924 (a statewide homelessness prevention strategy) and AB 2020 (housing program financing) (Governor’s office).
  • Signed Sept 30, 2026: AB 1626 (mental health training for youth coaches), AB 1665 (a related coaches bill he co-led with Assemblymember Blanca Pacheco), AB 2204 (sports programs in prisons) and AB 395 (holidays) (Governor’s office).

Bills, 2023-24 session. He was a lead author on the final version of 68 measures: 58 bills, of which 25 became law and 5 were vetoed, and 10 resolutions, 9 of them adopted (Legislature’s bill data). They include AB 28, an excise tax on guns and ammunition; AB 418, the California Food Safety Act; AB 2316 on banned substances in school food; AB 2432, the California Victims of Crime Act; and AB 1252, which put the Office of Gun Violence Prevention in statute (bill data). A photo caption in a KQED story says the Governor signed his gun and ammunition tax bill on Sept 26, 2023 (KQED).
