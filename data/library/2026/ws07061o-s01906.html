Driving the Day

MAHA SETS ITS SIGHTS ON AI — Leaders of the Make America Healthy Again movement are embracing artificial intelligence to help their mission to address chronic disease, even as public unease about AI grows nationwide, Amanda reports.

On Monday, Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. touted AI’s potential to analyze vast quantities of health data and lower health care costs at a summit hosted by the MAHA Institute, one of his movement’s nonprofit arms.

“If we don’t solve the problem of chronic disease, we’re not going to lower health care costs, and we need [super intelligence] to do that, and we need to consolidate all of these data sets and make them readable,” he told a crowd of MAHA supporters and executives. Last week, President Donald Trump ordered his agencies to refer to artificial intelligence as “super intelligence.” Kennedy added that he recently met with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.

Details: The half-day event centered on how the government can streamline access to health data and best leverage its large databases to improve public health. Speakers included Mark Gorton, president of the MAHA Institute; Nicole Kleinstreuer, a deputy director at the National Institutes of Health; and Dan Brillman and Amy Gleason, deputy administrators at the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services.

“Solving the health data problem is important for a number of multitrillion-dollar companies, and the MAHA Institute can channel corporate reform energy towards targeted policy plans and help them coordinate data modernization strategies,” Gorton said in his opening remarks.

Gleason spearheaded CMS’ Kill the Clipboard initiative, which allows patients to access their health records via QR codes and use AI to understand their health information. “AI can help solve a lot of this, but it does need the data to be able to do that. It needs innovation. It needs people to move faster,” Gleason said.

Some attendees voiced concerns about data privacy and corporate overreach. “When it comes to sharing our personal information. … I think we have to be very cautious,” said one attendee, adding that there are “too many unknowns.”

What’s next: The event precedes the flagship MAHA Summit taking place today, which has secured corporate donors spanning the American economy from OpenAI and Anthropic to Walmart.

Several panels at today’s event will focus on AI, including “AI and Healthcare” and “Building the AI Health Stack.”

AROUND THE AGENCIES

BY THE NUMBERS — The labor union that represents employees at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s Atlanta headquarters has launched a new website with detailed information on how much the agency’s workforce has decreased since the start of the second Trump administration.

The American Federation of Government Employees Local 2883’s website relies on data from the Office of Personnel Management, the Department of Health and Human Services’ employee directory and “union data” to offer the clearest picture yet of how the Trump administration has reshaped the CDC’s workforce.

According to the findings, the agency lost 30 percent of its workforce from March 2025 to May 2026 — about 3,539 people — though some remain on administrative leave as a result of the Trump administration’s reduction-in-force efforts. The analysis didn’t include contractors or members of the U.S. Public Health Service, uniformed public health professionals who often serve at the CDC.

“Dr. Schwartz and CDC’s new leadership are evaluating the agency’s structure and operations to ensure CDC can effectively advance the priorities of President Trump and Secretary Kennedy and fulfill its core public health mission,” an HHS spokesperson told POLITICO.

Details: The largest share of employees who left the agency — 857 — voluntarily retired, while 216 more opted for early retirement and 837 quit.

Another 800-plus were affected by the Trump administration’s RIFs, with 572 fired and 271 placed on administrative leave. Smaller shares left because of expired terms or other forms of termination or separation.

The CDC’s Office of Health Equity and Office of Equal Employment Opportunity lost all their employees, according to the website, and the Office of Human Resources lost nearly all its workers. HHS said last year that it was working to “centralize” human resources.

The National Center on Birth Defects and Developmental Disabilities lost about half its staff, as did the National Center for Chronic Disease Prevention and Health Promotion and the Office of Communications. At the time, HHS said some of the areas cut at the CDC would be moved to a new agency, called the Administration for a Healthy America, but that agency hasn’t been created, likely because Congress hasn’t funded it.

In the States

NEWSOM’S MAHA BID — California Gov. Gavin Newsom, a Democrat who’s increasingly been courting voters aligned with Kennedy’s Make America Healthy Again coalition, is touting a new MAHA-aligned accomplishment, POLITICO’s Nicole Norman reports.

In 2029, his state will likely become the first to label non-ultra-processed products in grocery stores — a year after he signed legislation defining “ultra-processed” foods and phasing those foods out of school lunches.

“California has never been afraid to challenge the status quo, and we’ll keep innovating, trying new things, and clearing the way for the rest of the country to follow,” Newsom said in a statement.

Details: The latest legislation — AB 2244 from Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, a Southern California Democrat — would allow outside companies registered with the state to certify products as non-ultra-processed, making them eligible to carry the state-sponsored seal. Grocery stores with gross sales of more than $10 million a year would also be required to prominently display three certified items if they carry 25 or more products bearing the seal.

Why it matters: The MAHA voter base that helped carry Trump to victory in 2024 is unimpressed with potential Republican presidential candidates and searching for its next champion. But it’s unclear whether Newsom’s action on healthy food would help him court those voters if he runs, as the governor also took firm stances on vaccines and masking during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Lobby Watch

FORMER HHS OFFICIAL’S NEW GIG — Miller Strategies is bringing on Braden Murphy, who was most recently deputy assistant secretary for legislation at the Department of Health and Human Services, as the GOP lobby shop continues to deepen its ties to Trump, our colleagues at POLITICO Influence report.

Murphy advised Kennedy on congressional affairs involving divisions like the Food and Drug Administration, the National Institutes of Health and the CDC. He also helped shepherd several HHS appointees through the Senate confirmation process. Before joining the administration, he worked for House Budget Chair Jodey Arrington (R-Texas) and former Speaker Kevin McCarthy (R-Calif.).

Miller Strategies also announced it is bringing on Noah Pollak, who was a senior adviser in Education Secretary Linda McMahon’s office and the Treasury Department’s Terrorism and Financial Intelligence office.

Jeff Miller, founder and CEO of Miller Strategies, said Pollak and Murphy “helped deliver many of President Trump’s legislative priorities over the past two years.”
