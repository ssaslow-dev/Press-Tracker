Political Notebook: CA budget includes funds for LGBTQ services

  • by Matthew S. Bajko, Assistant Editor
  • Tuesday, June 30, 2026
Share this Post:

State Senator John Laird, left, and Senate President pro Tempore Monique Limón looked on as Governor Gavin Newsom signed the state budget with Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas, second from right, and Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel.

Photo: Courtesy Governor’s Office

LGBTQ advocates notched a host of fiscal successes totaling more than $2 billion in the 2026-27 state budget and associated trailer bills that California Governor Gavin Newsom signed into law Monday. It marks the final budget for the outgoing Democratic leader, widely expected to seek to be his party’s presidential nominee in 2028, and the first time a state has established a statewide fund to support LGBTQ community centers.

Also among this year’s budget priorities for LGBTQ advocates were $26 million for a Gender-Affirming Care Fund to be used to ensure transgender, nonbinary, and gender-expansive people have access to the medically necessary care they need, and $30 million for the Uncompensated Care Fund, which is to be used to cover gender-affirming care alongside abortion and reproductive health services. The Legislature had included both in the budget they sent Newsom to sign.

“At a time when transgender youth and their families are facing unprecedented challenges across the country, California has chosen to stand as a beacon of hope, safety, and dignity,” stated California LGBTQ+ Health and Human Services Network Director Dannie Ceseña, who is Two-Spirit and Native. “We are deeply grateful to Governor Newsom and our state leaders for not just speaking about equality, but funding it. By protecting access to gender-affirming care and robustly supporting the Transgender Health Care Fund, California is actively saving lives and setting a powerful national standard.”

LGBTQ advocates in recent weeks had mounted an intense lobbying campaign to see legislators in Sacramento and the governor include the funding in the final $351.7 billion balanced state budget. State leaders also boosted California’s reserves to almost $29 billion in case of future fiscal shocks to the state’s finances.

In a video message his office posted about his signing the budget that begins on July 1, Newsom noted, "For decades, we've been told that government has to choose between balancing the books and investing in people. California proved that's a false choice. This budget reflects years of disciplined decisions that built historic reserves, paid down debt, strengthened our economy, and made transformational investments in education, healthcare, housing, infrastructure, and opportunity. We're leaving California stronger than we found it – and leaving the next generation a state that's fiscally sound, economically dominant, and ready for whatever comes next."

Helping oversee this year’s budget negotiations was gay state Senator John Laird (D-Santa Cruz) as chair of the Senate Budget and Fiscal Review Committee. Earlier in the month, after the Senate had approved several budget trailer bills that included the funding asks from LGBTQ advocates, Laird noted that they reflected “the same balanced approach that has guided budget negotiations this year. They provide additional revenues that help balance the budget over the next two years, reduce the structural deficit, maintain strong reserves, and avoid deeper cuts to services that Californians rely on every day.”

Acknowledging there was likely to be “debate about individual provisions, the alternative would have been more significant reductions to health care, education, housing, child care, public safety, and other essential programs,” added Laird. “This package represents another important step toward a final budget that protects Californians while strengthening the state’s long-term fiscal position.”

The money for gender-affirming care was a main focus of the inaugural TGI Youth Advocacy Day at the state Capitol. It took place June 23, a week prior to Newsom’s June 30 deadline to sign the budget bills. Equality California, the statewide LGBTQ advocacy organization, had also marshalled its members to contact the governor and legislators and urge them to back the Gender-Affirming Care Fund and other budgetary asks made by the community this year.

“At a time when the federal government continues attacking transgender people and undermining access to medically necessary health care, California is making a historic investment to ensure that our community can continue to access the care they need,” stated gay EQCA Executive Director Tony Hoang.

One of the fiscal priorities advocates secured is roughly $9 million for LGBTQ+ community centers and other LGBTQ+ serving organizations. Another $995,000 is to be directed to those LGBTQ service providers “in less resourced regions” of the state, according to the budget bill. Gay Los Angeles LGBT Center CEO Joe Hollendoner called the $10 million funding allocation “truly historic,” as it’s believed to be the nation's first statewide LGBTQ+ Community Center Fund.

"At a moment when LGBTQ+ communities across the country are under relentless attack and states are dismantling protections for our community, California has chosen a different path—one rooted in dignity, compassion, and action,” stated Hollendoner. “This is one of the most significant LGBTQ+ policy victories in our state's history. These investments will save lives, expand access to healthcare and housing, and strengthen the institutions our communities rely on to survive and thrive.”

LGBTQ advocates also secured $50 million to strengthen victim services through a Victims of Crime Act (VOCA) backfill. They were also successful in seeing $900 million be directed toward the state’s Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention (HHAP) Program.

Another priority was having state leaders designate nearly $1 billion over five years through the ADAP Rebate Fund expenditure plan to strengthen HIV prevention, treatment, care, and efforts to end the epidemics of HIV and other sexually transmitted infections. Lobbyist Ryan Souza, speaking Monday on behalf of the San Francisco AIDS Foundation, APLA Health based in Los Angeles, and the End the Epidemics Coalition at the Senate budget committee’s final hearing on the budget, thanked Newsom, the Legislative LGBTQ Caucus, and the Legislature for backing the ADAP spending plan.

Their “leadership, vision and collaboration allowed the ADAP Rebate Fund to be used in a truly generational investment in the effort to end the epidemic of HIV,” said Souza.

The budget also includes $15 million for the Chancellor’s Office of the California Community Colleges to distribute to community college districts so they can “provide additional funds to support LGBTQ+ students.”

The State Department of Education was also awarded $77,000 to continue its having a half-time position tasked with updating existing, and developing new, resources, strategies, and in-service teacher training to support LGBTQ and questioning students. The state agency also received $150,000 and a full-time position in its School Health and Safety Office “to support LGBTQ+ initiatives and best practices,” according to the budget bill.
