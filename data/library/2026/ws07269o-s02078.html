Unofficial results from California's top-two primary reveal numerous state legislative contests where only two candidates advanced, setting the stage for the November general election. Several incumbents secured decisive leads while a few races are rematches from previous elections. The system, which advances the top two vote-getters regardless of party, has already clarified many ballot lines across Los Angeles, San Bernardino, and Kern counties. Key districts include AD-34, AD-41, AD-43, and multiple others in the San Fernando and San Gabriel Valleys, with incumbents like Luz Rivas, Nick Schultz, and Blanca Rubio poised for November battles.

California's top-two primary system has resulted in numerous state legislative races where only two candidates advanced to the November general election, either due to being unopposed or facing a single opponent.

Under this system, the top two vote-getters in the primary move on regardless of party affiliation, and if only two candidates run, both automatically advance. Based on unofficial vote counts reported after 3:15 p.m. on Friday by county registrar offices, several Assembly races have effectively been decided or set for a rematch. In Assembly District 34, which spans Los Angeles, San Bernardino, and Kern counties and includes Palmdale, Lancaster, Barstow, and other communities, incumbent Republican Tom Lackey is termed out.

Democrat Juan Carrillo, first elected in 2022, and Republican challenger Paul Andre Marsh will face off again in the general election after Carrillo received 62.3% of the primary vote. In AD-41, covering parts of northern Antelope Valley and San Bernardino communities like Adelanto and Victorville, first-term Democrat John Harabedian secured 63.3% of the vote against Republican Adam Christopher Vena, and the two will compete in November.

For AD-43, Democrat Luz Rivas, seeking a second term, captured 74.9% of the vote against Republican Ricardo Benitez, who received 25.1%. The San Fernando Valley district includes Pacoima, Mission Hills, Sylmar, and other neighborhoods. In AD-44, Democrat Nick Schultz advanced from a three-person race with 71.2% of the vote, setting up a November contest against Republican Robert Daniels, who received 20.7%. The district spans from Sherman Oaks through North Hollywood and Burbank to La Crescenta.

Democrat Jesse Gabriel, first elected in 2018, received 66.2% of the vote against Republican Tracey Schroeder in AD-46; the western San Fernando Valley district includes Encino, Van Nuys, West Hills, and Woodland Hills. Democrat Blanca Rubio, first elected in 2016 and seeking a sixth and final term, won 65.3% of the primary vote over Republican Dan Tran, whom she previously defeated; the San Gabriel Valley district covers West Covina, Baldwin Park, Glendora, Covina, Azusa, Duarte, and Monrovia.

In AD-49, Democrat Mike Fong, first elected in a 2022 special election, received 68.2% of the vote against Republican owner of The Liu Law Firm, who earned 31.8%; the district includes Alhambra, Arcadia, El Monte, Monterey Park, Rosemead, San Gabriel, San Marino, South Pasadena, and Temple City. For AD-52, Democrat Jessica Caloza led a five-person race with 54.1% and will represent East Los Angeles, including Lincoln Heights, Highland Park, Glassell Park, Eagle Rock, Los Feliz, Silver Lake, and Echo Park.

In AD-53, Democrat Michelle Rodriguez, seeking a second term, won 62.3% of the vote against Republican Rafaela Romero, a special education aide. Democrat Mark Gonzalez ran unopposed in AD-54 and will represent Koreatown, Westlake, Chinatown, Little Tokyo, Boyle Heights, Vernon, and Montebello. In AD-57, Democrat Isaac Bryan, first elected in 2021, captured 64.6% of the vote in a four-person race.

The winner will represent southeastern Los Angeles County and the San Gabriel Valley, including El Monte, Hacienda Heights, La Habra Heights, La Puente, Pico Rivera, and Whittier.
