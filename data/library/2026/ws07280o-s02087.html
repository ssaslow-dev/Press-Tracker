California leaders to ask voters to improve state's ability to save money

California lawmakers and Gov. Gavin Newsom are proposing a ballot measure to expand the state's rainy-day fund to ramp up reserves and address outstanding debts, an issue that's expected to head to voters in November.

The Save for California's Future Act would change the California constitution to increase the maximum amount of money allowed under state law to be the state's rainy-day fund from 10% of the general fund to 20%. It would also require larger mandatory deposits into reserves. The measure would also update the list of eligible debt repayments that can be made using the fund.

The updated list includes public school debt, budget debt and the more than $20 billion the state owes to the federal government for COVID-19 unemployment benefits.

"I have a great deal of confidence it's going to resonate with voters because it resonates with common sense lived experience," said Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, who is the chairman of the Assembly's budget committee. "You should put money away during good times to prepare for a rainy day. I'm quite optimistic that there's going to be a strong campaign here, but I think this idea is going to resonate deeply with a lot of Californians."

State leaders will debate and vote on whether to place the measure on the ballot Thursday.
