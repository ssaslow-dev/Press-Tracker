California’s push to ban school lunch Staples leaves parents stunned and questioning Nutrition policies

By Knewz Staff,

2026-08-21

California lawmakers are pushing a new plan that could remove some familiar foods from public school lunch menus, leaving parents to wonder what their children will eat instead. Assembly Bill 1264, introduced by Democratic Assembly member Jesse Gabriel with bipartisan support, aims to phase out certain ultra-processed foods from California public schools, per NBC News. The proposed changes would begin in 2028. The goal is to fully eliminate the targeted foods by 2032.

Bill targets ultra-processed foods

The legislation does not name specific foods that would be banned. Instead, it would direct California’s Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment to create a legal definition of ultra-processed foods and identify which products should be removed from school meals.

That could eventually affect products commonly found in children’s diets, including chips, candy, instant noodles, packaged desserts and soft drinks. Gabriel said the goal is to protect children from foods containing ingredients that could negatively affect their health.

“The more evidence we see, the stronger our conviction becomes that it is important to protect our kids from dangerous chemicals. Our schools should not be serving students ultra-processed food products that are filled with chemical additives that can harm their physical and mental health.”

Parents could see familiar foods disappear

The proposal does not mean California schools would stop serving lunch or remove every packaged food. Instead, school districts could have to replace certain products or manufacturers could have to change their recipes to meet the new requirements.

Scott Faber of the Environmental Working Group, which supports the legislation, said students would still receive meals. “Even if you’re reading the label, it’s hard to know which foods are engineered to be overeaten versus foods that are created to nourish us. We may just feed them healthier food.”

One of the biggest challenges is determining exactly what qualifies as ultra-processed food. There is currently no single universal definition for the term, which is why the bill would have state health officials establish one for California schools.

Supporters point to health concerns

Supporters say the proposal comes as concerns grow about the amount of highly processed food in American diets. Research has linked high consumption of ultra-processed foods with health problems, including heart disease, diabetes, and cognitive decline.

University of Michigan psychology professor Ashley Gearhardt has also studied the addictive qualities of some highly processed foods. She claimed: “The foods that people show common signs of addiction with are those ultra-processed foods that are high in both carbohydrates and fats in a way that we don’t see in nature. There’s evidence that this combination amplifies the brain’s reward system.”

In 2023, Gabriel helped pass the California Food Safety Act, which banned four food additives.

California’s plan has bipartisan support

The proposal is also notable for its support from Republicans. Republican Assembly Minority Leader James Gallagher, a co-author of AB 1264, said children’s health should not be a partisan issue. “When it comes to our kids, we’ve got an obesity epidemic. Our kids should be having healthy food to eat, and increasingly, that is not the case.”

The push comes as other states and federal officials are also taking a closer look at food additives and processed foods. The bill’s first major hurdle will be moving through the California Legislature before any changes reach school cafeterias.
