California passes 'Non-Ultraprocessed Certified' seal bill

Legislation would also require grocery stores in California to display products bearing the label.

National Hog Farmer Staff

September 8, 2026

schedule2 Min Read

Getty Images

The California legislature has passed legislation to increase transparency around ultraprocessed foods. Authored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D - Encino), Assembly Bill 2244 establishes a first-in-the-nation “Non-Ultraprocessed Certified” seal for food manufacturers to place on products that meet clear standards for not being ultra-processed. Modeled after the “USDA Organic” label, the "seal would provide consumers with a simple, trustworthy way to identify healthier options with a quick glance," Gabriel said. The bill also would require grocery stores in California to display products bearing the label.

“While Washington D.C. is paralyzed by inaction, Republicans and Democrats in California are joining forces to empower consumers to avoid harmful ultra-processed foods,” said Gabriel. “Like the USDA Organic label, this new seal will provide consumers with clear, trustworthy information and make it easier for them to locate healthier foods that are free from harmful additives. Parents shouldn't need a Ph.D. in chemistry to understand what they’re feeding their kids. California began this movement three years ago by banning the most dangerous food additives, and this week’s vote is the next big step toward protecting our kids from harmful ultra-processed foods.”

Supporters of AB 2244 include the Environmental Working Group, the California Medical Association, the American Academy of Pediatrics, the American Diabetes Association, the American Heart Association, the Crohn’s & Colitis Foundation and the United Nurses Associations of California. The bill passed the State Senate on a vote of 32-0 and the State Assembly 72-0.

Last year, Gabriel authored and Governor Gavin Newsom signed into law The Real Food, Healthy Kids Act (AB 1264), legislation which established the nation’s first statutory definition of ultra-processed foods and set up a process to ban products from California schools. Assemblymember Gabriel also passed into law two other bills with bipartisan support: the California Food Safety Act (AB 418, 2023) and the California School Food Safety Act (AB 2316, 2024), which he says have "helped change the national conversation about food additives and ultra-processed foods, shaped federal policy, and inspired similar legislation across more than 30 other states."

AB 2244 now heads to Governor Newsom's desk, where he will have until Sept. 30 to decide whether to veto the bill or sign it into law.
