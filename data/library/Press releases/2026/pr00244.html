Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel and Mayor Karen Bass Announce $22 Million in State Funding for Sepulveda Basin Improvements

For immediate release:
Saturday, October 24, 2026
Xavieria Terry
Communications Director
Xavieria.Terry@asm.ca.gov

LOS ANGELES, CA — Today, Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) and Assemblymember Celeste Rodriguez (D-San Fernando Valley) presented a novelty check to Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass and local leaders. The presentation symbolized the $22 million state budget appropriation secured to support community improvements at the Sepulveda Basin, in the heart of the San Fernando Valley. Spanning more than 2,000 acres, the basin is the Valley’s largest public open space.

“Delivering for the Valley means fighting to ensure our communities get their fair share of state resources, and then turning those dollars into meaningful improvements for local families,” said Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino). “I’m proud that we secured $22 million in new funding for the Sepulveda Basin. This transformative funding will make our parks safer and more accessible, reduce wildfire risk, provide relief from extreme heat, and strengthen public safety. This investment will also deliver critical resources to LAFD to help firefighters protect our neighborhoods. Valley families deserve safe, welcoming outdoor spaces, and I’ll keep fighting to deliver the investments our community deserves.”

“We’ve delivered real progress in addressing long-standing public safety threats in the Sepulveda Basin and restoring this vital public space to Valley families,” said Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass. “With the leadership of Senator Henry Stern, Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel and Assemblymember Celeste Rodriguez in Sacramento, and the partnership of Councilmember Imelda Padilla, the City will be able to expand these efforts with more resources and ensure future generations of Angelenos can enjoy the Sepulveda Basin.”

"This $22 million investment allows us to continue the vegetation management work that keeps the Sepulveda Basin safer for the people who use it and the neighborhoods that surround it,” said Los Angeles Fire Department Battalion Chief Robert Barna. “Vegetation grows back and conditions change, so reducing fire risk takes a consistent, long-term commitment. Together with our partners, we are improving access for our firefighters and strengthening our readiness to respond."

This $22 million was funded through Proposition 4, the Climate Bond approved by California voters in 2024, which provides funding to help communities prepare for extreme heat, wildfire, and other climate-related risks while improving access to parks and outdoor spaces. 

2025 Budget Allocation Breakdown for the Sepulveda Basin 

This transformative funding will serve families across the San Fernando Valley and support improvements, including park accessibility, fire prevention, extreme heat mitigation, and public safety improvements.

  • Balboa Sports Complex - $8 million: Heat mitigation and public safety upgrades to soccer fields, including the installation of shade structures, bleachers, fencing, and lighting.
  • Hjelte Sports Complex - $4 million: Paving and facility improvement.
  • Victory Boulevard Upgrades - $6 million: Installation of four permanent pedestrian crosswalks and two pedestrian stoplights to improve access into the basin.
  • Los Angeles City Fire Department - $2 million: Fire mitigation and vegetation management.
  • River Rangers, Chrysalis - $2 million: Supporting vegetation management conducted by the Conservation Corps.
  • Total = $22 million
