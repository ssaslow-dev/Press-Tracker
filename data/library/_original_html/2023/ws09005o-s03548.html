Congress Unites to Grill TikTok CEO

TikTok CEO Shou Zi Chew was unsuccessful in swaying lawmakers on either side of the aisle about the security of the social media app as they grilled him for five hours on Capitol Hill. When asked point-blank if the app's Chinese parent company ByteDance spied on Americans, Chew answered, "I don't think that spying is the right way to describe it," saying the app is safe and separate from the CPP. But officials are not convinced, with Secretary of State Antony Blinken saying the app should be "ended one way or another."

So will TikTok be banned in the U.S.? Maybe. What's more likely... an even greater effort by lawmakers and the Biden administration to push ByteDance to sell its shares of the social media app. China responded for the first time Thursday, saying it strongly opposes any forced sale of the app, used by 150 million Americans.

TL/DR: "The long-term goal of the CCP [Chinese Communist Party] is the demise of the American power, and that starts with our youth,” Republican Rep. Dan Crenshaw said, warning user data could be used to promote Chinese interests.

What happens now? TikTok doesn’t operate in China, and Chew’s attempt to convince lawmakers that it was not part of the Chinese government didn’t go over well. Lawmakers seem largely united in proposing legislation to create a federal privacy law– a first in the nation– to cover social media, data brokers and more. Expect conversations surrounding child protections online to continue among lawmakers; however, implementing a “solution” will be tricky.

U.S., Canada Strike Deal to Divert Asylum Seekers

The U.S. and Canada have reached an agreement allowing both countries to reject asylum seekers at illegal border crossings as migrant crossings have surged on both sides. Under the revised Safe Third Country Agreement, Canada will provide a refugee program for 15,000 migrants fleeing violence and persecution in South and Central America.

The deal was reached before President Joe Biden's first visit to Ottawa. It will help decrease migrant crossings into the U.S., where the issue has sparked political instability under his administration. Canada has earned the reputation of being an open economy under Prime Minister Justin Trudeau; its 2022 population surged to a record high (> 1 million) while migrants crossing more than doubled from 2019. While friendly U.S.-Canada relations that date back over 150 years were strained during the Trump era, Biden's visit is crucial to express unity on Ukraine.

TL/DR: "We’re going to talk about our two democracies stepping up to meet the challenges of our time," said John Kirby of the National Security Council.

What happens now? Biden will announce the deal alongside Trudeau today before returning to the U.S. Trudeau is set to host Biden for a state dinner in the evening. Efforts to stabilize Haiti will also be discussed. The U.S. is pushing Canada to lead an international force on the island, but those calls have been dismissed by Canada until now. The leaders are expected to talk about other pressing issues, including trade and the need to reduce Chinese dependency, national security in the wake of the Chinese balloon saga, cleaner energy and electric vehicles, among other topics.

Medvedev says Putin’s Arrest Would be ‘Declaration of War’

Russia's former president Dmitri Medvedev has warned that any attempt by Germany to arrest Vladimir Putin would be a "declaration of war." The International Criminal Court cited Russia's alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children as grounds to issue an arrest warrant last week against Putin for war crimes, a move for which German officials have expressed significant support, confirming that Putin will be arrested should he enter German territory.

It is the first time an arrest warrant has been issued against the leader of one of the five permanent members of the U.N. Security Council. Germany is one of 30 members of NATO, so Medvedev's warning suggests that any arrest would put the military alliance at war with Russia. However, President Joe Biden said the warrant was "justified." Medvedev threatened to hit the ICC building in The Hague, Netherlands, with a hypersonic ballistic missile, warning the judges to "watch the skies closely."

TL/DR: Former Russian president says Germany’s threat to arrest Putin if he sets foot on German soil is akin to a “declaration of war.”

What happens now? While Berlin may have been the most outspoken supporter of the ICC warrant, Medvedev’s pointed threats against Germany ring somewhat hollow when considering that under the Rome Statute, the ICC obliges its 123 member states to arrest Putin and hand him over to the court. Furthermore, Moscow has not recognized the ICC's jurisdiction since 2016 and has denied accusations that its forces have committed war crimes in Ukraine, indicating that Medvedev’s threat is likely to be seen as little more than a coarsening of rhetoric between Russia and the West.

French Protests Turn Violent As City Hall Burns

Bordeaux city hall was set on fire as more than a million people took to the streets across France and threw fireworks at the police in protests meant to be largely peaceful against President Emmanuel Macron's pension plan. The police fired tear gas and arrested more than 100 people. Unions have called for fresh protests, which coincide with King Charles' first overseas visit as monarch, beginning Sunday.

Despite widespread anger, Macron said he will not budge and was in Brussels for an EU leaders summit yesterday. Charles' trip to France is part of efforts to rebuild Anglo-French ties following the U.K.'s decision to exit the European Union. However, with unions refusing to work, a lavish, red-carpet welcome is unlikely. The debate over Macron's overhaul mimics an ongoing U.S. discussion over the Social Security program, which analysts predict could be insolvent in the coming years.

TL/DR: Hundreds of thousands of protestors set Bordeaux town hall on fire and violently clashed with police to protest against President Emmanuel Macron's pension plan.

What happens now? Unions called for a tenth day of strikes today and for protests on March 28. However, Macron seems to have already won the battle, pushing through the bill without a parliament vote. However, unions and demonstrators aren’t backing down. Macron's plan not only increases the retirement age but also requires people to contribute to their pension for a longer time. The bill has particularly angered those working in difficult job environments, like garbage collectors (who retire at 57) and women. “It particularly penalizes women who are expected to produce future generations of the nation, and then find they are punished for doing so," social worker Marie, 46, told the Guardian.

Segregated Graduations? Not Quite Says a Michigan University

Grand Valley State University (GVSU) is being accused of racism after revealing its plans to host several smaller graduation ceremonies for students of different races and gender identities. The public university in Allendale, Michigan, said it would host "five unique graduation celebrations designed to honor our diverse graduates," in addition to the main ceremony, including graduation ceremonies solely for Asian, Black, "Latino/a/x" and Native American students, as well as a "Lavender Graduation" celebrating LGBTQ graduates.

Conservative commentator Matt Walsh, a columnist for The Daily Wire, called the plans "ridiculous" and added: "There will be no special celebrations for straight white people, of course." In a letter to Newsweek, the university stated that its plans do not represent segregation of graduation ceremonies, claiming these smaller, more intimate celebrations would serve as a complement to GVSU's traditional Commencement. "Grand Valley State University holds unified commencement ceremonies for all of its graduates," the university said.

TL/DR: A university’s attempt at diversity has been met with claims of racism after publishing plans to hold separate graduation ceremonies for different ethnic groups.

What happens now? GVSU’s plans are nothing new; several other universities, including Columbia and Harvard have both held similar ceremonies in recent years. But predictably, concerns over Critical Race Theory (CRT) have surfaced in the wake of the university’s announcement. Republican Senator Tom Cotton claimed GVSU’s separate graduations are proof that “segregation” was “the endpoint of critical race theory,” adding: "The problem is not just one 'woke' university embracing discrimination. Critical race theory is being pushed on our kids at school, it's peddled by HR departments at corporations, and the Biden administration has embraced it under the guise of 'racial equity.'"

California Bill Targets ‘Toxic’ Chemicals in Snacks

Is California banning Skittles? Not exactly. A new bill proposes banning foods and beverages containing five chemicals over health risks. The chemicals, brominated vegetable oil, red dye 3, potassium bromate, propylparaben and titanium dioxide, are found in popular products like Skittles, Hot Tamales and Skinnygirl. Those ingredients have been linked to cancer, reproductive issues and behavioral issues in kids and are already banned in the European Union.

If the bill passes, it doesn't mean these products will disappear from store shelves; the goal is for companies to switch to healthier ingredients. "All of these chemicals are banned in Europe, but [the companies] still sell in EU by making minor modifications to their recipes," Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, who co-authored the bill, told Newsweek. In 2014, Coke and Pepsi swapped brominated vegetable oil for a healthier alternative.

TL/DR: A California lawmaker accuses the Food and Drug Administration of having a "huge loophole for approving chemicals” and wants to ban five harmful ingredients in popular foods.

What happens now? The Assembly's Health Committee is expected to hold its first hearing on the bill in April. If the bill passes, California would be the first state to ban foods with specific additives. Gabriel hopes the legislation will prompt companies to change recipes across the country. It’s unclear if the FDA would change its stance on allowing these ingredients; an FDA spokesperson told Newsweek it does not comment on proposed or pending legislation.

Distant Planet Formed 150 Million Years Ago Found to Have Water

An exoplanet 40 light years away has a swirling dust storm occurring on its surface composed of methane, carbon dioxide and water.

The planet, which orbits two stars around 235.2 trillion miles away in the direction of the constellation Corvus, was analyzed by a team of astronomers led by Brittany Miles of the University of Arizona using NASA's James Webb Space Telescope. The team found giant gritty dust storms made from silicate clouds swirling on the gas giant's surface, as well as water, methane, and carbon monoxide, and carbon dioxide, describing the findings in a paper due to be published in the journal The Astrophysical Journal Letters.

According to NASA, this is the largest number of molecules ever identified all at once on a planet outside our solar system.
