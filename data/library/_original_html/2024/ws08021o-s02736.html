California attorney general issues rebuke to Trump immigration plans

Attorney General Rob Bonta pointed to existing law that prohibits local law enforcement from arresting people for immigration enforcement purposes.

Alan Riquelmy / December 4, 2024Link copied

SAN FRANCISCO (CN) — California Attorney General Rob Bonta pushed back Wednesday against President-elect Donald Trump’s rhetoric on immigration, saying the state will support its immigrant population.

Speaking in a San Francisco library, Bonta said his office has issued guidance to public institutions detailing how to comply with state law that restricts participation in federal immigration enforcement. He pointed to Senate Bill 54, passed in 2017, which prohibits local law enforcement from using resources to investigate, detain or arrest people on immigration issues.

“We’ve all heard President-elect Trump’s calls for mass deportations,” Bonta said, noting Trump has said he intends to use the military and National Guard. “It’s against what this state, and frankly what this country, stands for.”

Trump’s election to the presidency stunned many California Democrats, who quickly responded to his anticipated return to the White House. Governor Gavin Newsom days after the election called a special session of the Legislature, which intends to allocate money to Bonta’s office for legal fights against the Trump administration.

State Senator Scott Wiener, a San Francisco Democrat, said he has a bill that would include $25 million in funding for Bonta’s office, with another $10 million for county counsels and city attorneys.

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, an Encino Democrat, has a similar bill, as well as legislation that would immediately allocate $500,000 for case preparation.

Bonta declined Wednesday to give a recommendation to lawmakers on that issue, instead saying he remains focused on ensuring local and state agencies obey laws like Senate Bill 54.

Immigrants pay taxes and contribute to both the state and nation’s economic growth, Bonta said. Decreased levels of immigration lead to economic slowdowns and higher inflation. The attorney general called Trump’s rhetoric on immigration draconian and xenophobic.

California agencies will not spend money, time or resources enforcing federal immigration law, he added.

“Immigrants are the backbone of our nation,” Bonta said. “Immigrants made America great.”

The guidance Bonta’s office is giving to public institutions like libraries, courthouses and schools is meant to help them create plans for protecting immigrant rights and enable them to safety access those facilities. That’s accomplished by restricting support for immigration enforcement.

The information is for specific institutions, though Bonta noted any facility open to the public could adopt similar policies.

The state created the guidance after working with interested parties, advocates and government agencies. It includes protections everyone has when accessing a public institution, offers policy recommendations in line with the law, and provides model policies a facility could adopt.

Some policies could include prohibiting the collection or disclosure of information that could reveal someone’s immigration status, referring law enforcement with an immigration request to a specified person and documenting all such requests.

Senate Bill 54, the law Bonta referred to, requires him to publish model policies limiting immigration enforcement in public schools, health facilities, courthouses and other locations. It was written by former state Senator Kevin De León.

“Under the Trump administration, deportations have increased 40%, including 10,800 non-criminals whose only violation was to enter the country,” De León wrote in a 2017 bill analysis. “When local police enforce immigration laws, they rapidly lose the trust of the undocumented community. Crimes go unreported for fear of deportation. The perpetrators roam free to strike again. Our communities become less — not more — safe.”

Bonta said Wednesday’s announcement is the first step. He intends to roll out a series of know-your-rights documents in the future.

“California will remain the beacon of progress it has always been,” he said.

Republicans said Bonta’s move was little more than political posturing and a misguided effort when the state should be focused on issues Californians truly care about.

“We need the fighter against the high cost of living,” said Assemblymember David Tangipa, a Clovis Republican. “The fighter against high crime rates. We need those fighters.

“In my opinion, this is a big nothing-burger,” he added.
