California Governor Signs First US Law Banning Six Harmful Food Dyes In Schools

  • Written by: Ashima Sharda Mahindra
  • Updated: Oct 2, 2024, 04:00 IST

California has become the first US state to ban certain food dyes from being used in food and drinks served at schools. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the first-in-the-nation law, barring schools from serving six artificial food dyes commonly used in cereals. The legislation would go into effect on December 31, 2027, as the California School Food Safety Act will forbid schools from serving eatables containing artificial dyes: Blue 1, Blue 2, Green 3, Red 40, Yellow 5, and Yellow 6, which are associated with a high number of ADHD cases among kids. Read on to know more.

Synthetic food dyes cause hyperactivity and neurobehavioral problems in certain children, and are carcinogenic

California has become the first US state to ban school cafeterias from serving foods that contain six artificial dyes that are extremely dangerous for health, leading to physical and mental health issues. Governor Gavin Newsom - a Democrat, signed Assembly Bill 2316 into law, according to the California School Food Safety Act, which outlaws Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1, Blue 2, and Green 3 from the meals, drinks, and snacks served at public schools.

The legislation, introduced in March this year by Democratic Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel - three years after a state report linked consuming synthetic food dyes to hyperactivity and neurobehavioral problems in certain children, aims to reduce unhealthy foods from kids’ plates.

In 2021, the California Environmental Protection Agency’s Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment also found an association of behavioural issues in some children without ADHD diagnoses. “Overall, our review of human studies suggests that synthetic food dyes are associated with adverse neurobehavioral effects, such as inattentiveness, hyperactivity, and restlessness in sensitive children,” the study said. “The evidence supports a relationship between food dye exposure and adverse behavioral outcomes in children, both with and without pre-existing behavioral disorders.”

Coloured food dyes also linked to cancer

Many extensive studies have also shown that most of the coloured dyes used in foods like burgers, ice creams, candies, and baked goods, can be carcinogenic – leading to cancer.

The law will also keep popular snacks like Flamin' Hot Cheetos, Fruity Pebbles, and Starburst away from schoolchildren in the western state.

Earlier this week, Gabriel expressed his happiness over Newsom signing the bill. “We’re feeling really proud of the fact that we are changing the national conversation around food safety,” he said. “It is very clear that these are chemicals that can harm kids and interfere with their ability to learn,” Gabriel added.

What does the law say?

According to experts, the law does not ban any foods but requires manufacturers to remove the artificial dyes in their recipes and replace them with natural alternatives – like carrot or beet juice, which a lot of companies are already doing for their consumers in the European Union.

News reports say the law will go into effect on December 31, 2027, which will give manufacturers plenty of time to reformulate their recipes.

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says the law comes as the number of diagnoses of ADHD - a neurodevelopmental disorder that affects people of all ages but is mostly common in children and teens with its characterized persistent pattern of inattention, hyperactivity, and/or impulsivity that can interfere with daily life, trended upward in recent years.
