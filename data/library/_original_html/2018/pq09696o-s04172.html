POIZNER mulls comeback -- McCARTHY under fire (and giving TRUMP candy) -- SALVADORAN order throws Valley city into crisis

By Carla Marinucci (cmarinucci@politico.com) and David Siders (dsiders@politico.com) with Candice Norwood (cnorwood@politico.com)

THE BUZZ: FIRST IN PLAYBOOK –- When we last saw Steve Poizner, he was chairing Ohio Gov. John Kasich’s 2016 presidential campaign in California. But now, the multimillionaire tech executive who’s credited with pioneering GPS technology for the cell phone looks to be seriously preparing for a return to state politics. Sources say he’s been making calls to party insiders and major donors and polling in the field this week to gauge a run for a spot he’s previously held: state insurance commissioner.

-- Insiders say Poizner, the 2010 GOP gubernatorial candidate who lost the party’s nomination to billionaire Meg Whitman, believes that his experience as a leading tech entrepreneur — combined with his past experience in the job — are strong qualifications for the post in the wake of a devastating year in which California was battered by record wildfires and deadly mudslides. And another possible attraction, backers say: Poizner stood up to Trump in the last election — and the next insurance commissioner will be called on to take a strong stand both with the administration, and with insurance companies, on behalf of California homeowners’ interests who need disaster relief.

-- But he’ll have to take on Democratic state Sen. Ricardo Lara and Republican Peter Kuo, a former candidate for state Assembly and Senate, both declared candidates in the race.

-- TRUMP EFFECT? Robert Molnar, a longtime adviser to Poizner, tells POLITICO he’s not worried that Trump’s unpopularity in California could sink any hopes of a GOP win for statewide office. “We’re very cognizant of the political environment, but back in 2006 we were running against the first Latino lieutenant governor in California history, Cruz Bustamante. And in a year in which Republicans got demolished — and George Bush had a lower approval rating than Donald Trump — Steve won,’’ he said. “We’re well aware of the troubles that Republicans have had in California. But insurance commissioner is a regulator position — and Steve served four years and was a phenomenal consumer advocate who saved Californians billions of dollars.”

-- Poizner in 2016 told POLITICO he’s thought about getting back into California politics as a “disrupter” in the GOP and argued then he’s one of just a handful in California who has racked up “a record as a successful entrepreneur” with experience in three different worlds — “the public sector, the tech sector and the not-for-profit sector.” Now it’s looking like he’s heading back into the fray. Stay tuned.

GOV DEBATE TAKEAWAYS: The first major gubernatorial debate of the year is in the books. No fatal blows at the Saturday forum, but we now have something of a baseline for the field. The toplines:

-- Gavin Newsom, with his lead in polls and fundraising, has no reason to acknowledge anyone else on stage. His job is to defend when attacked and look like he’s already governor.

-- Antonio Villaraigosa will benefit from any debate in which Republicans John Cox and Travis Allen get to participate. He

was more effective at teeing off Allen than either Newsom or fellow Democrat John Chiang.

-- Chiang needs to get his licks in on Newsom, too, if he’s going to pull his campaign out of the dustbin. And Chiang did, joining Villaraigosa to go after Newsom on health care and education. It might just be the tick that causes Chiang to end many of his sentences with a question — “Right?” — but an attack has never sounded more polite: “Gavin, you have been inconsistent, with all due respect, right?”

-- Delaine Eastin is far better on stage than her polling (dismal) and fundraising (worse) would suggest. She has almost no chance of winning this election. But she’ll benefit as long as cautious debate moderators bend over backwards to give candidates equal time.
