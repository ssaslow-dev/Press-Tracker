Voters set to pick Assembly members in November

Christian Martinez
Ventura
March 9, 2018, 9:51 p.m. ET

All 80 California Assembly seats will be up for grabs this November, and in Ventura County, that means voters decide the fate of members in four districts: 37, 38, 44 and 45.

Voting will begin in earnest on June 5, with a statewide primary election. The top two vote-getters for each seat will move on to the runoff in November. 

Residents have until May 21 to register to vote.

District 37, which includes most of Santa Barbara County as well as the cities of Ventura, Ojai, Fillmore, Santa Paula and a portion of Oxnard, is represented by Democratic Assembly member Monique Limón.

Limón, who was elected to her first term in 2016, will face Ventura resident David L. Norrdin and Sofia Maya Collin, of Santa Barbara, both Democrats.

In 2016, Norrdin ran for a seat on the Ventura Unified School District board but came in last place with 2 percent of the vote. Collin, a tutor and associate property manager, will be in her first election.

Assembly member Jacqui Irwin, a Democrat, is currently serving her second term in the Assembly  as representative of District 44, which covers Thousand Oaks, Camarillo, Moorpark, Port Hueneme and portions of Oxnard.

The Thousand Oaks resident will face two opponents in June’s primary: Democrat Robert Zelinsky, of Oak Park, and Republican Ronda Baldwin-Kennedy, of Westlake Village.

Baldwin-Kennedy is a general-practice lawyer and currently serves as dean of the American Heritage University’s School of Law. Zelinsky is a chiropractor of 14 years with a practice based in Woodland Hills.

Republican Assembly member Dante Acosta represents District 38, which includes Simi Valley, Santa Clarita and Valencia.

Acosta is currently serving his first term after defeating Democrat Christy Smith in 2016.

Smith will run for the seat again along with Libertarian Kevin Shaw and Democrat Christine LaMonica.

Smith, a Valencia resident, currently serves on the board of the Newhall School District.

Shaw said he is currently a student at Pierce College in Woodland Hills, studying philosophy and political science.

LaMonica said Friday that she had decided not to actively campaign for the spot.

Acosta’s narrow margin of victory of less than 3 percent over Smith in 2016 could prove concerning for the candidate as it represented a dramatic drop from Republican Steve Wilk’s blowout in 2016 with 66 percent of the vote.

Meanwhile, just south of Simi Valley, the unincorporated Ventura County community of Bell Canyon is part of Assembly District 45, which also includes Calabasas, Northridge and Woodland Hills.

The district has not had a representative since December when Democratic Assembly member Matt Dabaneh resigned amid sexual assault allegations.

As a result, residents of the district will first vote for a candidate to finish Dababneh’s vacated term, which ends in December. 

That process will begin on April 3 with a special primary. As in other primaries, the top two vote-getters will participate in a runoff election.

Seven Democrats, one Republican and one candidate with no party preference will appear on the ballot for the special primary in April.

The Democrats are Ray Bishop, Jeff Bornstein, Daniel Brin, C.R. Cochrane, Jesse Gabriel, Ankur Patel and Tricia Robbins Kasson. Republican Justin M. Clark and no-party candidate Dennis P. Zine are also running for the seat.

However, due to the circumstances of the special election, a candidate who receives 50 percent of the vote plus one vote in the primary will win the seat outright. Should a runoff election be required, residents will cast their vote on June 5, the same day as the statewide primaries.

Also on June 5 and on the same ballot, District 45 constituents will cast their votes in the regular primary for the Assembly seat.

The top two candidates in that primary will move on to the general election in November. The candidate selected then will serve a full, two-year term.

For the two-year term, Democrats Zachary Pizzo and Serena “Sharpie” Sharp will also vie for the seat. Cochrane will not. The other six Democrats in the April 3 primary plan to run again.

Clark, the Republican, and Zine are also running for the full term.
