SESSIONS cracks down on immigration -GUBERNATORIAL debate night preview -- DOWN-TICKET Democrats hit the airwaves

By David Siders (dsiders@politico.com) and Carla Marinucci (cmarinucci@politico.com) with Candice Norwood (cnorwood@politico.com)

THE BUZZ: Following a glut of early gubernatorial forums and debates that the California electorate has mostly snoozed through, the showcase tonight in San Jose could prove pivotal in the contest. The debate, moderated by NBC’s Chuck Todd, comes as many voters receive their mail-in ballots and turn their attention for the first time to the race. Here’s what we’re watching for:

-- Gavin Newsom, who leads the field by a mile, has two potential plays here. The inventive option is to focus all of his energy on Republican John Cox. Newsom already displayed a willingness to do this in an advertisement — boosting Cox’s name I.D. and improving the prospect that Newsom could face (and trounce) a Republican in the top-two runoff instead of a Democrat. But the safer, more probable strategy is for Newsom to say little about his opponents and much more about President Donald Trump.

-- The debate within the debate: Cox and Travis Allen have been scrapping for months over who is more conservative, more pro-Trump. Expect that to continue. The two Republicans are vying for the votes of a little more than one-quarter of the electorate. That’s a small proportion of voters in this heavily Democratic state, but the Republicans’ ability to win it over could decide whether either of them advances to the November election.

-- The other debate within the debate: If Antonio Villaraigosa is going to beat Cox and Allen to finish second, he’s largely going to do so by taking votes from Newsom and John Chiang, not either of the Republicans. Villaraigosa will have no interest in giving Chiang any airtime, however. Expect Villaraigosa to pummel Newsom, while Chiang can fire more indiscriminately from his position closer to the back of the pack.

-- Does Delaine Eastin get a bump? Eastin, who continues to lag far behind in fundraising and public opinion polls, has little chance of advancing. But if she is going to do it, tonight represents her best chance. Eastin has turned in several accomplished debate performances in front of smaller audiences this cycle. Now she needs a big moment on a larger stage.

-- Logistics: The debate runs from 6:30 p.m. to 8 p.m. in downtown San Jose. The first 30 minutes of the debate is devoted largely to issues facing Northern California, followed by a broader, 60-minute discussion of issues of statewide concern. It will air on NBC stations in San Francisco, Los Angeles, San Diego, San Luis Obispo and Fresno. KCRA in Sacramento and KSBW in Monterey will also broadcast the debate. Tweet questions to @nbcbayarea and @siliconvalleycf on using the hashtag #CAGovDebate.

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: Wealthy tech guru Steve Poizner — the guy who helped put GPS on your cell phone — wasn’t at the state Republican convention last weekend, but there was buzz about how he could affect the GOP in 2020 and beyond.

Poizner, who was a Republican gubernatorial candidate in 2010, is now an independent running for state insurance commissioner, a job he’s held before. He’s in good position, leading the polls and rounding. He’s won the endorsements of all the major newspapers, and insiders say his success could be the harbinger of a political trend. He’s launching his first TV spot today. Watch it here.

-- The rise of the independents? “He fits the mood right now: anti-establishment, sick of partisan politics on both sides,’’

says Hoover Institution fellow Lanhee Chen, who’s advised both Mitt Romney and Marco Rubio’s campaigns. “The idea of an independent on the ballot may sell quite well ... and if Steve is successful, you will see people edge in that direction.”

-- Faulconer effect? Chen says how Poizner fares in November may be closely watched by other business-friendly, moderate Republicans in California, like San Diego Mayor Kevin Faulconer, whom many GOPers begged to run for governor this year.

Faulconer as an independent candidate for governor in 2022?

-- Game changer? Poizner’s strategist, Robert Molnar, says the campaign by Poizner, a former finance chair for the presidential campaign of John Kasich, could be a game-changer for the Republican Party in the age of Trump: “What we’ve found is that there is an absolute appetite to get away from this divisive, nothing-gets-done atmosphere,’’ he said. “Steve can win this and break the mold — and show this doesn’t have to be a one party state.”
