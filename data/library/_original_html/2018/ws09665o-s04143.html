Democrats Luz Rivas and Jesse Gabriel elected to Assembly from San Fernando Valley

By Kevin Modesti

Democrats Luz Rivas and Jesse Gabriel easily defeated Republican opponents Tuesday in a pair of special elections for state Assembly seats in the San Fernando Valley left open after sexual misconduct scandals.

Rivas, an engineer and educator, beat Ricardo Benitez, a contractor, 69.8 percent to 30.2 percent in the 39th District runoff between the top two finishers in an April 3 primary race to fill out the term of Raul Bocanegra, who resigned late last year.

Gabriel, an attorney, outdistanced Justin Clark, a 19-year-old Cal State Northridge student, with 63.9 percent of the vote to 36.1 percent in the other runoff in the 45th District to complete the term of Matt Dababneh, who also stepped aside in late 2017.

The same pairs also finished first and second in separate multi-candidate primaries Tuesday and will square off again in the Nov. 6 general election for the next full two-year term.

Voter registration in the 39th District, in the northeast Valley, favors Democrats over Republicans by about 3.5 to 1. Democrats outnumber Republicans by more than 2 to 1 in the 45th, in the west and north Valley.

Those were two of three Assembly districts in Los Angeles County where this year’s elections have been shaken up by the sexual harassment scandals that have swept through government, Hollywood and media, among other industries.

In the 58th District, Assemblywoman Cristina Garcia, D-Bell Gardens, made it into the November election by finishing first with 28.7 percent, just ahead of Republican Mike Simpfenderfer’s 26.7 percent. The Democrat faced seven opponents barely two weeks after she returned from a self-imposed leave of absence as the Assembly Rules Committee investigated allegations that she groped a male legislative staff member in 2014 and used offensive language while talking about a gay colleague and Asian-Americans. The investigation cleared Garcia of the groping charge but determined that she “commonly and pervasively” used vulgar language on the job.

In an unusual situation that might confuse some voters, the special-election runoff candidates in the 39th and 45th districts also appeared on Tuesday’s primary ballot, from which the top two finishers will advance to the November election for the next full two-year Assembly terms.

In the 39th District, Tuesday’s primary candidates were Rivas, Benitez, and Democrats Patty Lopez, a former Assembly member; Antonio Sanchez, a union activist; Bonnie Corwin; and Patrea Patrick.

In the 45th, the primary candidates on Tuesday’s ballot were Gabriel, Clark, and Democrats Ankur Patel, Tricia Robbins Kasson, Daniel Brin, Jeff Bornstein and Ray Bishop.

Garcia’s opponents included City of Commerce Mayor Ivan Altamirano and Bell Gardens Councilman Pedro Aceituno, as well as Miguel Angel Alvarado, John Paul Draper, Frine Medrano and Karla Salazar, all Democrats, and Simpfenderfer, the lone Republican.

In the 39th District campaign, the MIT- and Harvard-educated Rivas argued that her experience as founder of a Pacoima-based nonprofit that promotes science, tech and math education for girls will help her  improve job training in the area that includes Arleta, Lake View Terrace, Mission Hills, North Hollywood, Pacoima, Sun Valley, Sunland-Tujunga, Sylmar and San Fernando. Benitez styled himself as a people’s candidate battling against “the corrupt corporate state.”

In the 45th District, Gabriel campaigned as an active foil of the Trump administration, noting his work to bring lawsuits on behalf of DACA recipients. Clark emphasized the importance of making state government more “functional” in dealing with local needs. The district covers the west Valley communities of Calabasas, Hidden Hills, Canoga Park, Woodland Hills, West Hills, Winnetka, Northridge, Reseda, Tarzana and Encino, and the Ventura County community of Bell Canyon.

If Clark wins in November, he apparently would become the youngest state legislator in California history, beating the record held by John J. DeHaven, who was 20 when he was sworn in in 1869 as an Assembly member from Humboldt County, according to a California legislative historian.
