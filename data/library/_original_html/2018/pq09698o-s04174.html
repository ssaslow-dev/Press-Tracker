SPEIER: Willie Brown ‘smoking something’ on gubernatorial buzz — CHIANG loses longtime strategist — MENDOZA takes leave from SENATE

By David Siders (dsiders@politico.com) and Carla Marinucci (cmarinucci@politico.com) with Candice Norwood (cnorwood@politico.com)

THE BUZZ: It took a few days, but Rep. Jackie Speier says she isn’t running for governor. After former Assembly Speaker Willie Brown wrote over the weekend that he’d heard the Democratic congresswoman was “in Palm Springs with her advisers and mulling a possible run for governor,” Speier issued this rebuke:

-- “I’m not sure who Willie’s source is, but the only thing accurate about the post was that I was in Palm Springs with my family,” Speier said in a prepared statement Wednesday. "Willie was smoking something before it became legal on January 1st. I still have a lot of work to do in Washington.”

-- That all sounds cut and dry. But several Democrats told us -- and other publications -- this week that Speier was considering a run. Speier would have faced an uphill climb. The gubernatorial field is already crowded, and she would have started at a massive fundraising disadvantage. Her last run for statewide office, in 2006, ended in defeat in the Democratic primary for lieutenant governor to now-Rep. John Garamendi.

-- Still, Speier had reason to give the governor’s race a look. The only woman in the race, Delaine Eastin, has failed to find any kind of a foothold, while Speier is drawing national attention for her involvement in the #MeToo movement. If this does turn out to be a “year of the woman,” it would appear to represent an opening for Speier. As the SacBee’s Christopher Cadelago @ccadelago put it: “So, 1) Speier was cool w/ a false rumor about her political career being floated for days and didn't think it was worth communicating with her staff to bat it down; 2) Wilie Brown was right and she really was thinking about it. Either way, would've mostly helped Republicans.”

BUENOS DÍAS, good Thursday morning. Tony Mendoza is taking a paid leave of absence from the state Senate, while Parke Skelton is leaving John Chiang’s campaign for good. A controversial bill introduced Wednesday would ban the sale of gasoline-powered cars in California by 2040.

Where’s Jerry: No public schedule.

BENCHED: Embattled state Sen. Tony Mendoza reluctantly consented to a paid leave of absence Wednesday, facing sexual harassment allegations and a potential vote in the Senate to suspend him.

-- The announcement followed an hours-long caucus with Democratic colleagues on the Legislature’s first day of session this year, and Mendoza did not go quietly. “I am deeply disappointed that I was forced into this action without any due process, which is counter to the very essence of two significant pillars of our American society … fairness and justice,” he said in a statement. “This action today has set a dangerous precedent in that any legislator can be faced with any accusation from anyone and be subjected to a harsh penalty without the benefit of presenting his/her side of the story.”

-- Mendoza said he plans to return to the Senate no later than Feb. 1. Per the LATimes’ Patrick McGreevy, “Democrats had removed Mendoza from leadership positions including a banking committee chairmanship pending the outcome of an investigation, and pressured Mendoza to take the leave. A Republican resolution to expel Mendoza was not brought up for a vote after it was introduced by Sen. Andy Vidak of Hanford.”

CHIANG RESET: Parke Skelton, John Chiang’s longtime political strategist, confirmed Wednesday he has left Chiang’s gubernatorial campaign, after Chiang brought on new consultants at the end of last year. The move comes as Chiang

struggles to remain competitive in the governor’s race; he's lagging behind Democratic rivals Gavin Newsom and Antonio Villaraigosa.

-- No explanation from Skelton. He said in an email, “John has been a friend for 25 years and I firmly believe he will be a great governor. I wish him all the best.” Chiang has brought on Left Hook and Hilltop Public Solutions.

-- Also Wednesday, Newsom announced additions to his team, adding Nathan Click as communications director and Brian Brokaw as senior political adviser. Both are alums of the Kamala Harris U.S. Senate campaign.

HEALTH CARE STALL: Assembly Speaker Anthony Rendon, who came under intense criticism when a universal health care bill stalled in his chamber last year (the legislation lacked a funding mechanism), told reporters Wednesday that “absolutely nothing has happened” with the legislation in the months since, per the SacBee. Alexei Koseff @akoseff -- “In sitdown with reporters, @Rendon63rd said ‘absolutely nothing has happened’ with single-payer proposal SB 562 since he parked it last session: ‘The sponsors of the bill have sat on their hands and done nothing the past six months.’”
