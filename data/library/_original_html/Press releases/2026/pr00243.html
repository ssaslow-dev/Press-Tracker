Amid Youth Mental Health Crisis, California and NBA Champion Team Up on New Law to Get Coaches Into the Game

For immediate release:
Friday, October 2, 2026
Xavieria Terry
Communications Director
Xavieria.Terry@asm.ca.gov

SACRAMENTO, CA — This week, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill (AB) 1626, bipartisan legislation that will put mental health and substance use disorder training in the hands of youth sports coaches. 

“For many young people, timely mental health support can be transformative,” said Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino). “Coaches are trusted mentors who are often among the first to recognize when a young person is struggling or in crisis. AB 1626 will give youth sports coaches the tools and training they need to recognize warning signs, respond appropriately, and connect young athletes with support. I’m grateful to Steph Curry and our incredible coalition for championing this important effort, and to Governor Newsom for signing AB 1626 into law.”

“I'm grateful for all the coaches who have helped me build confidence and resilience, on and off the court,” said Stephen Curry, NBA Champion. “Through Eat. Learn. Play., we've seen what's possible when coaches have the training and resources to support a young athlete's well-being, not just their game. Every kid deserves that kind of coach, and we’ve been proud to make investments to ensure coaches in Oakland have the tools to be a great leader. Policies like Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel’s help create a strong foundation for coaching practices that can make a lasting impact in an athlete’s life.”

For coaches across California, AB 1626 turns good intentions into real preparation. It requires K-12 sports coaches to complete training on recognizing the warning signs of depression, anxiety, eating disorders, substance use disorders, and suicidal ideation — and on how to connect a struggling student athlete to resources. It also directs the California Department of Education to build a model training that recreational and club coaches statewide can use to do the same. 

Authored by Assemblymembers Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), Blanca Pacheco (D-Downey), and Jacqui Irwin (D-Thousand Oaks), AB 1626 passed the State Legislature with overwhelming bipartisan support.

A Crisis Playing Out on California’s Fields and Courts

The numbers behind AB 1626 tell a stark story. Studies show that teenagers throughout the state are struggling with mental health issues at an increasing rate. More isolated than ever before, California’s young men and boys have experienced an alarming rise in suicide rates and feel more disconnected from school, work, and relationships. Nearly one in four men under 30 report having no close friends at all. Meanwhile, young female athletes face their own elevated risks of depression, anxiety, body image struggles, and eating disorders.

Coaches are often uniquely positioned to catch the warning signs, as they see young athletes for numerous hours each week, through wins, losses, and everything in between — and they’re frequently the first to notice when something is wrong. Yet only one in five coaches report feeling highly confident in their ability to support a student dealing with a mental health or substance use disorder, and four in five say they feel less than fully prepared. Left without guidance, coaches may miss the moment to step in, or at worst, make a difficult situation harder, as stigma keeps students from speaking up.

Backed by Champions On and Off the Field

AB 1626 was sponsored by the California Alliance of Children and Family Services, Mental Health America of California, the Play Equity Fund, and the California Commission on the Status of Women and Girls, and backed by a broad and diverse coalition that includes State Superintendent of Public Instruction Tony Thurmond, the San Francisco Giants, U.S. Soccer Foundation, San Francisco 49ers, NAMI California, the American Academy of Pediatrics of California, the Association of California School Administrators, and dozens of other education, youth, and mental health organizations.

“This bill is about turning awareness into life-saving action,” said Assemblywoman Jacqui Irwin (D-Thousand Oaks). “The community I represent in Ventura County lost a remarkable person when the life of Sarah Shulze was tragically cut short by suicide in 2022. Sarah was a talented multi-event runner, and her loss underscores the urgent need to better equip the adults our students trust to recognize and respond to mental health warning signs.”

“As we work to address the youth mental health crisis, we must look at the full circle of adults who support our students,” said Assemblywoman Blanca Pacheco (D-Downey). “Coaches are trusted mentors who see student athletes in high-pressure moments, yet many don’t have the tools to respond to mental health challenges. I’m glad to partner on AB 1626, in coordination with my bill, AB 1665, to equip coaches to recognize when a student is struggling and connect them to support.”
