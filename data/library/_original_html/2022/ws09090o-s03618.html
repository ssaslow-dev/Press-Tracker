Donations needed at Woodland Hills drive-thru holiday drop-off

BY Susan Carpenter Woodland Hills

LOS ANGELES — Drop off a toy and meet your California Assembly Member. That’s the concept for a drive-thru holiday toy drive taking place Tuesday at the West Valley Food Pantry in Woodland Hills.

Residents who donate new, unwrapped toys for families in need during the drive-thru can meet with California Assembly Member Jesse Gabriel, D-Woodland Hills.

“He’s handing out information. We’re giving treats to everybody. And people driving through get to talk one on one with their state assemblyman,” West Valley Food Pantry Executive Director Debbie Decker said of the third annual event.

Assembly Member Gabriel initiated the drive-thru in 2020 due to COVID-19. Last year’s drive collected about 25 bags of toys.

One of the largest food pantries in LA, West Valley works with registered individuals who are financially marginalized, including 700 families who drive through to pick up food and 600 low-income seniors that receive food delivered to their homes. Their database includes 800 to 1,000 children, said Decker, who has set a goal of collecting 1,000 toys Tuesday.

The West Valley Food Pantry drive-thru holiday toy drive takes place at Prince of Peace Church in Woodland Hills. (Photo courtesy of California Assembly Member Jesse Gabriel's office)

During a separate event after the toy drive, but prior to Christmas, food pantry clients with children will be able to pick up a donated toy of their choosing. While their kids play outdoors at that event, parents go to a separate area where the donated toys are displayed to shop without paying. The selected toy is then wrapped by food pantry staff and put in a garbage bag for parents to put in their vehicles for their unsuspecting children.

“The toys they pick out are the things that their kids will play with, rather than some Santa handing a girl a toy for a child age 6 to 9,” Decker said. “Parents know their kids. They know what their kids will play with. They know their kids’ interests.”

Whether it’s a Lego set, a board game or a stuffed animal, Decker recommends that individuals who donate toys bring what their own kids would love.

“We try to make the best use of the gifts and get it to the kids who are going to love it because that’s really what Christmas is all about,” Decker said. “It’s being thoughtful. It’s being kind. It’s understanding what the needs are rather than just a handout.”

The drive-thru holiday toy drive takes place 3:30 to 6:30 p.m. Tuesday at the West Valley Food Pantry at Prince of Peace Church in Woodland Hills. To participate, people can RSVP here.
