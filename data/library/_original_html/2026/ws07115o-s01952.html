Editor’s Desk: Jesse Gabriel Is Succeeding where RFK Jr. Is Failing | Food and Environment Reporting Network

By Brent Cunningham

It’s no secret that RFK Jr. has not delivered for his MAHA constituents. On everything from glyphosate to ultra-processed foods, Kennedy has either yielded to Trump’s agenda and corporate lobbying, opted for toothless, voluntary regulation, or doubled down on other, retrograde priorities—like undermining seed oils and vaccines. 

Meanwhile, in the California State Assembly, Jesse Gabriel, a Democrat, has been quietly enacting a series of laws that do many of the things Kennedy promised: banning dyes and additives from school lunches, establishing the nation’s first statutory definition of ultraprocessed foods, and creating a label to help shoppers identify foods with minimally processed ingredients.

And as Jane Black explains in the final piece of our special series, MAHA and the Midterms, produced with Mother Jones, because of the size of California’s market, Gabriel’s legislation is forcing food companies to “clean up their act nationwide.” In fact, state legislatures across the country are succeeding where Kennedy has failed, “introducing more than 150 food safety bills since 2023,” Black writes. “The 15 states that have passed new laws are about equally divided between red and blue.”

This special series examined how shifting alliances and allegiances around the food issues MAHA champions might influence the midterm elections. It took several months to conceive, report, and publish. We think these stories represent some of the best journalism that’s been done on a movement that has captivated the country and whose issues affect us all.
