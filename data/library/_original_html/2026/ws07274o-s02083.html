Plan to expand California’s ‘rainy day fund’ set for November ballot

Brett Stover

(Inside California Politics) — Just hours before the deadline to place propositions on the November ballot, lawmakers approved a proposal that would allow the state to save significantly more money in a move that could help California better weather future economic downturns.

On Thursday, the state Senate and Assembly approved the “Save for California’s Future Act,” which is backed by the governor along with Democrats in both chambers of the Legislature. The act will now go to California voters, who will have the opportunity to approve — or reject — the proposition in November.

Asm. Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, called the act a “commitment to responsible stewardship” that will help future generations of Californians.

“Just like California families save for emergencies and unexpected expenses, our state government should set aside funding during good years to protect schools, public safety and essential public services,” the Assembly budget chair said in a statement. “By placing this proposal on the November ballot, we are giving California voters the power to create a stronger and more resilient rainy day fund.”

That fund — the Budget Stabilization Account — is currently capped at 10% of General Fund tax revenues. When it exceeds that threshold, California lawmakers must instead spend money that would have otherwise been saved on infrastructure.

Amid concerns over a future stock market crash, Democratic leaders from both chambers floated the idea earlier this year, and Gov. Gavin Newsom agreed this summer amid budget negotiations.

If voters approve the act, the constitutional cap on the Budget Stabilization Account would double to 20%. It would also increase annual deposits in the fund during years with high levels of capital gains taxes — money generated by stock marked booms that contribute to California’s often-irregular revenue levels.

Additionally, the proposition would expand the list of allowable payments from state reserves, allowing that money to be used to pay down debt including federal unemployment insurance loans.

Newsom called the act a “common sense” measure that would safeguard key programs and investments in the event of economic strife.

Not everyone is happy with the proposal, however. Asm. Carl DeMaio, R-San Diego, told Inside California Politics he believes lawmakers will simply save money instead of returning it to taxpayers.

The ballot measure would exclude deposits in the BSA from the “Gann limit,” a spending cap that requires excess tax revenue be paid out to schools and to taxpayers.

“So they can keep more of our money with high taxes and use that money to fund their wasteful spending?” DeMaio said. “This is insanity, and to call it fiscal responsibility? That’s chutzpah.”

Inside California Politics correspondent Eytan Wallace contributed to this story.
