California backs non-ultra-processed food label

    California lawmakers have approved legislation to create a voluntary certification scheme for packaged foods that meet standards for not being ultra-processed.

    Assembly Bill 2244 passed the State Senate by 32-0 and the State Assembly by 72-0. The legislation, introduced by assembly member Jesse Gabriel, will now go to Governor Gavin Newsom, who has until 30 September to sign or veto it.

    Under the proposals, qualifying food manufacturers would be able to apply for a ‘Non-Ultraprocessed Certified’ seal for use on product packaging.

    The California Department of Public Health would oversee the certification programme, with applications handled through accredited certification agents.

    The system is modelled on the USDA Organic programme and is intended to provide a standardised way of identifying packaged foods that meet the state’s criteria for non-ultra-processed products.

    California grocery retailers would also be required to display certified products, potentially giving participating manufacturers greater visibility in stores.

    The legislation follows a series of California measures targeting food ingredients and processing. In 2025, Newsom signed the Real Food, Healthy Kids Act, which introduced a statutory definition of ultra-processed foods and established a process for restricting certain products in schools.

    Gabriel previously authored the California Food Safety Act of 2023 and the California School Food Safety Act of 2024, which introduced further restrictions relating to food additives.

    AB 2244 is supported by organisations including the Environmental Working Group, California Medical Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, American Diabetes Association and American Heart Association.
