Gov. Gavin Newsom signed legislation creating the first-ever "Non-Ultraprocessed Certified" seal that food manufacturers can place on products that meet standards for not being ultra-processed.

Modeled after the USDA Organic label, the seal is intended to give shoppers a simple, front-of-package cue to identify healthier options without parsing lengthy ingredient lists, according to Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, who authored the bill.

"Like the USDA Organic label, this new seal will provide consumers with clear, trustworthy information and make it easier for them to locate healthier foods that are free from harmful additives. Parents shouldn't need a Ph.D. in chemistry to understand what they're feeding their kids," a statement from Gabriel said in part.

The legislation establishes a process overseen by the California Department of Public Health in which food manufacturers can apply through accredited certification agents to use the "Non-Ultraprocessed Certified" label on qualifying products.

The bill also requires California grocery stores to prominently identify products bearing the seal.

However, critics have warned the labels could confuse shoppers because certification programs often rely on different definitions, standards, and verification methods.
