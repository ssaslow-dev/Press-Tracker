(Inside California Politics) — A grieving Sacramento mother whose son was gunned down just blocks from the state Capitol is fighting to make sure no other family has to feel that pain.

"Every moment of every day, we remember him," she told Inside California Politics.

For more than a year, Deborah Grimes pushed to ban the sale of certain guns that can be converted to automatic weapons with a "switch" device. And last fall, Gov. Gavin Newsom signed a bill to do just that into law.

However, the debate is not over.

On the evening of July 4, 2022, Grimes' son, Gregory Najee Grimes, was shot seven times. The 31-year-old father and beloved high school football coach was killed in a shooting that injured four others, just a short distance from the California Capitol.

Grimes said her son was shot so many times because the shooter, who was recently sentenced to 25-years-to-life in prison, was able to convert a semi-automatic pistol into an automatic weapon by simply installing a device that allowed the gun to fire off multiple rounds per second.

"The switch that the perpetrator had was out of control, going everywhere," Grimes said. "The thought of that, what my son was experiencing — he tried to get away but could not outrun the bullets. That haunts me."

That's why Grimes spent much of last year supporting Assembly Bill 1127. Authored by Asm. Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, the new law will prohibit gun manufacturers from selling semi-automatic pistols that can be easily converted into machine guns.

"It does something that I think is very common sense, which says, 'We should not have illegal machine guns on our streets,'" Gabriel said.

With the support of Grimes and victims of gun violence, AB 1127 ultimately passed and was signed by Newsom last fall.

"Gun violence is the leading cause of death for children in the U.S," Gabriel said. "That's shameful, that's sad and that's wrong. It's something we as policymakers have to step up and address. […] If we're going to chose between the profits of the gun industry and the safety and the lives of our kids, that our kids come first. I think most Californians would agree with that."

However, several gun dealers and gun rights groups are opposed to the new law. They say the specific switch device is already illegal in California — a felony offense. The law has since been taken to court.

"You are making something that is already illegal, illegal-er on the hope that you are going to somehow make criminals comply with the law," Gun Owners of California Executive Director Emeritus Sam Paredes said. "Nothing can be further from the truth."

The law is still in effect and a judge has not yet issued a ruling on the lawsuit.

Grimes, meanwhile, said she will continue to fight for AB 1127 and other similar bills. She has now created a foundation named for her late son; it's aimed at promoting responsible gun training and reducing gun violence.
