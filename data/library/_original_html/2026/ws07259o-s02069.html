Governor Newsom signs California's record $351.7 billion state budget

SACRAMENTO, Calif. — Governor Gavin Newsom signed California's $351.7 billion budget Monday, approving a balanced spending plan for the 2026-27 fiscal year. Newsom and state Democratic leaders emphasized their plan not only eliminates a deficit for this year but also the following.

State reserves total $28.8 billion, according to a spokesperson for the Department of Finance.

The budget takes effect Wednesday, July 1, the start of the state's new fiscal year. It is the final budget of Newsom's administration before he leaves office.

Lawmakers sent the spending plan for the governor’s desk Monday after weeks of negotiations between the governor's office, the state Senate and the Assembly. The agreement passed despite Republican opposition because Democrats hold a supermajority in the Legislature.

The Legislature also passed what’s called budget trailer bills — specific provisions on how the money allocated for state programs in the actually budget bill will and should be utilized.

Among notable deliverables, the final budget restores or delays several health care reductions Newsom initially proposed. Lawmakers rejected cuts to In-Home Supportive Services, delayed raising Medi-Cal premiums for undocumented immigrants and pushed back the full reinstatement of the Medi-Cal asset test for seniors and people with disabilities.

Legislative analyst Chris Micheli said lawmakers deferred some health care funding decisions until next year, leaving California's next governor to decide whether to implement those cuts.

Republicans critics described the delays as Newsom “kicking the can down the road.”

The budget also includes $900 million for the Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention program, about $400 million more than Newsom originally proposed. It provides more than $40 million for elections to increase staffing, expand voter outreach, educate voters and combat misinformation, as promised by Newsom both before and in the weeks following the June primary election.

According to the governor's office, the budget continues funding for child care, affordable housing, public schools, health care programs, wildfire resilience and public safety while preserving nearly $30 billion in state reserves.

Republicans criticized the budget, arguing California continues to spend more than it takes in. Indeed, while the 2026-2027 fiscal year budget reflects higher-than-anticipated revenues due to the stock market boom as a result of success with artificial intelligence, experts and analysts note such revenue is volatile.

Republicans also continue to object to new tax proposals, including a renewal of  the managed care organization tax — the tax rate will be higher for private insurers. Critics, as well as Democratic leaders themselves, acknowledge such a tax increase on health plans could increase monthly premiums for Californians. About $100 more annually per enrollee, estimates show.

There will also be an expansion of the state's sales tax to some software and digital products, starting next year. The base sales tax will be 7.25%, but local jurisdictions can add on.

Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel said lawmakers worked to protect vulnerable Californians from federal funding reductions.

“We heard from many members of the Democratic caucus that that was simply unacceptable and that we needed to step up to protect our vulnerable communities… A lot of the work we’re doing is to soften the blow of those federal cuts to protect families in California. Unfortunately, we’re not in a place to backfill every federal cut, but we do feel a responsibility to protect our communities,” Democrat Gabriel said.

Assembly Budget Vice Chair David Tangipa said the state's spending priorities remain misplaced despite record revenue.

“While the state has always talked about cuts coming from the federal administration, there’s nothing that looks like we’re actually running out of money. We have more money than we’ve ever had before. And yet, we believe that some of the priorities are off in this budget,” Tangipa, a Republican, said.

Newsom also signed several budget trailer bills Monday to implement the spending plan, including measures covering health care, education, taxation and human services.
