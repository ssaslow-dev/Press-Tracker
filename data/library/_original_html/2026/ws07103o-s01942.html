One Thing To Know

analysis

By Marc Rod

If Democrats take control of Congress next year — or even if they win back a majority in only the House of Representatives — expect a legislative agenda that’s a lot tougher on Israel than it has been in a long time.

With growing support among House Democrats for legislation restricting U.S. aid and arms sales to Israel, bills such as the Block the Bombs Act, the Ceasefire Compliance Act or measures similar to this summer’s vote on cutting aid to Israel could come to the House floor, and continued fights over the U.S.-Israel relationship in funding bills and other legislation seem likely.

But the top target for senior Democrats at the moment is targeting settlement activity and settler violence in the West Bank. Asked by Jewish Insider at a press conference on Wednesday what Israel-related legislation Democrats might bring forward for a vote, House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries (D-NY) — who has generally been a reliable supporter of the U.S.-Israel relationship — pointed to a bill imposing sanctions related to West Bank violence.

The Big Reads

taking aim

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu pushed back against criticism of Israel over the war in Gaza and settler violence in the West Bank in his address to the United Nations General Assembly on Thursday, taking aim at New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, French President Emmanuel Macron, Qatar and Turkey, Jewish Insider’s AJ Rodriguez reports. The prime minister also rejected accusations that Israel committed genocide in Gaza, directly addressing Mamdani, who has repeatedly called Netanyahu a war criminal. “Israel didn’t commit genocide. Israel prevented genocide,” Netanyahu said. He also raised that Mamdani’s wife, Rama Duwaji, had liked multiple Instagram posts celebrating Hamas’ Oct. 7, 2023, attacks. Netanyahu went on to accuse foreign countries of spending “untold billions” to spread anti-Israel messages through traditional and social media, singling out Qatar for “massively funding American universities and toxic media outlets.”

Tumult in Turtle Bay: Several far-left Democrats expected to be elected to the House of Representatives in November — New York’s Darializa Avila Chevalier and Brad Lander, New Jersey’s Adam Hamawy, Pennsylvania’s Chris Rabb and Colorado’s Melat Kiros — attended a protest on Thursday organized by Jewish Voice for Peace that blocked the streets outside United Nations headquarters during Netanyahu’s speech, JI’s Marc Rod reports. Chevalier was among those arrested by the NYPD for refusing to comply with an order to disperse.

apa fray

A federal advisory board to the Department of Education recommended that the department suspend the accreditation authority of the American Psychological Association over the APA’s failure to address a “pervasive culture of antisemitism within the programs it accredits,” Jewish Insider’s Marc Rod reports. The recommendation comes after years of concerns by Jewish students and professionals about pervasive antisemitism and anti-Israel sentiment in psychology education, which in some cases paints Zionism as a mental illness to be treated. A final decision on the issue will be made in the coming months by the Department of Education, and the exact implications and consequences may depend on the decision made. But the recommendation could create significant uncertainty for hundreds of programs and internships and throw into question the licensing of psychology students and professionals across the country.

regime reckoning

Pressed during an event at the University of Michigan on Wednesday to condemn the Iranian regime’s massacre of tens of thousands of protesters earlier this year, Democratic Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed sidestepped the question, focusing his answer on criticizing the U.S. war in Iran, Jewish Insider’s Marc Rod reports. A Persian American student — who said that his cousin had been “hunted in the streets” by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps for wearing her hijab improperly and that his uncle was “sentenced to die [and] received 50 lashings” — pressed El-Sayed to “renounce” those who supported assassinated Iranian Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. El-Sayed responded by criticizing the U.S. for taking the role of the “world’s policeman,”adding, “Here’s my point: one can disagree with the governance of a foreign country. One can stand in solidarity with freedom and democracy movements, but my worry is when one decides that we are going to go in and take out the leadership of a foreign government.”

Jeb Bush and Mark Wallace, respectively the chairman and CEO of United Against Nuclear Iran, call on the Trump administration to crack down on Malaysia over the support that entities in the country are providing to Iran. “While the focus has been on the Strait of Hormuz, a lower-profile maritime front thousands of miles away affects how much control Iran retains. Wine of the Week

2001 Ben Haim Merlot

Opening any aged bottle of Israeli wine is a wager. The cork comes out, the glass fills, and in an instant the wine declares itself: spoiled, merely drinkable or something closer to a revelation. Last Friday night, over a spectacular Shabbat dinner with dear friends from Rome and New Hampshire, we scored twice, and I was urged to share the highlights with my dear readers. The first bottle was a 2001 Ben Haim Merlot, a masterpiece on release and only more extraordinary for the quarter century it has spent quietly composing itself in the cellar. All port all the time, was the thought that crossed my mind as it touched my palate: deep, plummy, warm, its edges rounded by patience into something almost confectionary. Few of us will ever hold this bottle again. Take heart. The second wine on the table that evening remains within reach.

The Tulip Fingerprint 2014 is a work of art in a bottle. Five grapes married over 36 months in oak, it opens with a certain hauteur, rigid and demanding, presenting all five varietals for inspection in a single nanosecond, and every one arriving in full dress. The mid-palate yields to wild berries, and the finish unfolds at leisure into dark chocolate and hot fudge. For all its age, this wine will hold another 15 years with grace. Serve it alongside sautéed short ribs over a bed of farro, and give it the evening it deserves.
