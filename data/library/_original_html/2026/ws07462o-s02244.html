Republicans navigate Alex Pretti’s killing

RED LINE — National Republicans have signaled unease over the killing of ICU nurse Alex Pretti in Minneapolis, but you won’t catch the leading GOP candidates for governor crossing the Trump administration.

Riverside County Sheriff Chad Bianco appeared to sympathize with the federal Border Patrol officers in a social media post Sunday that did not explicitly mention the incident, saying, “Celebrities and talking heads think they understand what it’s like to put on a uniform and make life or death decisions.”

Former Fox News host Steve Hilton issued a joint statement with Michael Gates — a candidate for attorney general who spent part of last year in the Trump administration Justice Department — blaming the violence in part on blue city and state policies that limit cooperation between federal agents and police.

“The violence and lawlessness we are seeing in Minneapolis and other ‘sanctuary’ states and cities, where people are getting injured and even killed, is happening because of reckless ‘sanctuary’ policies that have put federal enforcement in our streets and neighborhoods,” they wrote.

BIG PICTURE: There is an outside chance both Bianco and Hilton advance through California’s June top-two primary if the sprawling field of Democratic candidates continues to split voters. The two Republicans have been leading in several public and private polls due to the large number of Democrats in the race and with a high proportion of voters remaining undecided.

By avoiding criticism of the president, each keeps alive the chance of getting an endorsement from President Donald Trump that would help the winner consolidate the GOP vote before the primary — important if only one Republican can make it to the ballot in November. But presidential allegiance could prove problematic if Hilton or Bianco ends up in a head-to-head matchup against a Democrat in the deeply blue state.

Hilton’s take … Hilton told Playbook that he hasn’t asked Trump for his endorsement and did not issue his statement with the president in mind. “My positions on any of these issues are based on what I think,” he said.

There’s a “100 percent” chance the run-off election will include one Republican and one Democrat, he said, and he’s operating under that assumption.

“The unions and the Democrat machine will get together, and they will try to choose the Republican that they want to run against, the one that they consider to be the weakest candidate, and they will promote that person,” causing consolidation on the GOP side, he predicted.

Getting Loomered … That’s not to discount the GOP’s role in picking favorites. Conservative activist and Trump adviser Laura Loomer tore into Bianco on Monday for a well-documented clip of the sheriff kneeling with Black Lives Matter protesters in 2020 and for declining to violate California’s sanctuary policy that restricts law enforcement cooperation with ICE.

One outlier … Jon Slavet, a recent GOP entrant who has been running to his opponents’ middle, broke with his party and called for ICE to withdraw from Minneapolis.

Across the map … Some endangered Republican House members have not commented on Pretti’s death. Rep. Ken Calvert, who is running for a new, deep-red Southern California district against fellow GOP Rep. Young Kim, advocated for cooperation between police and immigration agents, but called for a review of the killing, which he called a “tragedy.” Rep. Darrell Issa, a conservative Trump ally facing a tough primary due to Prop 50, praised the president’s response.

RELATED: State Democratic lawmakers responded by announcing immigration legislation Monday, Nicole Norman reports for subscribers. Assemblymembers Jesse Gabriel and Juan Carrillo will introduce proposals to prevent state property from being used for immigration operations and require California’s attorney general to investigate any incident in which a federal immigration officer is involved in a shooting.

Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas also signaled support for congressional Democrats forcing a government shutdown over Department of Homeland Security funding.

“I’m calling on our national Democratic leaders to stand resolutely against this president. If that means shutting down the federal government, then so be it,” he told reporters.

Rivas called on Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer to “lead with strength” or “step aside.”

CAMPAIGN YEAR

TOM TALKS TAXES — Tom Steyer, the progressive billionaire running for governor, released a nearly 900-word Substack post Monday sharing his thoughts on the wealth tax ballot initiative that has panicked billionaires, strained organized labor and gotten an unequivocal thumbs-down from Gov. Gavin Newsom.

Steyer’s own position is harder to pin down. His op-ed says he “has real concerns” about the design of the one-time, 5 percent tax that would overwhelmingly go toward backfilling federal cuts to Medi-Cal. But, he added, “if there’s an opportunity to tax wealthy people to fund health care and education, I’d vote for it all day long.”

So is he backing the lightning rod initiative from SEIU-UHW? Some media outlets interpreted it that way. But Steyer, in an interview with Playbook, stopped short of endorsing the initiative. “I am not,” he said. “I’m saying this: That ballot measure addresses a real need. It does it in a specific way … I believe there are other ways and probably better ways to solve that problem.”

Steyer, in his essay and in the interview, was more enthusiastic about his proposals to bring in more revenue — a “split roll” policy that would change how commercial properties are taxed and closing the so-called “water’s edge” loophole on corporate profits shifted overseas.

He also said he backed the permanent extension of higher income tax rates to fund public education, which a coalition of labor unions is seeking to put on the November ballot. Those supporters have worried that a second revenue-raising measure, such as the wealth tax, could imperil both initiatives’ prospects.

“I know that people have worried about that, and the people who have worried about it are smart,” he said. “But I want to see what actually ends up on the ballot.” — Melanie Mason

GUESS WHO’S BACK — Former Democratic Assemblymember Rudy Salas is communicating to people that he’s open to challenging frontline Rep. David Valadao again this year but has not taken concrete steps toward launching a campaign, he told Playbook on Monday.

Salas, who fell short of ousting Valadao each of the last two cycles, said he’s waiting until closer to the March candidate filing deadline to make a decision. He would join a fierce primary between moderate Assemblymember Jasmeet Bains and progressive Visalia school board member Randy Villegas.

Valadao’s district became slightly more Democratic under Proposition 50 and, as one of California’s most competitive congressional battlegrounds, it could be pivotal to House control in the midterms.

STATE CAPITOL

NEWSOM GETS CLAIM SHARKS — State senators today passed a bill clamping down on so-called veteran benefits ‘claim sharks,’ sending it to Newsom’s desk after a contentious legislative battle.

The bill from Assemblymember Pilar Schiavo and state Sens. Bob Archuleta and Sabrina Cervantes would ban unaccredited attorneys and agents from charging veterans for assisting them with benefit claims.

Former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi and California Rep. Mark Takano advocated on behalf of SB 694, which drew fierce opposition from the veteran claims industry. Senators approved the legislation with a 25-6 vote.

Asked after the vote if proponents think the governor will sign the bill, Schiavo said they’re “hopeful and optimistic.”

“The governor has been a huge champion for protecting consumers,” she said. “We believe that this is in line with his values and the values of Californians.” — Lindsey Holden

ENDORSEMENTS

A SHADOW CAMPAIGN PAYS OFF — Scott Wiener is on track to receive the California Democratic Party’s endorsement to succeed Nancy Pelosi in Congress — and it isn’t even a close contest.

Over the weekend, Wiener received support from 117 out of 151 delegates — or more than 77 percent — in a preendorsement vote for San Francisco’s open House seat. Supervisor Connie Chan received 30 votes, or less than 20 percent. Wealthy former tech executive Saikat Chakrabarti received no votes.

Wiener is now all-but-assured to notch the endorsement at the party’s Feb. 20-22 convention. His showing speaks to how Wiener, an influential state senator, spent years building his shadow campaign to succeed Pelosi, including by packing local Democratic groups with his allies who can help secure the nod.

It’s a welcome shot of adrenaline for Wiener, a powerful state senator, after a bruising few weeks on the campaign trail over his stance on Israel’s actions in Gaza.

NO SMALL FRY — Josh Fryday has been endorsed by the state’s two largest teachers’ unions in the race for lieutenant governor, the California Teachers Association and the California Federation of Teachers. Fryday, who works as the chief service officer in Newsom’s Cabinet, proposes to expand the state’s program to help subsidize college costs for students who volunteer in K-12 schools or with other community programs.

BIG NODS FOR KIM — Jane Kim, who announced her candidacy two weeks ago, has quickly racked up endorsements in the race for state insurance commissioner. Kim, a former San Francisco supervisor, has recently received nods from the California Teachers Association, California Federation of Teachers, SEIU California, the National Union of Healthcare Workers, Sen. Bernie Sanders and the California Working Families Party (she’s on leave as the party’s executive director).

ON THE HILL

LURIE TO WASHINGTON — San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie is traveling to D.C. today to attend the U.S. Conference of Mayors. But he is also expected to visit Capitol Hill to urge lawmakers to undo federal cuts hammering his city’s budget.

An adviser said Lurie plans to meet with California Sens. Adam Schiff and Alex Padilla, along with a host of other lawmakers, including Hawaii Sen. Brian Schatz. In addition to reversing cuts from Trump’s “One Big Beautiful Bill,” the mayor is expected to urge the senators to support increasing funding for disaster relief, seawall construction and other infrastructure.

Lurie is also scheduled to speak during the mayors’ forum, including at a fireside chat with Oklahoma City Mayor David Holt, president of the conference.

TECH TALK

FIRST IN POLITICO: STOP THE FLOP — Newsom is reviewing whether social media platform TikTok is violating state law by censoring content critical of Trump, our colleague Tyler Katzenberger scooped Monday evening.

“It’s time to investigate,” Newsom said in an X post confirming the probe.

Newsom’s review comes amid wider accusations that TikTok is flagging for review or throttling criticism of the Trump administration and Immigration and Customs Enforcement, just days after the platform closed a Trump-backed deal to avoid a U.S. ban.

In a separate X post Monday, the governor’s press account said Newsom was launching the review after his office “received reports — and independently confirmed instances — of suppressed content critical of President Trump.” The post further said Newsom is “calling on the California Department of Justice to determine whether it violates California law.”

More TikTok drama: Wiener told Playbook on Monday that a recent TikTok video he posted criticizing ICE received zero views. When asked about Wiener’s claim, a TikTok spokesperson pointed to a company X post that attributed recent bugs, including engagement concerns to a “major infrastructure issue” caused by a power outage at one of its U.S. data center sites.

“Clearly, TikTok is suppressing criticisms of ICE,” Wiener said.

TOP TALKERS

NURSES OUT — Tens of thousands of Kaiser Permanente health care workers in California and Hawaii walked off the job early Monday to kick off an open-ended strike, the Los Angeles Times reports. The union representing the striking workers, the United Nurses Association of California/Union of Health Care Professionals, said it launched the strike in the wake of stalled contract talks.

TECHIES AGAINST ICE — Bay Area tech workers are circulating a petition asking their CEOs to use their clout with Trump to curb ICE’s activities. Our colleagues at California Decoded report that as of Monday afternoon, over 500 workers at firms including Google, Meta, and OpenAI had signed the petition asking CEOs to cancel any contracts with ICE, speak out against the agency’s violence and demand the Trump administration pull officers out of cities. The petition also references Trump’s calling off a “surge” of officers to San Francisco after speaking with Mayor Daniel Lurie, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff.
