Why Silicon Valley’s Matt Mahan bubble is bursting

Presented by Californians for Energy Independence

WRITING ON THE WALL — Silicon Valley’s effort to place Matt Mahan in the governor’s mansion is stalling out amid recriminations, donor frustration and second-guessing.

The San Jose mayor entered California’s wide-open governor’s race as tech’s chosen champion: a centrist Democrat with founder credentials, elite backers and promises of tens of millions of dollars to catapult him through the June primary.

But his underwhelming campaign has become a cautionary tale about an untested candidate and inexperienced donors chasing a vision that has failed to materialize with a week left — a humbling experience for some of Mahan’s backers in tech, many of them relative newcomers to state politics who are working to build enduring political power in California.

Undergirding the disappointment is a characteristically Silicon Valley attitude about setbacks and millions squandered: They went fast and broke things, and in the process learned something about politics.

“This is our education,” said Garry Tan, a venture capitalist who has been one of Mahan’s most vocal supporters.“This is first grade, second grade for me, personally. We won’t be first and second graders forever.”

The case for Mahan’s viability always rested on an infusion of quick cash. As a first-term mayor who was unknown to most Californians, Mahan would need an enormous windfall to propel him into contention — especially after entering the race late and leaving himself just months to break through. Supporters helped lure him in by assuring him the money would be there.

It was a plausible promise. A former tech entrepreneur with a Harvard connection to Facebook co-founder Mark Zuckerberg, Mahan nurtured deep ties to his hometown industry and followed a line of tech-friendly California Democrats like Rep. Sam Liccardo.

Joe Green, a Harvard roommate of Zuckerberg’s who recruited Mahan to his startup more than a decade ago, became campaign chair and a conduit to the industry. An outside committee backing Mahan quickly raised millions of dollars. Bay Area venture capitalist Michael Moritz and Los Angeles mall magnate Rick Caruso — who passed on running himself — backed Mahan and worked their connections to raise money. Green, Moritz and Caruso did not respond to requests for comment.

But the money never matched the ambition. The principal independent expenditure committee’s presentation to donors estimated they would need around $46 million. It managed roughly half of that.

“He raised the worst-of-all-worlds kind of money,” said a campaign supporter who was granted anonymity to describe internal dynamics. “Enough that he ran a real campaign, but not enough to get the … name ID that he needs.”

Mahan supporters have still poured nearly $50 million into his campaign and allied PACs, one of which trailed only billionaire Tom Steyer in overall spending. But he’s never caught or overtaken the race’s top Democrats.

Mahan spokesperson Tasha Dean said in a statement that Mahan had shown strength despite entering the race “a virtual unknown against well-known repeat candidates,” arguing “the clickbait class and our opponents have spent more time talking about poll numbers than plans to fix our problems.”

GOVERNOR'S RACE

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: CLOSING HIT — A super PAC created to oppose Tom Steyer’s campaign for governor will today launch a $4 million closing TV ad buy, hammering the billionaire climate activist over allegations that he hasn’t properly reported payments to content creators and influencers.

“Tom Steyer says he’ll do anything to buy the governor’s race,” the 30-second ad states. “Now he’s under investigation by California’s government ethics watchdog.”

The spot alludes to complaints supporters of Xavier Becerra have filed with the California Fair Political Practices Commission, accusing Steyer of failing to disclose those payments. Steyer’s campaign has, in turn, accused Becerra and various influencers of failing to report payments.

Steyer has poured roughly $200 million of his own wealth into the primary, making his run the most expensive candidate campaign in state history — surpassing Meg Whitman’s 2010 bid for governor (not accounting for inflation).

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: HOMESTRETCH HELP — Bay Area Rep. Lateefah Simon has endorsed Steyer, praising him in a statement for using “his own resources to take on the fossil fuel industry, to fund climate justice in the communities that needed it the most, and to stand with working people.”

FOR GOOD MEASURE

SIGNATURES INCOMING — Supporters of a Bay Area ballot measure to save ailing transit systems said they have submitted more than 300,000 signatures to qualify their measure — all but ensuring it will appear on the ballot for millions of voters in the region.

The proposal would hike sales taxes in five counties, including San Francisco, Santa Clara and Alameda counties, to spare BART, Muni and other transit systems from severe service cuts. To qualify, supporters only need 186,000 valid signatures, so they have a healthy cushion.

“We blew through our goal — showing clearly that the Bay Area supports public transit and the strength of the broad coalition behind this effort,” said Jeff Cretan, a spokesperson for the Connect Bay Area campaign.

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: ONSLAUGHT CONTINUES — The wealth tax-fighting Golden State Promise is going up with a new spot today featuring leaders from the California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce and the CalAsian Chamber blasting the measure. Part of an $8 million ad buy, the ad is set to run on digital and broadcast statewide. Watch it here.

TOP TALKERS

OUT OF THE WOODS YET? Fire officials said Monday that the risk of a massive chemical explosion has been alleviated for residents of Orange County. But, as the Los Angeles Times reports, evacuation orders remained in place for some 50,000 residents of Garden Grove, Cypress, Stanton, Anaheim, Buena Park and Westminster.

The Orange County Fire Authority said the risk of pressurized methyl methacrylate exploding at an aerospace plastics facility was “off the table” as the temperature in the damaged tank lowered. “That is incredible positive news as we turn the corner of this incident,” said division chief Craig Covey.

CAMPAIGN WINDFALL — Congressional candidate Saikat Chakrabarti has poured nearly $10 million of his wealth into the campaign to succeed Nancy Pelosi. As Mission Local reports, Chakrabarti, who made his riches as a founding engineer at Stripe, is locked in a fierce battle with Supervisor Connie Chan for the No. 2 spot to advance from the primary. They are competing for a chance to face front-runner Scott Wiener, a state senator in the general election.

Chakrabarti’s self-funding has already made his bid the most expensive candidate campaign in San Francisco history, exceeding the $9 million that Mayor Daniel Lurie, an heir to the billion-dollar Levi Strauss fortune, poured into his 2024 campaign.

Chakrabarti has flooded the city with TV ads and an army of paid canvassers as he seeks to convince Democrats that he’s the most progressive, non-establishment candidate in the race. He also recently picked up the endorsements of Reps. Ilhan Omar and Rashida Tlaib, though his former boss and fellow “Squad” member Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez has declined to endorse.
