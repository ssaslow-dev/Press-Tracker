Toxics off the table rally: We won't let Big Food do to our democracy what it has done to our diet

Ken gives opening remarks at rally against industry-backed bill

Ken Cook

Sep 17, 2026

On September 15, EWG hosted the “Toxics off the table!” rally in Washington, D.C., to call on lawmakers to oppose the FRESH Act- an industry-backed bill that would make it easier for companies to add new chemicals to our food with less government oversight and erase hundreds of state food safety laws that protect families from toxic ingredients.

Sign our petition here: https://act.ewg.org/7vTW0NMIQkyucQ6wVRE8qg2

Below are Ken’s opening remarks at the rally:

Hello everyone. Thank you for coming.

I’m Ken Cook with Environmental Working Group. Welcome to the Toxics off the table Rally.

So many amazing food movement leaders here today—here from Congress, state legislatures, nonprofits, the academy, the private sector.

Those of you who know me know I am not one to court controversy. But I want to start by acknowledging the food movement leader who by all rights should be here today. A leader who has done more than anyone in Washington to set us on course to fight chronic disease and obesity by insisting that food is medicine, especially for our kids. A leader who has championed the idea that it is a central duty of government to ensure that our kids get fresh food. Clean food. Real food.

That leader could not be here in person today, but we know you are here in spirit…Michelle Obama.

We are here today united in the cause of stopping something very wrong from happening up on that Hill behind us. We are going to stop Big Food’s FRESH Act from ever becoming the law of the land. And today, an amazing cross section of food movement leaders will tell you in shocking detail exactly why it is essential that we stand our ground.

But first, let me put a positive spin on this rally. Because we wouldn’t be here to stop something wrong if we hadn’t been doing somethings right with our food system to make America healthy. This was well before anyone said we needed to make America healthy again.

If dozens of state legislatures of every color—red, blue and purple--are following the lead California established in 2023—banning or labeling sketchy food ingredients and additives, taking on Big Food’s biggest profit center—the scourge of ultra-processed food, we must be doing something right.

State government has long been seen as the laboratory of democracy. Lately it’s been the kitchen.

It all started with a dad who happened to be a member of the California Assembly, who insisted that his kids, and all kids, deserved clean healthy food. That dad has authored four landmark food reform bills—all with bipartisan support, 3 of them enacted into law and a fourth on his governors desk—all of which would be wiped out, and be impossible to pass if the FRESH Act becomes law. That dad stands here to fight alongside us today.

California Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel.

Think about it. We have California governor Gavin Newsom and Texas Governor Greg Abbot signing laws to clean up the food in their states.

Jesse, we must be doing something right.

If Democratic governor Katie Hobbs in Arizona and Republican Governor Patrick Morrisey of West Virginia are signing food reform bills, we must be doing something right.

Food reform bills are moving in state after state—we map them all on the EWG web site and it’s hard to keep up. There’s an important food reform bill sitting on New York Governor Kathy Hochul’s desk right now.

We must be doing something right. It feels good, doesn’t it? To break Big Food’s paralyzing grip here in Washington by cooking up some good food policy in the states.

Are we going to do a lot more? You’re damn right we are. We are not tired of winning. Not tired here in America—not tired around the world. Because this is a global movement.

And that movement must be doing something right when Big Food brings over 290 lawsuits against governments around the world to block or repeal food ingredient, safety or labeling laws.

And here’s another sure sign we must be doing something right: Big Food has propped up a front group to pass a federal law—the FRESH Act—that will preempt all of these food reform laws, and make further reforms impossible. This is sweeping preemption—field preemption, legal scholars call it—that will bind the hands of every duly elected state legislature on virtually any policy related to food.

That’s right. The food companies that that compromised and degraded our diets for profit want to do the very same thing to our democracy—for profit.

There’s no hiding your anti-democratic ambitions behind your front group, Americans for Ingredient Transparency. We’re calling food companies and their brands by name. We’re talking to you PepsiCo, to you, Coca-Cola. And to Nestle, General Mills, Kraft Heinz, Tyson Foods, Conagra, Mars, Cargill—and all the rest.

Your front group doesn’t have customers. But you do. Your front group doesn’t worry about brand loyalty. You live and die by it.

Which is why, today, EWG is launching a campaign to get food companies to withdraw financial support, and disavow the purpose of, Americans for Ingredient Transparency, and its multi-million-dollar influence peddling assault on our diet and our democracy.

Americans Who Eat—not a front group!—you can join our campaign by going to this web page and signing our petition:

www.ewg.org/bigfood

It’s too late for food companies and your front group to save the FRESH Act. Your own customers, and the Toxics Off The Table coalition, are going to kill it.

But maybe it’s not too late for food companies to salvage a shred of your reputation.

We think your customers will want to know what you’re up to, hiding behind that front group.

We think they will join our campaign, and demand that you pull you the hell out of it.
