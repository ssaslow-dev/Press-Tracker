Election 2026 · State of California

California Assembly District 55

Ashley M. Brown and Isaac G. Bryan (incumbent) are the candidates for California Assembly District 55 on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

The State Assembly is one of the two houses of the California Legislature, which holds the state's lawmaking power. The Assembly has 80 members. Its members write and vote on state laws and, with the Senate, pass the state budget, which the Constitution requires by June 15 each year.

What it means for the City of LA

About 84% of the district's voting precincts and 75% of its land are in the City of Los Angeles, from Mar Vista, Del Rey and Palms to Mid City, West Adams and the Crenshaw area. It also includes Culver City, View Park-Windsor Hills and Ladera Heights. State laws bind the City, and the state budget funds programs the City runs. Example: state law requires the City to plan housing for all income levels and update that plan on a set schedule (Government Code sections 65580 and 65588).

Isaac G. Bryan Incumbent

Bryan was the founding director of the UCLA Black Policy Project and director of public policy for UCLA's Ralph J. Bunche Center, and worked for the City of Los Angeles, where he wrote the City's first report on the needs of formerly incarcerated residents (Assembly biography). Born to a teenage mother, he was raised in a large foster and adoptive family (The Voice of Black Los Angeles). The job

Powers. The California Constitution gives the state’s lawmaking power to the Legislature, which is the Senate plus the 80-member Assembly (art. IV, sec. 1). A bill needs a majority of each house to pass (art. IV, sec. 8). The Legislature can override a Governor’s veto with two-thirds of each house (art. IV, sec. 10). Tax increases need two-thirds of each house (art. XIII A, sec. 3). The Legislature can put constitutional amendments on the ballot by a two-thirds vote of each house (art. XVIII, sec. 1). Five of the 14 statewide measures on this ballot (Props 1, 2, 4, 5 and 43) were placed there by the Legislature (Secretary of State voter guide).

Budget. The Legislature must pass the budget bill by midnight on June 15, and a majority vote is enough (art. IV, sec. 12). CalMatters reported that the 2026-27 budget deal totaled $352 billion. It added taxes, including a sales tax on some computer software, and gave counties $900 million for homelessness (CalMatters).

The district.

  • About 467,000 people live in the 55th District. The Census Bureau’s 2020-2024 survey estimates the population as 34% Latino, 29% white, 20% Black and 10% Asian, with a median household income of $93,558 (Census Reporter).
  • The Daily News lists Crenshaw, Culver City, Ladera Heights, Mar Vista, Del Rey, Palms, Pico-Robertson, Beverly Grove and Mid-Wilshire (Daily News). LAist gives voter registration as 63.1% Democratic, 20.8% no party preference and 9.7% Republican (LAist).
  • Of the district’s 1,702 LA County voting precincts, 1,425 are in the City of LA, 167 in Culver City, and 110 in unincorporated View Park and Ladera Heights (County Registrar precinct map).

Records

Isaac G. Bryan

Office and roles. Bryan chairs the Natural Resources Committee and the Select Committee on the Status of Boys and Men of Color. He sits on Governmental Organization, Judiciary, Privacy and Consumer Protection, and the Joint Legislative Committee on Climate Change Policies, and is vice chair of the Legislative Black Caucus (Assembly biography). He was Majority Leader under Speaker Robert Rivas until November 2023, when Rivas replaced him with Cecilia Aguiar-Curry and made him chair of Natural Resources (LA Times).

Bills, 2025-26 session. The Legislature’s bill data lists Bryan as a lead author of the final version of 41 bills, alone or jointly; resolutions are left out. He co-led six of them with another lead author: AB 562 (Solache), AB 1645 (Mark González), AB 1900 (Kalra), AB 2157 (Connolly), AB 2204 (Gabriel) and SB 352 (Sen. Eloise Gómez Reyes). The count leaves out AB 40 and AB 1138: he was lead author of earlier versions, but the final versions list Assemblymembers Mia Bonta and Rick Chavez Zbur. As of the Oct 1, 2026 data file, after the Governor’s Sept 30 deadline to act on bills (art. IV, sec. 10), 28 had become law, 4 were vetoed and 9 did not pass (bill data; Governor’s Sept 30 update).

  • Laws include AB 246 (the Social Security Tenant Protection Act of 2025), AB 247 (wages for incarcerated fire crew members), AB 1388 (law enforcement settlement agreements), AB 1455 (emergency rules for ember-resistant zones), AB 1768, which lets Los Angeles and Contra Costa counties seek local sales taxes above the state cap, and AB 2157 (extending the Displaced Oil and Gas Worker Pilot Program; led by Assemblymember Damon Connolly, with Bryan as joint lead author).
  • Vetoed: AB 7, which would have allowed college admissions preference for descendants of people enslaved in the United States. On Sept 27 the Governor also vetoed AB 1099, on intake for developmental services, and AB 1647, on transferring juveniles to adult criminal court (Governor’s Sept 27 update).
  • Signed Sept 27 to 29: AB 1661, on low-production oil wells and health payments near the Baldwin Hills (LA Focus), and AB 1457, on environmental justice in general plans, both signed Sept 27 (Governor’s office); and AB 1349, which targets “ghost tickets” sold by resellers, signed Sept 27. Newsom asked Bryan to revisit its exemptions for some sellers (LA Times). Also signed Sept 27: AB 1573 on housing element target populations, AB 2162 on county housing funds for nonminor dependents and young adults, and AB 1646 on visits at juvenile facilities, with Asm. Mark González as joint lead author (Governor’s Sept 27 update). AB 1655, on CalWORKs aid during a parent’s immigration detention, was signed Sept 29 (Governor’s office).
  • Vetoed Sept 30, 2026: AB 1537, barring California peace officers from side jobs in immigration enforcement (see Key moments) (veto message).
  • Signed Sept 30, 2026: AB 2599, requiring corporate disclosure of ties to slavery (LA Sentinel; Alliance for Reparations, Reconciliation, and Truth, via IBW21); AB 1883 on workplace surveillance tools, with a signing message (Governor’s office); and two he co-led, AB 1645 (the HUGS Act on prison visits, led by Asm. Mark González) and AB 2204 (organized sports in prisons, led by Asm. Jesse Gabriel) (Governor’s Sept 30 update).

Bills, 2023-24 session. He was a lead author of the final version of 51 bills, alone or jointly; resolutions are left out. Of those, 26 became law, 6 were vetoed and 19 did not pass (bill data). His laws include AB 2716 on low-production oil wells in the Baldwin Hills and AB 1986 on banned books in state prisons. He was a joint lead author of AB 1, collective bargaining for legislative staff (led by Asm. Tina McKinnor); AB 3089, a formal state apology for slavery (led by Asm. Reggie Jones-Sawyer); and AB 2602 on contracts for digital replicas of performers (led by Asm. Ash Kalra).
