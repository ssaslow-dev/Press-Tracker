$2.5 Billion Relief Bill to Accelerate Los Angeles Firestorm Recovery

By Los Angeles Issue on 26 January 2025

In order to fast-track recovery efforts for firestorm-devastated Los Angeles, Governor Gavin Newsom signed bipartisan legislation this week allocating over $2.5 billion in disaster relief. Standing alongside community leaders in Altadena, an area heavily affected by the fires, Newsom emphasized the state’s commitment to providing swift and meaningful support to impacted residents and businesses.

The relief package, a product of the legislature’s special session, includes funding to strengthen emergency response efforts, expedite rebuilding, and restore fire-damaged schools. Introduced by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel and Senator Scott Wiener, the legislation reflects a united effort by California leaders to address the devastation. Newsom highlighted the urgent need for action, contrasting California’s proactive measures with what he described as partisan delays at the federal level.

“California is standing shoulder to shoulder with Los Angeles,” Newsom said. “This $2.5 billion isn’t just relief—it’s a lifeline to help rebuild homes, businesses, and schools without unnecessary red tape. Unlike political games in Washington, this state is delivering results.”

The relief funds will support emergency efforts like sheltering survivors, debris removal, hazard assessments, and traffic control. An additional $4 million will help local governments speed up planning and building approvals, and $1 million will assist in rebuilding school facilities damaged by the fires.

Legislative leaders, including Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas and Senate President Pro Tempore Mike McGuire, praised the bipartisan collaboration. Rivas emphasized the long-term commitment to recovery, while McGuire called the funding a down payment on the state’s promise to stand by Angelenos until recovery is complete.

In addition to the relief package, Governor Newsom has issued a series of executive orders to streamline rebuilding efforts, create temporary housing, and protect firestorm survivors from price gouging and exploitation. Measures include tax relief for individuals and businesses, expedited housing construction, and mortgage assistance. The state is also mobilizing debris removal and implementing measures to prevent mudslides and flooding in fire-damaged areas.

With the relief bill signed into law, Los Angeles is poised to rebuild faster and stronger, ensuring that communities recover with the full backing of the state government. Newsom reaffirmed his administration’s commitment, stating, “This is just the beginning. We’ll be here every step of the way.”
