California budget still not finalized as deadline approaches

SACRAMENTO, Calif. —

California leaders are on the clock to finalize the state's spending plan as the deadline approaches.

Lawmakers have one major hurdle to overcome when deciding on the budget— the state's $12 billion budget shortfall.

State leaders continued their negotiations at the Capitol on Monday, but remained tight-lipped on the discussions behind closed doors.

One thing that has been a sticking point for some is the program that provides health care for undocumented residents in California.

Another issue sparking a back-and-forth is whether to reform the California Environmental Quality Act to make it easier to build new housing in more populated parts of the state.

Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) said the federal government has also been providing challenges for state lawmakers.

"It's been a challenging budget environment for the state, particularly with everything going on in Washington, D.C., the threats that we see from the federal government. But we are working hard with our partners in the Senate and the administration, trying to work through the multitude of issues and, hoping to get to the finish line pretty soon," Gabriel said. "Our approach of compassion and fiscal responsibility is really what's driving our approach here."

KCRA 3 Political Correspondent Ashley Zavala said she has heard different possibilities about when the budget could be up for a vote—ranging from as early as Tuesday morning to next Monday, the day before the budget needs to go into effect.

Gabby Williams scores 26 points, sends Valkyries to 68-64 Game 1 win in defensive WNBA Finals opener

SAN FRANCISCO —

Gabby Williams converted two free throws with 1:18 left to put Golden State ahead, two more with 4.1 seconds to go and finished with 26 points facing constant pressure from Atlanta’s stingy defense, and the Valkyries held off the Dream 68-64 in Game 1 of the WNBA Finals on Saturday.

Williams scored 16 of her points in the second quarter and no other Valkyries reached double figures as they shot 32.1% and 7 of 33 from 3-point range.

“We always knew we were going to find a way somehow,” Williams said on the microphone postgame as fans chanted “Gabby! Gabby!”

Rhyne Howard missed a 3-point try with 1:09 left but Angel Reese made a steal moments later. Naz Hillmon missed from deep. Kiah Stokes missed the first of two free throws with 23.8 seconds left. Then Allisha Gray missed a 3 with 9 seconds left.

Williams hit the free throws to seal the victory.

Game 2 in the best-of-seven series is Monday night back at Chase Center, where the ear-piercing noise from a raucous “Ballhalla” crowd made for the exact hostile environment Valkyries coach Natalie Nakase requested from the home fans.
