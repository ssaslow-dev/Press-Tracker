Meet the crypto billionaire hedging his bets for governor

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: MATCHUP MAKER — Crypto billionaire and San Francisco megadonor Chris Larsen is spending heavily to back both a Democrat and Republican in the crowded race for governor.

Larsen told Playbook in an interview that he’s making a maximum $39,200 contribution to Republican Steve Hilton, a former Fox News host and author — matching his prior contribution to Democrat and former Rep. Katie Porter.

The Ripple co-founder, who’s worth an estimated $10.2 billion, said he wants Hilton and Porter to advance to the November election because they are both — despite their partisan disagreements — focused on “kitchen table issues” like California’s crushing affordability crisis.

Playbook spoke with Larsen about his unusual move, as well as his support for Porter despite her recent headwinds. Highlights from the interview, which has been condensed for clarity and space:

Why are you supporting both Hilton and Porter in this race?

I’m 100 percent behind Katie Porter, but I’ve met with Steve a couple of times, and I’d say they share something similar. They’re focused on really basic stuff. California’s problems are some pretty basic things around costs and getting rid of some of the overburdensome [rules] regulations that are holding us back on multiple fronts. Both of them come at that ‘back to basics, kitchen table’ thing. I know the affordability [mantra] is so popular everywhere. But I think both of them are coming at it genuinely, and have been for some time. It’s weird, they actually get along. I guess they’ve connected with each other. They both have the right temperaments for this divisive world we live in, and that’s also why I think it’d be good to have a Republican and a Dem in the final two — rather than having two Dems or two Republicans, which theoretically could happen.

Why would you prefer to have a Democrat and Republican in the runoff?

Things are just way, way too vilified, divided. We’ve got to start having conversations with the other side. So, having two Dems in there, you would lose that conversation, right? I don’t think that’s good for this state. We still have a lot of Republicans in the state. I know they’re in the minority, but they’re not represented at all. Generally, a one party supermajority is not really the best thing to have. You’re missing out on a lot of key debates that millions of people want to have. So I think it’s healthier to have one person from each party.

You’ve given heavily to Democrats in the past. What would you say to allies who might be surprised that you’re giving to a Republican and former Fox News host?

Well, I think we have to get away from those labels. You have to meet him. When you meet him, it’s like, he’s a reasonable guy and he’s focused on the right things and seems respectful. Everybody has to be judged by who they are. We got into problems with this kind of labeling people. The problems you have in this country, in this state, they’re coming from both extremes — the far left that’s done some wacky stuff and the far right that’s done some wacky stuff. There’s a huge range between those two points.

What if Porter and Hilton both advance from the June top-two primary — who would you support in November?

Well, I’m behind Katie Porter and have been for a long time. I still hope that she wins. But that’s a good matchup, and that brings out the best in her. And it brings out the best on the other side, too, with those two as the key voices in this contest. It’s a good thing for the debate.

What are those “kitchen table” issues they’re both talking about that you think need to be front of mind in the race?

It’s the big-cost things that are really holding people back: housing, obviously; education, that’s too expensive; energy costs. And then there’s just way over — and I’m not even going to say overregulation because regulation is important — it’s just burden, like this crazy number of burdensome [rules] that have obviously been put in there by special interests who control Sacramento. We’ve got to start hacking through that jungle because that’s a big part of what’s really hurting us.

Are you surprised that Porter doesn’t have more momentum? She’s been a front-runner, at least among Democrats, but her polling seems to have plateaued since her viral videos controversy.

Oh, not at all. I just did an event for her with a bunch of business folks and other leaders in San Francisco. I have to say, she just hit it out of the park, and that’s not necessarily an easy group to win over. You had people coming in curious, going out convinced. Also, the viral video thing, the fact that that’s an issue is the stupidest thing I’ve ever heard. I mean, she’s tough. She comes across as tough. And, you know what? Right on. We need somebody who’s tough to take on the folks that are driving Sacramento. She’s not a Sacramento person, she was in Congress. So she comes in not with a lot of IOUs, so to speak.

You emphasized your take on the viral videos controversy. Can you talk more about why you think that’s unimportant in the scope of the race?

It’s beyond unimportant. It actually is proof of how tough she is. Frankly, I don’t see it as an issue at all. Because she used the F word? Okay, fine. To handle this state, you know how complex it is, you’re gonna have to have some thick skin. And that’s what I take away.

GOVERNOR'S RACE

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: MIDDLE MARCH — Rep. Eric Swalwell jumps out to a lead over longtime front-runner Katie Porter in a new governor’s race poll commissioned by California Issues Forum, a nonprofit tied to the moderate Democratic caucus that puts on trips and forums connecting lawmakers and interest group representatives.

The FM3 poll of likely voters finds Republicans Chad Bianco and Steve Hilton in an effective three-way tie with Swalwell for first place in the high teens, hinting at Democrats’ nightmare lockout scenario, followed by Porter at 13 percent, and then everyone else in single digits. Democratic voters prefer Swalwell (30 percent) to Porter (21 percent). But there’s still plenty of variability: One-fifth of voters remain undecided, and the poll doesn’t ask about Attorney General Rob Bonta, a heavyweight poised to leap in.

The survey also approximates what voters will actually see on the ballot by using likely designations, which means candidates’ current gigs rather than old ones — so ex-Representative Porter as a “law professor/community organizer” and former HHS Secretary Xavier Becerra as an “attorney,” for instance. — Jeremy B. CAMPAIGN YEAR(S)

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: INSURE THING — State Sen. Ben Allen is rolling out endorsements from Reps. Julia Brownley, George Whitesides, Laura Friedman and Nanette Barragán, bringing his total congressional endorsements in the insurance commissioner’s race to 10.

He’s also ruling out switching to the attorney general’s race if Bonta runs for governor, according to a spokesperson for his campaign, waving away rumors that he might make a change. Two potential candidates to replace Bonta, Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel and state Sen. Tom Umberg, are among 21 of Allen’s colleagues in the state Legislature who are newly endorsing him. He has now locked up support from 40 state legislators, including the leaders of both chambers and former Senate President pro Tem Mike McGuire.

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: TRADES PRAISE — The State Building & Construction Trades Council of California has endorsed former state Senate Education Committee Chair Josh Newman in the state superintendent’s race. Trades President Chris Hannan praised Newman’s resume on career training in offering the endorsement, which passed over labor-aligned Democrats including Al Muratsuchi, Nichelle Henderson and Anthony Rendon.

“Josh shares our belief that students should be informed about the full range of opportunities available to them after graduation — from traditional college pathways to state-approved apprenticeship programs that lead to high-quality union construction careers,” Hannan said in a statement.

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: COURIC WADES IN — Brian Goldsmith, a Democrat running for the state Senate, has won the endorsement of journalist Katie Couric, as well as the support of two state lawmakers, Gabriel and state Sen. Henry Stern.

Goldsmith, a media consultant and former journalist, previously worked with Couric and produced her infamous interview with Sarah Palin during the 2008 presidential campaign. Couric, who lives in New York, offered this recommendation: “I’ve met a lot of smart people during the course of my career but, without question, Brian Goldsmith is one of the smartest.”

He is running in a crowded field of candidates vying for the open Senate seat in District 24 (Hollywood Hills, Santa Monica, Malibu). The district’s current senator, Allen, completes his final term next year.

ON THE AIRWAVES

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: CHASING LAWYERS, NOT LAWSUITS — A PAC pushing to reform tort law in California will today unveil a new TV ad targeting personal-injury attorneys. The spot comes as rival ballot initiatives sponsored by Uber and the Consumer Attorneys of California are proposed for the November 2026 ballot.

“Californians pay for ambulance lawyer schemes,” the ad states. “Lawsuit abuse makes greedy ambulance lawyers rich. We need reforms to end lawsuit abuse.”

The new ad, part of a six-figure buy to air during major sporting events like NBA and NFL games, was paid for by Protecting American Consumers Together, an advocacy group whose major donors include Uber and Waffle House Inc.
