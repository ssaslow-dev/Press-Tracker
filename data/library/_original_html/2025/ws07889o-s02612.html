Scott Wiener in the hot seat

THE BUZZ: AGAINST THE GRAIN — State Sen. Scott Wiener isn’t one to shirk away from a fierce policy fight in Sacramento — and he isn’t sorry about it.

From his efforts to slash regulations on housing construction, impose safety guardrails on artificial intelligence or protect the rights of transgender people, Wiener is — per usual — taking on an abundance of political hot potatoes this legislative session.

But the San Francisco Democrat didn’t sound worried about irking his fellow lawmakers along the way. If anything, he seems to relish the slog.

“We’re representing our constituents, we’re representing our beliefs, and sometimes that can create some tension,” Wiener told Playbook. “We’re all grown ups, and you just don’t take that kind of stuff personally.”

Wiener, who chairs the powerful Senate Budget Committee, visited POLITICO’s Sacramento bureau on Monday for a wide-ranging team interview. Five top highlights:

1) On threats to Medi-Cal funding: Wiener stressed the uncertainty of federal funding for Medicaid when asked about rising costs in the state’s program for low-income residents, Medi-Cal. “Trump and the Republicans in Congress are trying to take a wrecking ball to Medicaid, in addition to Medicare and Social Security and SNAP… we don’t know what’s going to happen,” he said.

Amid the federal uncertainty, Wiener said his team is digging into Medi-Cal cost increases that have caused a multibillion-dollar shortfall. He said it was “premature” to comment on how the state might respond, or whether cuts to coverage for undocumented residents were on the table.

But he hinted why he feels strongly about defending that coverage: “You will just flood ERs with non-emergency issues. People will be seeking their primary care in ERs, which is not sustainable for anyone.”

2) On overhauling CEQA: One of Wiener’s most controversial bills this year would overhaul the landmark California Environmental Quality Act, which is often blamed for delaying or blocking the construction of new housing. His proposal, SB 607, would exempt many types of urban infill housing projects from CEQA review.

Wiener conceded the bill will be a heavy lift amid opposition from some labor unions and environmentalists. “There are still strong institutional forces that want to keep CEQA exactly as it is, because some of them have a vested economic interest,” he said.

He added, “Don’t get me wrong: It’s going to be hard. But I do think that we can do it. And yeah, it’s possible we’ll all fall on our face. There’s no doubt about it, but we’re going to work hard.”

3) On Newsom and transgender athletes: Wiener rebuked Gov. Gavin Newsom’s recent podcast remarks in which he broke with Democrats and said he opposes transgender athletes competing in female college and youth sports. “That sort of comment was deeply troubling to me and to many other people,” he said.

But Wiener was also careful to telegraph that he still considers Newsom an ally on many issues with respect to the LGBTQ+ community. He said he expects the governor will still work with lawmakers on legislation to protect the community in areas beyond sports.

“The governor also has a long track record supporting LGBTQ people,” Wiener said. “He has signed some very, very politically challenging bills. I want to make sure we never forget that.”

4) On waiting for Pelosi’s decision: Wiener has been candid about his plans to run for San Francisco’s congressional seat whenever Nancy Pelosi retires. But the former speaker has stuck around longer than many city insiders expected. Wiener insisted he’s not sweating what could happen.

“I’m not waiting with bated breath every day. I think we’re lucky to have her,” he said. “I have a lot on my plate right now, and that is my focus.”

If Pelosi doesn’t retire before the 2026 midterms, is there any scenario where Wiener would consider challenging her? His answer was definitive. “I’m not running against Nancy Pelosi,” Wiener said.

5) On the 2026 race for governor: He declined to say whom he’d like to succeed Newsom after his final term ends next year. The senator also wouldn’t say if he would support former Vice President Kamala Harris, his longtime friend, who is seriously eyeing the 2026 race.

“I love Kamala dearly, and I’m not going to comment,” Wiener said.

SAN FRANCISCO

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: ALL ABOARD — San Francisco Mayor Daniel Lurie will launch a ballot measure committee today with one clear objective: Save Muni from its impending fiscal cliff, which could lead to steep cuts in bus and subway service.

Lurie’s committee is a fundraising vehicle as he works to shape a potential 2026 ballot measure that would ask voters to provide the transit agency with a financial lifeline. Like many transit providers in the state, Muni’s budget is still in disarray after a pandemic-era drop in ridership exacerbated by a surge in remote work, especially in the tech sector.

The mayor has emphasized the crucial role transit will play in downtown San Francisco’s economic recovery, as he works to lure workers, tourists and others back to the urban core. At the same time, Lurie also stressed that the city needs to do more to make Muni feel safe and clean to riders.

“The pandemic and challenges downtown hit Muni particularly hard, but the funding crisis we face today had been anticipated for years and necessary changes were not made,” Lurie told Playbook in a statement. “That ends now.”

Many details of the Muni funding ballot measure are still unclear, but Lurie signaled that it will likely have guardrails to help appeal to voters skeptical about paying more to support the agency. “San Franciscans will not be asked to sign a blank check,” he added, vowing to “bring a new era of accountability and reliability to Muni.”

CAMPAIGN YEAR(S)

DISASSEMBLING — Democratic Assemblymember Esmeralda Soria announced she is running to replace term-limited Anna Caballero in the state Senate next year.

Soria’s announcement tees up a tough Dem-on-Dem contest with Fresno City Councilmember Nelson Esparza, who is also running to succeed Caballero. Soria is endorsed by Caballero, but Reps. Jim Costa and Adam Gray are backing Esparza.

Former Merced Mayor Mike Murphy, a Republican, has filed a statement of intention to run for Soria’s Assembly district — which Trump flipped in 2024. The Assembly seat was already a top target for Republicans due to the red shift and rumors that Soria would run for the upper house.

TARGETS CONFIRMED — The National Republican Campaign Committee named its targets for the 2026 midterms, and they include five battleground Californians: Reps. Josh Harder, Dave Min, George Whitesides, Derek Tran and Gray.

The list includes the three seats Democrats recently flipped in California (Gray, Whitesides and Tran) and two other Democratic victors who won by fewer than four points.

“House Republicans are in the majority and on offense,” NRCC Chair Richard Hudson (R-N.C.) said in a statement. “Meanwhile, vulnerable House Democrats have been hard at work demonstrating they are painfully out of touch with hardworking Americans.”

STATE CAPITOL

KARDASHIAN-APPROVED — Assemblymember Isaac Bryan’s bill to increase pay for incarcerated firefighters got a celebrity boost on Monday night, when Kim Kardashian shared an endorsement via her Instagram story.

“The world watched California’s incarcerated firefighters working 24 hour shifts and saving homes, lives and communities during the Los Angeles fires, the Park Fire in Northern California and hundreds of other fires this season,” she said in her social media post.

Kardashian noted that she and her half-sister, Kendall Jenner, had visited a fire camp to learn more about the issue.

“Now our California legislature gets to decide how to honor their lifesaving efforts with a fair wage that appreciates the danger and importance of their work,” she added. “This issue has enormous bipartisan support in the legislature.”

Kardashian went on to thank Bryan, Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel, Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas and Senate President Pro Tem Mike McGuire “for your leadership on this.”

The reality star — who has previously used her enormous platform to lobby on criminal justice issues — expressed support for incarcerated firefighter pay raises earlier in the year.

The Assembly Public Safety Committee advanced Bryan’s legislation last week, sending it to the Appropriations Committee. Even with broad support, the assemblymember will likely need all the help he can get to secure the funding needed to enact it.

Bryan has said the proposal is likely to cost tens of millions of dollars — a heavy lift in a challenging budget year. — Lindsey Holden

MAKE AMERICA GAY AGAIN — Badlands, the Sacramento gay night club, announced in an Instagram story that it has banned “MAGA-related attire” after a guest recently wore such garb in the venue leading “to discomfort among some patrons.” Playbook can only assume this was a response to a state GOP convention attendee who sported a Make America Great Again hat in the club Saturday night after Republican party festivities wrapped up at the nearby Hyatt Regency.
