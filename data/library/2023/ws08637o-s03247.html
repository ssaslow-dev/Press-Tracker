California Gov. Gavin Newsom announced today that he will visit Israel on Friday.

“I’m on my way to Israel,” Newsom shared earlier in a message posted on X, the social media site formerly known as Twitter. “I’ll be meeting with those impacted by the horrific terrorist attacks and offering California’s support.”

The Hamas terror attacks on Oct. 7 killed 1,200 Israelis and injured nearly 3,000, while over 150 others — including Americans — were taken captive. The attack was the most deadly day for the world’s Jewish population since the Holocaust, and sparked a full-scale war between Israel and Hamas in the Gaza Strip, now at the expense of thousands of human lives caught between the conflict.

According to Al Jazeera, at least 1,400 Israeli people have been killed and 3,800 injured since the initial attack. In Gaza, the death toll reportedly stands at 3,478, while 12,065 others have been wounded. The violence has sparked protests in support of Israel and/or Palestinians around the globe, and Los Angeles is no exception.

Today, several dozen protesters gathered outside Vice President Kamala Harris’ Brentwood home, calling for a cease fire in the war between Israel and Hamas. Vice President Harris, however, was not there.

“We are outside the house of Kamala Harris and Douglas Emhoff asking them to join us in demanding a cease-fire,” organizer David Shapiro told the Los Angeles Times. “We are American Jews with ancestors around the world. We honor our tradition rooted in the belief that human life is sacred. To achieve genuine safety for ourselves as Jews, for Israelis and for Palestinians, we need to work together toward equality and justice for all and an end to apartheid.”

The emotional division resulting from the conflict led Gov. Newsom on Wednesday to authorize the immediate expansion of funds to bolster safety and security at religious institutions in California amid the ongoing conflict in the Middle East.

“No matter how and where one prays, every Californian deserves to be safe,” he said.

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel and Senator Scott Wiener, two fellow California Democrats and Co-Chairs of the California Legislative Jewish Caucus, expressed their support and gratitude for the safety measure. “These critical security resources will make a huge difference and send a powerful message when so many in our community are feeling on edge,” they said. “We are deeply grateful to Governor Newsom for his longstanding solidarity with the Jewish community and for standing with us in word and in deed during these challenging times.”

Newsom is touching down in the Jewish state on his way to China. Starting Monday, he will travel throughout the country to meet key figures to advance climate action, promote economic development and tourism, and encourage cultural exchange between countries to actively combat xenophobia.

The California governor is standing in solidarity with the rest of his political party in support of Israel, including President Joe Biden, who just wrapped his own quick visit, which resulted in Israeli officials allowing humanitarian aid to flow into Gaza.
