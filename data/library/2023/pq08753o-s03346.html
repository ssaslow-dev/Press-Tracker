CALIFORNIA LEGISLATURE PASSES BAN ON FOUR CHEMICALS IN PROCESSED FOODS

By Curt Barry

Posted September 13, 2023 California lawmakers have passed an apparent first-in-the-nation bill that would ban four toxic chemicals from processed foods sold in the state beginning in 2027, with backers claiming the move will in part help to prevent cancer, reproductive, and behavioral health problems in children.

However, they also narrowed the bill from an earlier version, removing titanium dioxide from the list of chemicals to be banned, in order to lessen industry opposition and ensure the bill cleared the state Senate -- after a July amendment that delayed the ban from 2025 to 2027.

"Today's bipartisan vote marks a huge step forward in our effort to protect children and families in California from dangerous and toxic chemicals in our food supply," claimed Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), author of the bill, AB 418, in a Sept. 12 press release.

"It's unacceptable that the U.S. is so far behind the rest of the world when it comes to food safety," he added. "This bill will not ban any foods or products -- it simply will require food companies to make minor modifications to their recipes and switch to the safer alternative ingredients that they already use in Europe and so many other places around the globe." Gabriel's bill cleared the state Senate Sept. 11 on a 33-3 vote, and passed the Assembly Sept. 12 on a 65-6 concurrence vote to approve amendments that had been made in the upper chamber. Sept. 14 is the last day of the 2023 legislative session, and Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) then has until Oct. 14 to sign or veto bills.

AB 418 prohibits the manufacture, sale or distribution in California of any food product containing Red Dye No. 3, potassium bromate, brominated vegetable oil, or propyl paraben, all with an effective date of 2027, according to Gabriel's release. "The use of these chemicals has already been banned in the 27 nations in the European Union (EU) as well as many other countries due to scientific research linking them to significant health harms, including cancer, reproductive issues, and behavioral and developmental issues in children," the lawmaker said.

Major industry and business groups strongly opposed AB 418 until it was amended Sept. 1 to remove titanium dioxide from its list of banned substances. And while the California Chamber of Commerce, Dairy Institute of California, and American Chemistry Council declared neutrality on the narrowed bill, other groups remained opposed.

EU Model In particular, critics have pointed out that the EU is currently reviewing its ban of titanium dioxide in foods in part because it is allowed and used extensively in pharmaceuticals even though the health risk exposure level is the same as when it is an ingredient in food.

California "would have inevitably faced the exact issue here if we adopted the EU policy around titanium dioxide -- how can California reconcile banning it in food but still allow usage in all pharmaceuticals -- they share the same route of exposure so these policies would be irreconcilable -- which is what the EU is now facing," says one industry source. In the broader context, "I think the precedent of circumventing state and federal food safety regimes is terrible precedent," the source adds. "The California Legislature is effectively outsourcing its food safety to the EU. My understanding is at least some of these banned chemicals have alternatives -- whether they share all the same properties, have sufficient supply, change taste or otherwise have negative implications I cannot say."

But supporters of AB 418, including co-sponsor Environmental Working Group (EWG), have argued in part that the Food & Drug Administration (FDA) is failing to review and ban the four chemicals included in the final version of the bill as well as many other substances found in foods in the United States.

"AB 418 is the most important food safety bill in more than a decade and its passage would be a historic victory for protecting kids and families from dangerous food chemicals," Scott Faber, EWG's senior vice president for government affairs, said in the Gabriel press release. "For decades, chemical companies have been able to exploit a loophole that allows food additives to escape adequate review and oversight by the FDA. Since the FDA has failed to keep us safe, it has become the responsibility of states like California to step up and lead."

Gabriel's office says "many major brands and manufacturers," including Coke, Pepsi, Gatorade and Panera, "have voluntarily stopped using the additives that would be banned under AB 418 because of concerns about their impact on human health. One of these chemicals -- Red Dye No. 3 -- is already banned by the FDA for use in cosmetics but is somehow still allowed in food." -- Curt Barry (cbarry@iwpnews.com)
