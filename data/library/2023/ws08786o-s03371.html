Recognizing fake news and using social media responsibly is not something that's always taught in schools. It can also be a breeding ground for misinformation.

And what happens to our democracy when fake news enters the hands of students? Well, with a new bill finding its way into K-12 curriculum, we wanted to discuss more about media literacy.

California Assemblymember Marc Berman, D-23, joined Midday Edition to talk about the bill he authored, AB 873, which would require K-12 students to learn about media literacy in their core subjects. It just passed the state legislature last week, and is close to being signed by Gov. Gavin Newsom.

There's also another bill in the pipeline: AB 787, sponsored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-46, that would survey school districts around the state about what their current standards are with media literacy. It would then come up with a model curriculum to put forth.

"A lack of media literacy is troubling because as long as a purveyor of lies can have the wrappings of legitimacy ... people who don't have a real proper training, not only in media literacy but in critical thinking, can fall prey to the kind of lies that are exploited by charlatans."

Brian Levin, professor emeritus at Cal State San Bernadino

CalMatters education reporter Carolyn Jones told KPBS Midday Edition that teaching children media literacy is important because they are "digital natives."

"They grew up on the internet. This is something that most kids today have been familiar with," Jones said. "And so it's very, very important that they learn this information."

Professor Emeritus Brian Levin of Cal State San Bernadino said a lack of media literacy can make it easier for misinformation to create extremism and hate.

"A lack of media literacy is troubling," Levin told Midday Edition. "As long as a purveyor of lies can have the wrappings of legitimacy — they have a microphone, they have a studio, they have so many opportunities with regard to the presentational aspects of news that people who don't have a real proper training, not only in media literacy but in critical thinking, can fall prey to the kind of lies that are exploited by charlatans."
