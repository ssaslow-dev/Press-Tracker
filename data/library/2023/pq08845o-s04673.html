CALIFORNIA LAWMAKERS WEIGH PFAS BAN BILLS AS VOTING DEADLINE NEARS

By Curt Barry

Posted August 24, 2023 With less than a month left in California's legislative session, state lawmakers are poised to consider final votes on bills that would require the phaseout of PFAS in artificial turf, cleaning and menstrual products, as well as other legislation limiting an array of toxic chemicals in cosmetics and food products.

Staff for Assemblywoman Akilah Weber (D-La Mesa) are "currently engaging with stakeholders and there may be one more round of amendments" to California's reportedly first-in-the-nation bill to phase out per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) in household, industrial and institutional cleaning products beginning in 2026 before a Senate vote, according to a legislative source. "But the plan is to get the bill to the governor's desk as soon as possible."

That bill, AB 727, was recently amended to delay the ban until 2028 for floor sealers and finishes, in response to concerns from that sector. It cleared the Senate Appropriations Committee through a consent rule and is awaiting a vote on the Senate floor.

The deadline for bills to pass fiscal panels is Sept. 1, and the last day of the legislative session is Sept. 14. Gov. Gavin Newsom (D) then will have until Oct. 14 to sign or veto bills.

Separately, the Senate Appropriations Committee is expected soon to consider whether to approve AB 1423, which would ban beginning in 2024 the installation of artificial turf containing specified levels of PFAS "at certain public and private institutions, including local schools, and would completely ban the manufacture, distribution, and sale of the same type of turf," according to an analysis by the committee.

AB 1423's prohibition on purchasing or installing covered surfaces containing intentionally added PFAS or PFAS at or above 20 parts per million (ppm) would specifically apply to: any public entity, including local governmental agencies; a public or private school serving pupils in kindergarten through 12th grade; and a public or private institution of higher education, the analysis says.

While the ban on purchases would begin Jan. 1, 2024, it "would not affect any of the above who have concluded the design and permitting of a covered surface, contracted for the installation of a covered surface, or purchased a covered surface on or before December 31, 2023," the analysis notes.

In addition, AB 1423 "would request but not require the University of California to comply with the installation prohibition that would commence January 1, 2024."

The measure would also require a manufacturer or installer of "covered surfaces" that proposes to sell, design or install a field with artificial turf containing intentionally added PFAS or PFAS at or above 20 ppm "to notify the recipient of the turf."

And beginning Jan. 1, 2026, AB 1423 would prohibit "any person or entity from manufacturing, distributing, selling, or offering for sale in the state any 'covered surface' containing intentionally added PFAS or PFAS at or above 20 ppm, effectively banning the product."

The committee analysis continues that manufacturers of "covered surfaces" would have to use the least toxic alternative when replacing PFAS and conduct their alternatives analysis "to determine the least toxic alternative in compliance with the requirements of the Green Chemistry Act."

However, the bill would repeal its prohibitions if the Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) adopts a "regulatory response governing the activity covered by it."

Another PFAS-ban bill is awaiting a vote on the Senate floor. That measure, AB 246 by Assemblywoman Diane Papan (D-San Mateo), would prohibit, beginning Jan. 1, 2025, "a person from manufacturing, distributing, selling, or offering for sale in the state any menstrual products that contain intentionally added" PFAS, or, beginning Jan. 1, 2027, "concentrations of PFAS at or above 10 parts per million (ppm)," according to a Senate floor analysis.

Earlier this year, California lawmakers added specific enforcement and penalty provisions to AB 246 along with the bills for cleaning products and artificial turf, aiming to bolster their effectiveness in the absence of current state actions in those areas.

Non-PFAS Bills The Legislature is also considering final votes on restrictions for chemicals other than PFAS, including AB 496 by Assemblywoman Laura Friedman (D-Burbank). That measure would, beginning Jan. 1, 2027, ban "a person or entity from manufacturing, selling, delivering, holding or offering for sale in commerce any cosmetic product that contains any of the 26 intentionally added ingredients specified in this bill," a Senate floor analysis says.

The chemical ingredients are: lily aldehyde, acetaldehyde, cyclohexylamine, cyclotetrasiloxane, phytonadione, sodium perborate, styrene, trichloroacetic acid, tricresyl phosphate, vinyl acetate, 2-chloracetamide, allyl isothiocyanate, anthraquinone, malachite green, oil from the seeds of Laurus nobilis L, pyrogallol, C.I. disperse blue 1, trisodium nitrilotriacetate, various boron substances, C.I. disperse blue 3, basic green 1, basic blue 7, 3(or 5)-((4(benzylmethylamino)phenyl)azo)-1,2 -(or 1,4)-dimethyl-1H-1,2,4-triazolium and its salts, basic violet 4, basic blue 3, and basic blue 9.

Friedman's original proposal for AB 496 would have banned the chemicals from cosmetics beginning in 2025; however, she amended AB 496 on March 8 to delay bans for about half of the target substances by two years.

The intent of the bill is "to be consistent with the approach of the EU's cosmetic regulation," according to a previous committee analysis. "All of the chemicals listed in AB 496 have been fully banned in the EU Directive and its Annexes and consequently have already been removed from cosmetic products sold in the EU."

The Senate is also poised to vote on AB 418 by Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) and Assemblywoman Buffy Wicks (DOakland), which would ban five toxic chemicals from processed foods sold in the state, with backers claiming the move would in part help to prevent cancer, reproductive, and behavioral health problems in children -- in what they say would mark the first state action nationwide to ban certain chemicals in foods.

AB 418 would, beginning Jan. 1, 2027, bar "a person or entity from manufacturing, selling, delivering, distributing, holding, or offering for sale, in commerce a food product that contains" brominated vegetable oil, potassium bromate, propylparaben, red dye 3 or titanium dioxide. The bill was amended last month to delay the ban date from 2025 to 2027. -Curt Barry (cbarry@iwpnews.com)
