THREE STATES bid heads to ballot -- REPUBLICANS fail to reach DACA deal -- TRUMP’s giant foreign policy bet

By David Siders (dsiders@politico.com) and Carla Marinucci (cmarinucci@politico.com) with Maya Parthasarathy (mparthasarathy@politico.com)

THE BUZZ: We now enter a holding pattern in the wake of Donald Trump’s G7 showing and the summit in North Korea, with the effectiveness of the president's diplomacy far from certain. But in five months, look for outcomes either way to throw yet another wrench into the Trump dynamic in California’s competitive House races.

-- As POLITICO’s Christopher Cadelago and Ben White report, “To President Donald Trump’s camp, his scorched-earth challenge to key U.S. allies over trade and his valedictory denuclearization pact with Korean dictator Kim Jong Un amount to a compelling midterm pitch from the man who promised to shake up the global order. But the president’s big moves of the past few days also represent a giant bet that he can keep his negotiating partners at the table between now and November.

-- … “While Trump’s North Korea peace gambit could spark a diplomatic blowup — or worse — the president has already cracked open an escape hatch, conceding to reporters in Singapore that he may be wrong to trust Kim. ‘I don’t know that I’ll ever admit that, but I’ll find some kind of an excuse,’ Trump said.

-- “His bellicose strategy on trade, however, presents significant economic perils for the president and his party heading into the elections, with fewer opportunities to pass the buck. Economists warn that the biggest risk to an otherwise strong U.S. economy is a Trump-induced global trade war that could see the collapse of the North American Free Trade agreement, a tariff battle on automobiles with the European Union and a series of escalating trade levies with China that could drive up consumer prices and anger voters.” Story -- Democrats, of course, are ready to pounce. On North Korea, Sen. Dianne Feinstein said in a prepared statement that it was “important that the two leaders met and were able to create an opportunity for serious discussion.” But she added, “Given our history with North Korea, I’m concerned that President Trump said he trusts Kim Jong Un’s commitment to denuclearize … With little to show beyond a brief joint statement with few details, President Trump agreed to end military exercises with South Korea … It concerns me that the president is making concessions to North Korea with nothing to show in return.”

DEPARTMENT OF LONGSHOTS: “Radical plan to split California into three states earns spot on November ballot,” by LATimes’ John Myers: “California’s 168-year run as a single entity, hugging the continent’s edge for hundreds of miles and sprawling east across mountains and desert, could come to an end next year — as a controversial plan to split the Golden State into three new jurisdictions qualified Tuesday for the Nov. 6 ballot.

-- “If a majority of voters who cast ballots agree, a long and contentious process would begin for three separate states to take the place of California, with one primarily centered around Los Angeles and the other two divvying up the counties to the north and south. Completion of the radical plan — far from certain, given its many hurdles at judicial, state and federal levels — would make history.” Story NEIGHBOR STATE: Harry Reid still has it in Nevada, scoring significant victories in Tuesday’s election. As POLITICO’s Daniel Strauss reports, “Susie Lee, endorsed by the former Senate Democratic leader in Nevada’s 3rd Congressional District, won her primary, while another Reid acolyte, Steven Horsford, cruised to victory in the Democratic primary in Nevada’s 4th Congressional District.

-- “In Nevada’s contentious Democratic primary for governor, Reid endorsed Clark County Commission Chair Steve Sisolak over Commission Vice Chair Chris Giunchigliani, and Reid was active on Sisolak’s behalf behind the scenes. Not only did Reid work to steer donors away from Giunchigliani, he told her directly that Sisolak would be a stronger general election candidate. On Tuesday, Sisolak beat Giunchigliani by double digits.

ROSENSTEIN V NUNES: “Rosenstein plans to call on House to investigate its own staff,” by CNN’s Laura Jarrett: “Deputy Attorney General Rod Rosenstein's ongoing battle with House Republicans reached new heights Tuesday, as the No. 2 senior leader of the Justice Department plans to call on the House to investigate its own committee staff.

-- Rosenstein has butted heads with House Intelligence Chairman Devin Nunes for months over a subpoena for documents related to the Russia investigation, but the battle spilled out into public view Tuesday after Fox News reported staff on the committee felt ‘personally attacked’ at a meeting with Rosenstein in January. Justice Department officials dispute the recounting of the closed-door meeting detailed in the story, and Rosenstein plans to "request that the House general counsel conduct an internal investigation of these Congressional staffers' conduct" when he returns from a foreign trip this week, DOJ said.” Story HARRIS WATCH: Sen. Cory Booker, Kirsten Gillibrand, Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren -- all potential 2020 presidential candidates.

-- “The Mexican immigrants Trump actually needs” by Pam Ortega for POLITICO: “During his campaign for president, Donald Trump pledged to cut back on immigration from Mexico. But there’s one type of Mexican immigrant his administration is admitting in larger numbers: low-skilled agricultural guest workers.

“While the policy might surprise some of the president’s supporters, it reflects a reality that immigration experts say rarely surfaces in the public debate over immigration: American agriculture is highly dependent on seasonal foreign labor. And as the Trump administration cracks down harder on illegal border crossing from Mexico, the agricultural sector is pressing the federal government to expand guest worker visas so more Mexican workers can enter the country legally.” Story.

-- “A New Approach To Racial Trauma: Black Residents Build Safe Spaces In Wake Of Stephon Clark’s Death” by Capital Public Radio’s Sammy Caiola and Adhiti Bandlamudi: “When police fatally shot unarmed black man Stephon Clark this March, communities erupted in protest. They pleaded for change at city council meetings and screamed for justice on the streets. But in the shooting’s immediate aftermath, and as Clark’s brother suffered from very public mental-health episodes, grieving residents began discussing how mental and emotional support wasn’t widely available in communities of color. … Experts say the shock of a police killing — on top of the racism that black residents experience on a daily basis — can lead to high stress levels and serious mental health struggles.” Story.
