Election results: Gabriel, 19-year-old Clark head to runoff in west San Fernando Valley Assembly race

By Kevin Modesti

Democrat Jesse Gabriel and 19-year-old Republican Justin Clark have advanced to a runoff in a special election to replace Matt Dababneh in a California Assembly seat representing the west San Fernando Valley.

With all precincts reporting Tuesday, Gabriel, an attorney, had 32 percent of the votes among eight candidates, and Clark, a Cal State Northridge political science student who was the only Republican in the race. had 27.1 percent. Democrat Tricia Robbins Kasson was third with 16 percent, Democrat Ankur Patel fourth with 10.1 percent, and independent Dennis Zine fifth with 8.5 percent.

Under special-election rules, any candidate getting more than 50 percent Tuesday would have won outright and soon begun serving the remaining months of a term that ends Dec. 3.

Gabriel and Clark face a runoff June 5, which also is the day of the primary for the new full two-year term. Among Democratic candidates Gabriel, Robbins Kasson, Patel, Ray Bishop, Jeff Bornstein and Daniel Brin, it was Gabriel and Robbins Kasson who got the most attention.

Gabriel, a constitutional rights attorney whose campaign was endorsed by the Democratic Party of the San Fernando Valley, emphasized his role in filing lawsuit against the Trump administration on behalf of young immigrant “Dreamers.” Robbins Kasson, an aide to L.A. City Councilman Bob Blumenfield, focused on local issues and telling voters about her role in efforts to revitalize business corridors in Canoga Park and Reseda.

But the race was the most prone to produce intrigue among the day’s three special elections in state Assembly districts in Los Angeles County. That’s partly because a large field of candidates were battling for the relatively few voters who participate in a stand-alone special election. And partly because it was hard to tell how voters would react to Zine and Clark.

Clark acknowledged his political inexperience but noted wryly that his youth ensures that he is “not touched by corruption.”

Zine, a well-known former L.A. City Council member with a conservative bent, remained on the ballot even though he announced his withdrawal from the race in February. More recently, Zine said he was not campaigning but would serve if elected.

Although Democrats outnumber Republicans 48.7 percent to 22.1 percent (with 24.3 percent listing no party preference) in the district’s latest voter-registration report, it was anticipated that Clark or Zine could advance to the runoff by consolidating GOP support.

The 45th District includes the west Valley communities of Calabasas, Hidden Hills, Canoga Park, Woodland Hills, West Hills, Winnetka, Northridge, Reseda, Tarzana and Encino and the Ventura County community of Bell Canyon.

The special election was scheduled after Dababneh, a Democrat, resigned from the Assembly late last year after being accused by several women of sexual misconduct.

It was one of three special elections in L.A. on Tuesday. In the northeast San Fernando Valley, six candidates were running to replace Raul Bocanegra, who resigned amid sexual-harassment allegations, and in a district that includes parts of L.A., Culver City and Inglewood, four were vying to replace Sebastian Ridley-Thomas, who resigned, citing health problems.
