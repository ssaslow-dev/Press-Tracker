GARCETTI ricochets through swing states -- HARKEY’s wild interview -- Legal challenges to #MeToo laws -- GOP PAC attacks ROUDA -- OAKLAND A’s clinch playoff spot; GIANTS fire GM

By Maya Parthasarathy; Carla Marinucci; Jeremy B White

THE BUZZ: As he contemplates a presidential run, Los Angeles Mayor Eric Garcetti has demonstrated his ability to project his political influence beyond California’s borders.

-- He spent the last few days ricocheting from South Carolina to Nevada, where he helped rally support for Democratic House candidates and their backers. He visited the Charleston County Democratic Party’s jamboree, a pair of South Carolina churches and a union hall in Las Vegas. That came after a swing through Ohio earlier this month.

-- The cross-country tour parallels Lt. Gov. Gavin Newsom stumping for California congressional candidates and underscores a central dynamic of 2018: It’s all-hands-on-deck as the party focuses on flipping the House, and Democrats with national ambitions are raising their profiles by pitching in.

-- Garcetti’s national rainmaking continues Tuesday with an event that’s back on his home turf but focused elsewhere.

Harnessing his connections to the entertainment industry glitterati — as he has before — the mayor is hosting a fundraiser for other state Democratic parties that will feature Jimmy Kimmel and DJ Khaled, seeking to raise $100,000 apiece for party organizations in California, Iowa, Nevada, New Hampshire, Michigan, Minnesota, Oklahoma, Ohio, Pennsylvania and Wisconsin. Garcetti noted in a statement that those parties are instrumental to local efforts to flip Congress — and that state legislatures shape House races by controlling redistricting (in other places).

MITCHELL WEIGHS IN -- A POLITICO story by Carla Marinucci this week on Democratic women legislators donating to state Assemblywoman Cristina Garcia — still under ongoing investigation for sexual harassment — originally noted that state Sen. Holly Mitchell did not respond for comment. We regret we missed her attempt to get in touch in time for deadline. So here’s the response from Mitchell, who spoke to POLITICO Monday and defended her $2,500 contribution to Garcia:

-- “From my perspective ... [Garcia] was cleared of all sexual harassment allegations. She is a sitting member of the legislature and she is a colleague,’’ said Mitchell, the vice-chair of the Joint Legislative Subcommittee on Sexual Harassment Prevention and Response. She said that the investigation of an alleged groping incident by Garcia was reopened because of the assembly's procedure to allow an appeal from former legislative staffer Daniel Fierro. "The [state] Senate does not have a system of appeal,’’ Mitchell said. “That’s on the Assembly — and that’s not my house,’’ she said.

-- Mitchell added that her campaign-season contribution to Garcia follows her long-standing practice of supporting fellow Democrats and said that she had no control over the timing of the governor's decision to sign a bill the same week to better protect legislative sexual harassment victims. “I have made significant contributions across the board. I’m a team player, and I support my colleagues who are sitting members. ... So given all of those things, that’s why I made my contribution — and stand by it.”
