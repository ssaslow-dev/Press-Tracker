Matchups set for Ventura County's Assembly seats in the November general election

Christian Martinez
Ventura
June 6, 2018, 9:00 p.m. ET

Matchups for Ventura County’s state Assembly races were set for November’s general election Wednesday morning once all precincts had reported results from Tuesday’s primary election.

The primary narrowed some fields of candidates while also providing a preview of what is to come in the fall contests. 

Incumbent Assemblyman Dante Acosta, R-Santa Clarita, who represents District 38, received nearly 33,200 votes or 54.2 percent of the total vote Tuesday, according to the California Secretary of State’s Office.

His one and only challenger, Democrat Christy Smith, received just more than 28,000 votes, 45.8 percent of the ballots cast.

Acosta, a first-term incumbent, and Smith, a board member for the Newhall School District, will face off in November for the second consecutive general election.

Acosta defeated Smith in 2016 with nearly 53 percent of the vote, outperforming Republican voter registration in the district, which represents Simi Valley and much of the Santa Clarita Valley.

The incumbent described Tuesday’s primary as a “very elaborate poll,” adding that he was pleased with the results. He said voters were happy with him and highlighted his stance against the gas tax and bipartisan record as reasons to return him to office.

For his opponent, however, the primary results showed room for improvement.

“We have a lot more work to do,” Smith said, “but it’s a good place to start.

“I haven’t reached every family yet,” she said.

Heading into the general election campaign, Smith said she would focus on increasing voter turnout, which was low Tuesday.

According to the California Secretary of State’s Office, voter turnout was below 26 percent for both Ventura and Los Angeles counties.

“There is a lot to be said about that,” she said.

In the race for Assembly District 44, Republican Ronda Baldwin-Kennedy emerged as the challenger to two-term incumbent Jacqui Irwin, D-Thousand Oaks.

The district represents much of central and eastern Ventura County along with Westlake Village.

Baldwin-Kennedy received more than 25,250 votes, good for 45.2 percent of the ballots cast. Irwin, seeking a third term, received 28,422 votes, just under 51 percent of the vote total.

Democrat Robert Zelinksky was eliminated after receiving less than 4 percent of the vote.

“I’m encouraged by the results,” Irwin said. “I feel good about it.” 

The incumbent said her campaign spent little for the primary and noted that voter turnout was low. Irwin said she felt confident heading into the general election.

“We have very clear differences in ideology,” she said of her opponent. “I’m not sure if (Baldwin-Kennedy) is going to have the same relationship with voters.” 

Baldwin-Kennedy also found Tuesday’s results encouraging.

“I campaigned lightly. I’m just imagining what I can do with a full campaign,” she said. “Jacqui Irwin has to realize that I’m a candidate to contend with.”

Assembly District 37 voters overwhelmingly cast their ballots for first-term incumbent Monique Limón, D-Santa Barbara.

Limón received nearly 42,300 votes Tuesday, 83.1 percent of the vote total far more than her second-place opponent, Democrat David Norrdin, of Ventura, who garnered 10 percent.

Sofia Collin, a Santa Barbara Democrat, was eliminated from the race, having received less than 7 percent of the vote.

“It’s humbling to have that type of margin,” Limón said, adding that she would not take her support for granted.

“I think it’s a reflection of continuing to work hard,” she said. “That will not change.”

Limón said she plans to conduct a full campaign with voter calls and precinct walks in her district, which represents much of Santa Barbara County and western Ventura County.

Norrdin did not reply to calls as of Wednesday afternoon.

Democrat Jesse Gabriel emerged victorious in a special general election for Assembly District 45, defeating Republican opponent Justin Clark with nearly 64 percent of the total vote.

Gabriel will serve as Assembly member for the district until the end of 2018.

The previous incumbent, Democrat Matt Dababneh, resigned in December amid sexual misconduct allegations, prompting the special general election.

A regular primary was also held Tuesday, with Gabriel and Clark emerging as the victors.

The pair will face off again in November for a full, two-year term as assemblymember for the district.
