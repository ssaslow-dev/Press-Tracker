At a glance: Assembly seats contested across L.A. County

By Elizabeth Chou

Sixteen lawmakers across Los Angeles County were fending off challengers to their state Assembly seats during Tuesday’s primary election, and for the most part appeared to have no trouble staying in the game.

There are 24 Assembly races on the county ballot, 16 of which were contested, with the sitting members hoping to continue representing districts throughout the Los Angeles region, including in the San Fernando, Santa Clarita and San Gabriel valleys.

Several of the incumbents are opposed by one other person, which means both the sitting member and the challenger will advance to the general election under the state’s top-two primary system. But in many of the other races there are two or more challengers, and the incumbents will need to make sure they are among the top two vote-getters if they want to stay in the race.

Among the incumbents fighting to hold onto their seats is Cristina Garcia, who was accused of sexual harassment earlier this year and on Tuesday faced off against seven challengers. Early results from vote-by-mail ballots showed a Republican challenger, Mike Simpfenderfer, nearly neck-and-neck with her.

There are also three other seats up for grabs, two of which are in the San Fernando Valley, where lawmakers Raul Bocanegra and Matt Dababneh recently stepped down following allegations of sexual harassment. Those seats are currently vacant, so run-off elections were also on Tuesday’s ballot to decide who will complete the unfinished terms. The third open seat — which represents a district that includes West Los Angeles, Culver City and other communities north of Inglewood — was left vacant after Sebastian Ridley-Thomas stepped down citing health reasons.

The top two candidates in each of the open or contested races will advance to the general election in November. Assembly members from five districts ran unopposed, and will be able to keep their seats for another term. They are the 64th District’s Mike Gipson, of Carson; the 50th District’s Richard Bloom, of Santa Monica; the 51st District’s Wendy Carrillo, of Fullerton; the 48th District’s Blanca E. Rubio, of Baldwin Park and the 43rd District’s Laura Friedman, of Glendale. Here is a round-up of how the early vote-by-mail results sorted out in the contested and open races:

36th District: Republican Assemblyman Tom Lackey of Lancaster was holding onto a frontrunner position against his Democratic challenger, Steve Fox, an attorney from Palmdale who held the office before him, as both advance to the general election.

38th District: Republican Assemblyman Dante Acosta of Santa Clarita was holding a commanding lead against Newhall School District Board Member Christy Smith, a Democrat from Valencia who came close to unseating Acosta in 2016. In the previous race, she garnered just over 48 percent of the votes during the general election that year. Both Acosta and Smith will advance to the general election.

39th District: Democrat Luz Rivas and Republican Ricardo Antonio Benitez were both holding commanding leads ahead of four other candidates vying for an open seat representing the northeast San Fernando Valley. The pair were also pitted against each other in a run-off race Tuesday to determine who will complete Bocanegra’s unfinished term. 41st District: Democratic Assemblyman Chris Holden of Los Angeles was commanding an overwhelming lead against challenger Alan S. Reynolds, a development engineer from South Pasadena who did not pick a party preference. Democrat Kenneth Rotter, an attorney from Pasadena, trailed even further behind.

44th District: Democratic Jacqui Irwin of Camarillo was defending her seat against strong results from Republican Ronda Baldwin-Kennedy, an attorney. Despite a third candidate who garnered a tiny fraction of the results, the two appear to be headed to the general election if the trend continues.

45th District: Justin Clark, a Republican, and Democrat Jesse Gabriel, an attorney, were neck-and-neck atop a field of seven candidates looking to claim an open seat in the west San Fernando Valley that became vacant after Matt Dababneh stepped down in the wake of sexual harassment allegations. Clark and Gabriel are also facing off in a concurrent run-off election to complete Dababneh’s unfinished term. The remaining candidates for the full term was led by Tricia Robbins Kasson, an economic development director.

46th District: Democratic Assemblyman Adrin Nazarian of Studio City was well ahead of his challenger, Roxanne Beckford Hoge, a Republican from Valley Village, in the early results, as both advance to the general election. 49th District: Democratic Assemblyman Ed Chau of Arcadia held an overwhelming lead against a challenge from Republican Burton Brink, a retired Sheriff’s Sergeant from Arcadia, in the early numbers, as both advance to the general election. 52nd District: Democratic Assemblyman Freddie Rodriguez of Pomona held a strong lead against his three challengers, after the early vote-by-mail results. The battle for the second spot was being led by Toni Holle, a Republican from Chino. 53rd District: Democratic Assemblyman Miguel Santiago came in with a commanding lead, while fellow Democrat Kevin Hee Young Jang, an attorney from Los Angeles, appeared to be in position to advance with him to the general election. 54th District: Democrat Sydney K. Kamlager was flying above the five other candidates in contention for the open seat left vacant by Ridley-Thomas. Republican Glen Ratcliff, a small business owner, was holding a strong lead among the other candidates and appeared in position to also advance to the general election, although educator and scientist Tepring Piquado, was not far behind in the early numbers.

55th District: Republican Assemblyman Phillip Chen, of Diamond Bar, was holding a wide lead over several challengers, including a Democratic challenger Gregg D. Fritchle, a social worker from Covina.

57th District: Democratic Assemblyman Ian C. Calderon was commanding the largest share of the votes in the early results, which also show two Republican challengers battling for the chance to also advance to the general election. They are educator Jessica Martinez of La Habra and journeyman lineman Oscar J. Llamas of Whittier. Democrat Justin Joshua Valero, university professor, was in fourth place.

58th District: Seven challengers were looking to unseat Democratic Assemblywoman Cristina Garcia, and early results show a Republican, Mike Simpfenderfer, leading the pack. Simpfenderfer, who describes himself as a father and victim’s advocate, came in with slightly more votes than Garcia in the initial vote-by-mail results but appeared to begin losing ground as more numbers came in through the night. Pedro Aceituno, a city councilman, was in third place and leading the field of six Democratic challengers. Garcia has faced allegations of sexual harassment, which was found unsubstantiated following an investigation. The accuser is currently appealing the decision.

59th District: Democratic Assemblyman Reggie Jones-Sawyer of Inglewood was holding a overwhelming lead ahead of challenger Leslie Hagan-Morgan, a fellow Democrat from Hawthorne, as both advance to the general election. 62nd District: Democratic Assemblywoman Autumn Burke of Marina Del Rey was taking an easy lead against broker and businessman Al L. Hernandez, a Republican challenger, as both advance to the general election.

63rd District: Democratic Assemblyman Anthony Rendon was holding a wide lead as he faced challenges from Democrat Maria Estrada, a community advocate and accountant from Beverly Hills, and Republican Adam Joshua Miller, a documentary producer from Lakewood.

66th District: Democratic Assemblyman Al Muratsuchi of Torrance was in a dead heat with Republican challenger Frank Scotto, a small business owner from Torrance. If the trend continues, the two are expected to advance to the general election, despite another challenger.

70th District: Democratic Assemblyman Patrick O’Donnell of Long Beach was having no problems defending his seat in the early results. Libertarian Honor “Mimi” Robson, a professional engineer from Long Beach, appeared the strongest among the field of three challengers.
