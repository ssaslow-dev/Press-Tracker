Election Day is Tuesday. Here are some races that will impact San Fernando Valley voters

By Ryan Carter

The June 5 primary election is upon us, which means it’s time for a quick reminder of some races and measures San Fernando Valley voters will see — depending on what part of the Valley you’re in, of course.

On the municipal level — city and county — the list is relatively sparse, though in Glendale and Burbank voters are considering charter amendments that will have lasting impact. Many Valley voters will consider whether a veteran politician should be able to remain in her seat on the L.A. County Board of Supervisors.

Across the Valley, a spate of high-profile state and federal seats are on the ballot, including two special elections and a spate of primaries.

In the spirit of the notion that all politics is local, here’s a rundown on the most local items in the Valley area, starting with the municipal measures.

• Glendale’s Charter Amendment P seeks to improve and increase voter participation with a change in the charter of the date of the general municipal election from April in odd numbered years to coincide with the statewide primary election in even numbered years beginning in March 2020 (or as determined by the state Legislature).

• Burbank has three measures on the ballot seeking changes to the City Charter for infrastructure and timing of municipal and school board elections with hopes of increasing voter turnout. Measure T asks voters if they want to amend the city’s charter to continue the transfer of not more than 7 percent of Burbank Water and Power’s sales of electricity to the city’s general fund. Measure V asks voters if they want to change the city’s odd-numbered year general elections to evennumbered years, get rid of the city’s primary election, extend terms of city officials by a year and eight months to coincide with the even-year elections and simplify the election process laid out in the charter — all with a goal of increasing voter turnout. And in a similar spirit, Measure Y also seeks to change the city’s school board elections to even-numbered years.

Now, to the politicians:

• The L.A. County Board of Supervisors District 3 seat represents a slice of the San Fernando Valley from Atwater Village to Calabasas. Incumbent Sheila Kuehl is running against challengers Daniel Glaser and Eric Preven. The seat, which encompasses a swath of Valley neighborhoods, is expected to go to Kuehl, known as a progressive icon and former state legislator in a region, where she has championed the fight on LGBQT issues and child welfare. But the lesser-known Glaser and Preven think more could be done on homelessness and have called for more transparency among officials. Kuehl took office in 2014 after years as a state legislator. The real question though, is what voters think. If history is any guide, incumbent supervisors tend to hold on to their seats, for a while.

And then there are the state and national seats. The Valley has its share.

• Two special elections are on the ballot to determine who will represent pieces of the Valley in the state Assembly.— Democrat Jesse Gabriel and 19-year-old Republican Justin Clark are challenging each other in a runoff in a special election to replace Matt Dababneh in the California Assembly’s District 45. The 45th District includes the west Valley communities of Calabasas, Hidden Hills, Canoga Park, Woodland Hills, West Hills, Winnetka, Northridge, Reseda, Tarzana and Encino and the Ventura County community of Bell Canyon. The special election was scheduled after Dababneh, a Democrat, resigned from the Assembly late last year after being accused by several women of sexual misconduct. Gabriel, a constitutional rights attorney whose campaign was endorsed by the Democratic Party of the San Fernando Valley, has emphasized his role in filing a lawsuit against the Trump administration on behalf of young immigrant “Dreamers.” Clark, a political science student at CSUN, has run on an anti-corruption platform.— In the 39th District, candidates are also looking to fill a seat left open by a leader who resigned after allegations of sexual misconduct. Democrat Luz Rivas is in a special election runoff against Republican Ricardo Benitez to replace Raul Bocanegra, representing the northeast San Fernando Valley in the California Assembly. The 39th District includes Arleta, Lake View Terrace, Mission Hills, North Hollywood, Pacoima, Sun Valley, Sunland-Tujunga, Sylmar and San Fernando. Bocanegra, a Democrat, resigned late last year, the first of three California legislators to quit after being accused of sexual harassment.— In the hotly contested 25th Congressional District, Republican incumbent Steve Knight looks to hold onto his seat against Democratic challengers, who are waging an all-out war against each other to see who will face him in November. The seat is the most competitive among LA County congressional races, especially given that the district’s voters went for Hillary Clinton in the 2016 Presidential Election.

— The 29th Congressional race might have been a bit off your radar until a child sex-abuse allegation emerged against incumbent Tony Cárdenas, a Democrat who has denied them. Cárdenas is running for a fourth term to represent an area that includes Panorama City, Van Nuys, San Fernando, Sylmar, Pacoima, Arleta, Sun Valley and North Hollywood and is about two-thirds Latino. But what might have been a “slam dunk” Democratic district has been disrupted by the allegations, leaving an opening in the primary for his challengers: Juan Rey (no party affiliation), Democrat Joseph Shammas; Republican Benito Benny Bernal; and Angelica Maria Duenas, Green.
