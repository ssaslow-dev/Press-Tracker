Young Wolf new man on council

AGOURA HILLS

on November 11, 2022


By John Loesing
newstip@theacorn.com

ONE-TWO PUNCH—Agoura Hills City Councilmember and Mayor Deborah Klein Lopez celebrates her reelection with new challenger and fellow winner Jeremy Wolf. MICHAEL COONS/Acorn Newspapers

The election hasn’t been declared official but the message is clear: 31-year-old Jeremy Wolf appears to have won election to the Agoura Hills City Council on his first try. Wolf, who is chair of the city’s planning commission, becomes the youngest person to be elected to the city council in Agoura Hills’ 40-year history.

A longtime volunteer in the city and a staunch advocate for open space, Wolf won 28% of the vote and finished second in the Nov. 8 election.

Wolf said he knocked on more than 4,500 doors from one end of the city to the other to introduce himself to the residents, and raised more than $30,000 for a campaign that hit the ground running and never looked back.

“I feel really good about the work I put in and feel good about the campaign I ran,” said Wolf, who works as district director for state Sen. Henry Stern.

“I’m on cloud nine, I couldn’t be happier,” he said.

Mayor Deborah Klein Lopez, who also works in government as field representative for 45th District Assembly member Jesse Gabriel, was successful in her first reelection bid and was the top vote-getter with 29% of the more than 16,000 votes cast.

CIVIC DUTY—A voter casts his ballot at Agoura Hills City Hall. JOHN LOESING/Acorn Newspapers

Lopez and Wolf ran as a ticket, a pair of candidates both with strong feelings for the environment and quality of life in Agoura Hills

“Jeremy and I walked from mid-July to last night (Nov. 7), Lopez said.

“It’s nice to be with someone who shares your values and vision for Agoura Hills. People recognized me and know what I stand for.

“I’m honored that the voters of Agoura Hills have recognized my hard work over the past four years.”

Lopez said she wasn’t deterred by attacks last summer against her and the city’s housing plan, a road map for development that opened the door to hundreds of new units along Kanan Road, among other areas.

The units are required in order for the city to meet the state’s mandate for new housing.

“People understood the decision we made was the best for the city and minimized those concerns (about traffic and emergency evacuation). People understood we were working for them.”

Lopez said the city’s new bicycle master plan and clean energy initiative will be areas of hard work that lie ahead for the city in 2023.

Chris Anstead, the only member of the council to vote against the city’s housing plan due to concerns about emergency evacuation, placed third in the voting with 21% and was also elected to a four-year term on the council.

Like Lopez, it was his first attempt at reelection.

David Levine Bramante (12%), a real estate agent and Liberty Canyon resident, and Jan Gerstel (9%), president of the Morrison Ranch Homeowners Association, finished fourth and fifth respectively.
