Newsom signs legislation to extend to-go cocktails, outdoor dining expansion in California

Updated: 10:50 PM PDT Oct 8, 2021Editorial Standards ⓘKCRA StaffSACRAMENTO, Calif. —

Gov. Gavin Newsom on Friday signed legislation that will allow to-go cocktails and outdoor dining expansions to continue at California restaurants.

"We couldn't be more thankful that this is going through," said Brandon Robinson, owner of Flatstick Pub in Sacramento.

During the pandemic, his team bought 4,000 glass growlers for to-go beers. Each one can hold about four pints.

"We didn't have much options during that time, so we invested in the growlers, and you know the glass was back-ordered for months. So we waited months to get it and by the time we actually got the glass in the fall of 2020, things were starting to open back up,” Robinson said.

Amid the pandemic last year, California's Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control started allowing of-age patrons to pick up cocktails when they bought a meal.

Senate Bill 389, which allows restaurants to sell to-go alcoholic beverages with food orders, will sunset after five years, Sen. Bill Dodd, D-Napa, bill co-author, said in a news release on the bill signing.

"It's definitely been great for us," said Raymond James Irwin, the owner of Fizz Champagne & Bubbles Bar in Downtown Sacramento. "We definitely have been selling fizz to-go in cups and whatnot with lids to people waiting in line. It's been a great thing for long lines at DOCO. It's great for us and they can take some fizz while they're waiting in the line."

The to-go beverages must be purchased with a meal, and there's a maximum of two drinks per meal. The drinks must be packed with a secure lid or cap preventing consumption without breaking the seal, and mixed drinks and cocktails cannot exceed more than 4.5 ounces of distilled spirits.

"It's always convenient to get everything at the same place when you don't have to run to two or three different venues," said Cindy Vandemoer, who was dining with her daughter at Fizz. "Makes it really handy too, and maybe you have a really good bartender that'll make you a nice drink."

"This is a pathway for these businesses to, frankly, make up for a lot of the constraints that have been imposed on them over the course of the last 18 months," Gov. Newsom said at a news conference at an Oakland restaurant.

As COVID-19 forced many restaurants to drastically change how they operated, more than 35 states started allowing eateries and/or bars to sell cocktails to-go. Since then, more than a dozen states have passed legislation making to-go cocktails permanent, according to the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States.

The outdoor dining expansions, also known as parklets, became popular during the pandemic as a way for restaurants to serve food while complying with COVID-19 restrictions. With the parklets, restaurants were given permission to take up space in front of their business for outside dining.

Assembly Bill 61 and Senate Bill 314 give businesses that temporarily expanded their premises a one-year grace period after the end of the emergency to apply for permanent expansion.

"Outdoor dining has been a critical lifeline that has helped these establishments keep their doors open during these challenging times. AB 61 provides important flexibility so that restaurants can safely expand outdoor dining and continue to serve the communities they call home," said Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino.

SB 314 allows restaurants to continue serving alcohol in parklets.

"Without 314, we can't really make parklets work," Laurie Thomas, executive director of the Golden Gate Restaurant Association, told the San Francisco Chronicle last month.
