Teen gifts ‘Bundles of Kindness’

Rachael Rosenberg packs each bag with survival items. (photo courtesy of Rachael Rosenberg)

As most children prepare for their 13th birthday, they may consider what gifts they want or who to invite to their party, but as she prepared to welcome her teenage years, Rachael Rosenberg was contemplating how she could best serve others.

Since she was 2 years old, Valley resident Rosenberg has been volunteering.

As a small child, she’d tag along with her family to food pantries throughout Los Angeles County, helping stock the shelves she could reach. As she grew older, she signed up at her temple to make sandwiches for those experiencing homelessness, and even cooked some meals to deliver on her own.

“Ever since I can remember, I’ve seen people who are homeless all over L.A.,” Rosenberg said. “I’ve been surrounded by it constantly, and I couldn’t understand why no one was helping or making an effort to actually change how people were living, and I wanted to do something.”

As she was considering volunteer options for her bat mitzvah project, Rosenberg said she wanted to start making a deeper connection with the individuals she was serving. So, she founded Bundles of Kindness to do just that.

The organization packs bags with “survival items” to be given to individuals experiencing homelessness, particularly in heavily-populated areas such as Hollywood, along Highland and Fairfax avenues. The bags include umbrellas, flashlights, socks, dental hygiene products, underwear, sleep mats, haircut vouchers, feminine hygiene products and snacks.

“We try to give items that people need, but maybe aren’t items that people would often consider when donating,” Rosenberg said.

At the beginning, Rosenberg created an Amazon wish list and asked family and friends to donate items. As the organization grew, she began asking large businesses for donations.

Soon after, packages began to show up at Rosenberg’s home, where she operated Bundles of Kindness in the beginning stages.

The family’s garage was quickly overrun with boxes, and items began overflowing into the living room and foyer. One day, the doorbell rang and Rosenberg was met with a donation of 2,500 pairs of socks.

“It was very chaotic,” Rosenberg said with a laugh. “I definitely tripped a few times here and there.”

Now a 15-year-old homeschool student, Rosenberg operates Bundles of Kindness out of a storage unit. To date, she has hand-delivered more than 500 bags to individuals throughout Los Angeles County, including in Beverly Hills near City Hall, West Hollywood along Santa Monica Boulevard and Hollywood along La Brea Avenues. She also regularly distributes bags outside the Veterans Affairs Hospital on Wilshire Boulevard

“It’s so great to see the individuals in person,” Rosenberg said. “Some people have even started crying, and it’s very touching and it just shows that everything that I’ve done is worth it, and that more people should be doing something to contribute, even if it’s small.”

To create the bags, Rosenberg has hosted two assembly days, inviting members of the community to Sherman Oaks to help stuff items into her personalized bags that read “Rachael’s Bundles of Kindness.”

“Without the community support, I don’t know if this would be possible to this degree,” she said. “It shows that more people care about it than you would think.”

Rosenberg has received several recognitions for her work, including acknowledgement from L.A. City Councilman Bob Blumenfield, 3rd District, and being named ABC 7’s Cool Kid of the Week. On Nov. 14, state Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel (D-Woodland Hills) named Rosenberg the 2021 Youth Activist of the Year.

“Rachael has delivered hope, dignity and important necessities to hundreds of our homeless neighbors around Los Angeles,” Gabriel said. “My office had the opportunity to support one of her projects and we were so inspired by how someone of her age could put together such a successful event with so many resources. Rachael doesn’t wait for others to address the issues that matter – she does it herself, and that makes her an inspiration to us all.”

Rosenberg’s mom, Deborah Rosenberg, said she’s proud to see the impact her daughter has had on the community.

“You can raise a child and support them in lots of different ways, but when it all boils down, you want your children to be kind, you want your children to be aware of their community around them and you want them to take an active role in being committed to making the world a better place,” Deborah Rosenberg said. “And she does that.”

Rosenberg is currently planning another round of distribution of about 500 winter care packages to individuals throughout L.A., though exact sites are still to be determined.

Rosenberg said she hopes to one day turn Bundles of Kindness into its own nonprofit that offers long-term assistance to homeless individuals. For now, though, she’s proud of the work she’s doing.

“I just want to help encourage other people to do something about the problems they see and kind of prove that you don’t have to be an adult to make a change,” Rosenberg said.
