California records reveal Maui lawmakers, other pandemic travel

A Delta plane taxis at Los Angeles International Airport on July 12, 2018 in Los Angeles, California. | Mario Tama/Getty Images

By Jeremy B. White

03/15/2021 06:43 PM EDT

Updated: 03/15/2021 10:01 PM EDT

California lawmakers repeatedly traveled out of state last year on interest group-funded trips despite the coronavirus pandemic, according to financial disclosures.

Legislators headed to Portland, Ore., and Seattle in the fall. Some visited a conference in Arizona days before Gov. Gavin Newsom ordered California into a statewide lockdown. Lawmakers went to Japan before the coronavirus swamped California but when it was already present in China.

Documents also confirm POLITICO’s earlier reporting that more than a dozen lawmakers of both parties flew to Hawaii in November and revealed a previously undisclosed attendee: state Sen. Scott Wilk (R-Santa Clarita), who has since become Senate minority leader.

The disclosures show that five state lawmakers who did not respond to repeated calls and emails last fall about the trip did indeed jet to Maui: Assemblymembers Jim Cooper (D-Elk Grove), Tom Daly (D-Anaheim), Patrick O’Donnell (D-Long Beach), former Assemblymember Bill Brough (R-Dana Point) and Sen. Susan Rubio (D-Baldwin Park).

Where did they go: An annual voyage to Hawaii that mingles legislators with interest group representatives draws headlines every year. Legislators faced an unusually large backlash this year for crossing an ocean when Californians were being urged to stay home. But lawmakers also attended conferences around California and outside the state’s borders:

— Japan: A half-dozen lawmakers traveled to Japan for a trip hosted by the Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs at a cost of around $12,000. The trip occurred in early February, shortly before the first coronavirus case of unknown origin was confirmed in California.

— Oregon, Washington: Several lawmakers traveled to the Pacific Northwest in October for a trip funded by the California Foundation on the Environment and the Economy to the tune of about $3,000.

The foundation was an active sponsor of trips last year. Lawmakers reported thousands of dollars’ worth of CFEE conferences in South Lake Tahoe, in Berkeley, in Napa and around the Central Valley.

— Arizona: Three state lawmakers reported going to Scottsdale, Ariz., for a mid-March conference sponsored by the first responders’ crisis referral service Safe Call Now. Assemblymembers Cooper, Heath Flora (R-Ripon) and Freddie Rodriguez (D-Pomona) returned March 14, five days before Newsom issued a stay-at-home order.

What else: Two particularly well-attended events were pre-pandemic: The California Life Sciences Association hosted numerous legislators for a January conference in Yountville, and the California Teachers Association hosted several Bay Area lawmakers for a January retreat in Sonoma.

Baby gifts were popular for lawmakers who became parents. Sen. Monique Limón (D-Santa Barbara) reported dozens of baby shower gifts from fellow legislators; Assemblymember Sabrina Cervantes (D-Riverside), the mother of triplets, received multiple sets of three onesies; and Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) received multiple baby swaddles.

And the Los Angeles Dodgers’ World Series victory made it into gift forms. Multiple lawmakers reported receiving Dodgers world champion T-shirts at a fundraiser for Assembly Speaker Anthony Rendon (D-Lakewood) the day after the Dodgers claimed their first title in decades.
