Frontline GOPers caught in health care jam

CONCEPTS OF A PLAN — Endangered House Republicans in California were forced back into health care limbo as the Trump administration on Monday delayed announcing a plan to extend Obamacare subsidies and started reworking the details amid backlash from hard-liners.

The whiplash was jarring — following reporting that the administration was planning to announce a health care framework including a two-year extension of the subsidies due to expire at the end of next month.

Republican Rep. Kevin Kiley expressed, before the delay, that he was “grateful that the President is endorsing the key components of our legislation,” referring to a bipartisan bill from him and Rep. Sam Liccardo geared at renewing the tax credits dividing the Republican conference.

Hours later, the White House’s support was in flux. It didn’t release specifics or hold an expected Monday afternoon announcement, limiting the political cover to vulnerable members absorbing attacks over the impending expiration.

Adding to the intraparty division: Conservatives were blindsided by President Donald Trump’s initial framework and were still evaluating Monday whether they would support it while waiting for information on whether it would expand restrictions on abortion-related funding, POLITICO reported.

It’s now unclear what exactly the president will propose, and more than that, what he’ll be able to get through the House as Speaker Mike Johnson labors to wrangle his members.

The purgatory state for the tax credits allowed opponents of frontline California Republicans, namely Kiley and Rep. David Valadao, to slam them over their party’s inability or unwillingness to extend the Affordable Care Act subsidies.

“Kevin Kiley and David Valadao can pretend they care about keeping health care affordable all they want – but when their party guts Medicaid and threatens ACA tax credits, they fall right in line every time,” Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee spokesperson Anna Elsasser said in a statement.

Democrats are prioritizing health care in their campaigns to oust both representatives. Though Kiley hasn’t declared which district he’s running in, Democrats are lining up in the open 6th, focusing their messaging heavily on his vote for Medicaid cuts earlier this year.

The Democratic challengers to Valadao, who lost a race in 2018 after voting to repeal the Affordable Care Act, have blasted him for the same thing.

Asked about extending Obamacare more broadly, he recently told POLITICO that, “obviously, there’s a lot of problems with the ACA and just extending it cleanly, I don’t think is a solution, but we’ll see how it plays out.”

The White House and vulnerable Republicans have little time to act before the midterms. With subsidies set to expire next month, constituents’ premiums could climb before Election Day. And health care groups aren’t optimistic that a broader restructuring of the signature health care law is possible before then.

“With just weeks until Californians must make their decisions about whether to keep their covered plans, there’s little time to thoughtfully implement sweeping financing or eligibility changes and high risk of unintended consequences that threaten health coverage and leave lives hanging in the balance,” California Pan-Ethnic Health Network Executive Director Kiran Savage-Sangwan said in a statement.

— Mia McCarthy contributed to this report

SACRAMENTO SCANDAL

‘COLLECTIVE OUTRAGE’ — Democratic consultant Steve Maviglio and other friends of Newsom’s indicted former chief of staff Dana Williamson have started fundraising for her legal defense. Williamson is currently under investigation for alleged wire and tax fraud, according to the Department of Justice, and has pleaded not guilty to all charges.

“From the moment news broke about our friend Dana, she’s received an outpouring of support from friends, family and colleagues across the nation,” the organizers of the page for her fundraising wrote. “Your kind messages -- fueled by collective outrage and fierce loyalty -- have been invaluable as she fights the legal battle of her life.”

Williamson is accused of helping funnel around $225,000 from a dormant campaign account apparently belonging to former Health and Human Services Secretary Xavier Becerra to Becerra’s former chief of staff.

Maviglio has vigorously defended her. Another Sacramento fixture, Mark Krausse, made an opening $1,000 donation to the fund. Newsom appointed Krausse to the state labor relations board when Williamson was chief of staff in the administration, and Krausse previously worked at PG&E and served as director of the Fair Political Practices Commission — the state’s campaign finance watchdog.

“We love you, Dana! Anything we can do to help!” Krausse wrote on the page, signing the note: “Krausse family.”

CAMPAIGN YEAR(S)

DROPOUT ALERT — Businessperson Stephen J. Cloobeck ended his campaign for governor Monday evening and endorsed Rep. Eric Swalwell in the race during a joint appearance on CNN’s “The Story Is with Elex Michaelson.”

“I’m gonna merge my campaign into his and give him all the hard work that I’ve worked on,” said the Democratic time share executive, who’d been polling at below 1 percent in the race.

Though he has few votes to send Swalwell’s way, Cloobeck previously told Playbook he could financially back the East Bay representative’s campaign after pouring more than $10 million into his own candidacy.

FROM O.C. TO THE BOE — State Sen. Tom Umberg announced this morning he’s running for the state Board of Equalization as he’s forced out of the Legislature by term limits next year. The prolifically productive former federal prosecutor has served three stints in the Legislature, dating back to 1990, where he has focused heavily on criminal justice issues.

Umberg, a Democrat from Orange County, launches his campaign for the Southern California-based 4th District with more than $1 million on hand and endorsements from Sen. Adam Schiff and several House and state legislative Democrats. In a statement, he said he’s running to “bring disciplined oversight, fairness, and an unwavering commitment to the public good on behalf of the people of Southern California.”

HOUSING

MASTER COLLAPSE — Local politicians, business leaders and disaster relief experts put together a proposal for an agency charged with rebuilding the Pacific Palisades and Altadena after this year’s fires.

But, as Liam Dillon writes, “the plan failed spectacularly. Distrustful victims revolted, while local officials, skittish about the new agency’s power, fragmented. The state lawmaker pushing legislation to make the idea a reality abandoned it.”

The abrupt collapse of the Resilient Rebuilding Authority underscored how elected officials have been unable to develop a master plan for the recovery effort and lock in adequate funding to carry it out. Nearly a year after the twin blazes claimed 31 lives and destroyed 16,000 homes, businesses, schools and other structures, local politicians have been left to pursue various solutions and homeowners to largely forge ahead on their own.

ON THE HILL

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: TRAIN COPS — Rep. Laura Friedman plans later today to announce legislation aimed at funneling federal funding to urban cities to hire more police officers to patrol public transit.

The bipartisan bill, coauthored by New York Republican Rep. Nicole Malliotakis, would establish grants of a so far unspecified size that could be used for hiring officers and making safety-focused improvements, such as installing shields for train operators.

“Every Angeleno deserves access to safe and affordable transit and that’s exactly what my new, bipartisan legislation is all about,” Friedman said in a statement. “The Safe and Affordable Transit Act will restore confidence and help make sure public transportation is reliable, affordable, and safe for every family, every day.”

The bill is backed by the Amalgamated Transit Union, which reps transit workers. Its first version includes a $50 million annual appropriation through the 2030 fiscal year and would require a study on how to reduce crime on public transit.
