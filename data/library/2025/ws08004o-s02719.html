New California laws direct fines to victim services, bolster protections statewide

by Christina Mendez

Fri, January 3, 2025 at 9:59 PM

Updated Fri, January 3, 2025 at 10:00 PM

New laws to support the victims of domestic violence and human trafficking are going into effect for 2025. (FILE)

REDDING, Calif. — Nine new laws signed by Governor Newsom are taking effect this year with the goal of strengthening safety by supporting survivors across the state.

Laws like AB 2020, SB 963 and SB 989 are supposed to enforce a certain standard when it comes to victim care for law enforcement as well as in emergency rooms.

“Our local allied agency partners and the hospitals and my incredible staff, they are doing some of this already even before it came into law, so the fact that it's coming into law means that it's going to be sustained across the state in a really cool way,” said CEO of One Safe Place Kimberly Johnson.

Leaders at One Safe Place said they appreciate all the new laws, especially the ones they believe are established by people who are doing the groundwork with these victims and know what is needed.

AB 2432 is supposed to fund victim services like One Safe Place, by creating the California Victim Crime Fund which is supposed to guarantee consistent resources by directing fines from corporate white-collar crimes into it.

“I don't know if anyone in the victim services space will ever say there's enough resources to serve all of the victims,” said Johnson. “Last year the victims of crime act there was significant funding risk and so the fact these funds have been put in place that will ensure the sustainability of a lot of the services we provide.”

Johnson says in the past ten years they have made significant progress in the world of victim services, in terms of making the topic less taboo as well as recognizing these things happen all across the state.

While she acknowledges the improvements, she also knows there is still work to be done and appreciates the consideration of smaller counties when previous legislation hasn’t.

“Often times when we think of statewide legislation you have everything from Los Angeles county all the way up to Modoc and Shasta County you have everything in between and I think legislation that is informed that support not only the larger urban centers but also makes a lot of sense and it able to be executed and is accessible in the smaller rural counties always gets me really excited.” said Johnson.

According to the Governor’s office, these are the laws regarding domestic violence and human trafficking support that will be taking effect in 2025.

  • SB1414: Toughens penalties for adults who solicit or exploit minors. (Senator Shannon Grove)
  • SB 989: Strengthens Law Enforcement training for investigations of deaths that might involve domestic violence. (Senator Angelique Ashby)
  • AB 2020: Improves responses to human trafficking survivors for law enforcement working with human trafficking survivors by requiring consistent and compassionate support. (Assemblymember Mia Bonta)
  • SB 963: Provides emergency room protections that ensure victims of domestic violence or human trafficking can safely and confidentially identify themselves to staff members that connect them with local support services. (Senator Angelique Ashby)
  • AB1966: Raises Awareness at major events by requiring the venue that is hosting to provide human trafficking resources. (Assemblymember Laurie Davies)
  • AB 2432: Funds victim service by creating the California Crime Victims Fund to guarantee more resources by directing funds from corporate white-collar crimes into it. (Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel)
  • SB 554: Allows victims to file for protection wherever they feel safest. (Senator Dave Cortese)
  • AB 2024: Makes the process to obtain a restraining order easier (Assemblymember Blanca Pacheco)
  • AB 2308: Extends domestic violence protective order up to 15 years. (Assemblymember Laurie Davies)
