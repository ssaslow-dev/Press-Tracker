Glock ban bill passes California State Assembly

Jackson Ellison

The California Assembly has passed a bill to ban GLOCK pistols, citing the threat of mass shootings from easily converted firearms.

SACRAMENTO, Calif. — A bill to ban the sale of GLOCK pistols in California, one of the most popular guns in the country, passed through the State Assembly.

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel authored AB 1127, which would ban the sale of semiautomatic pistols that could be easily converted with an attachment to a fully automatic weapon. The bill passed on a party-line vote 58-17, with Republicans opposing.

The effort comes after the converted weapons have been used in several mass shootings, including the 2022 Sacramento K Street shooting.

“As parents and lawmakers, we refuse to stand idly by while illegal machine guns are threatening our schools and communities. This commonsense legislation will ensure that the gun industry is held accountable and that we are doing everything possible to protect our communities from mass shootings,” said Asm. Gabriel in a statement following the vote.

It is already federally illegal to own machine guns and the sale of pistol switches is already prohibited in California. Still, advocates say they can be easily brought into the state or made illegally through processes like 3D printing.

“Ultimately, we need the manufacturers of these easily convertible pistols to act responsibly and change their design, and they shouldn’t be allowed to sell in California until they do so,” said Krystal Lopilato, the director for public policy for Everytown for Gun Safety, at a committee hearing.

Glocks are one of the most popular gun manufacturers for both law enforcement and civilians. Supporters of the ban argue that the semi-automatic pistols, with the addition of small switch devices, are too easily convertible to fire automatically.

Gun rights advocates like Sam Paredes, president of Gun Owners of California, argue the legislation is unnecessary because any firearm can theoretically be converted to fully automatic, just not as easily as with switch devices. He argues the bill will only criminalize lawful gun dealers and owners.

“What the state of California needs to do is they need to focus on the criminal element, increasing the punishments for the misuse of any firearms or modifying any gun into a configuration,” Paredes said.

Paredes says Glock has already addressed the issue of its guns through its generations four and five models; but those models are not authorized for sale under the California handgun roster.

“It is a ban, because anyone else in the future who wants to purchase a Glock will not be allowed to do so without committing an egregious crime,” Paredes said.

Under the bill, law enforcement and military personnel would be exempt from the ban and would still be able to purchase the pistols.

The bill will now be reviewed in the state senate. If the bill passes through the legislature and is signed into law by Governor Gavin Newsom, Paredes says he and other leaders of gun rights organizations will file an immediate legal challenge.

“Every pro-Second Amendment organization in the country finds this law to be egregious, a vile usurpation of the Second Amendment, and everyone is planning on joining together to file a lawsuit of massive proportions against this bill,” Paredes said.
