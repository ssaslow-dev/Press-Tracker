Beware: California Israel Lobby 2026 Agenda

Marcy Winograd

Almost overnight the CA Jewish Public Affairs Committee (JPAC) changed its name to Jewish California, a rebranding likely aimed at distancing JPAC from AIPAC, a rhyming acronym for the toxic American Israel Public Affairs Committee. The rebranding of JPAC followed a 2025 interview in which podcaster Van Latham asked California Governor Gavin Newsom, a Presidential hopeful, if he accepted AIPAC money on the campaign trail. After squirming silly and hem-hawing “interesting” a dozen times, Newsom responded that he hadn’t thought about AIPAC in years, only to add that he thought about “JPAC perhaps more, but AIPAC less and less.”

Social media followers were aghast.

Had the Governor napped during Israel’s obliteration of Gaza?

Boom!

JPAC vanished to reemerge as Jewish California–purporting to represent 1.2 million Jews in California–with the same 40 plus Israel lobby groups hurling its 2026 agenda to quarterbacks at the CA Legislative Jewish Caucus (LJC). This 18-member lawmaker caucus, which once flew an Israeli flag on its Sacramento state portal, is led by Assemblymembers Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino), chair, and vice-chairs Dawn Addis (D-Morro Bay) and Senator Josh Becker (D-San Mateo), who once claimed “there was no Palestine." LJC caucus members–all Democrats often referred to as PEP or Progressive Except for Palestine–include four state senate lawmakers and 14 assembly members (listed here), most of whom are Jewish with the exception of Zbur (Santa Monica) and the Rubio sisters, business Democrats in the San Gabriel Valley. All are Israel besties who belong to a caucus out of step with most Democrats. A Gallup poll (2/26), reveals that 65% of Democrats sympathize more with Palestine than Israel.

Pull back the curtain on the LJC and, voila, there’s Jewish California promoting its 2026 priority bills and requests for $165 million for Holocaust museums, Holocaust education and IDF-trained private security at religious institutions. Jewish CA partners include the usual suspects: the nationally boycotted Anti-Defamation League (ADL), Jewish Federation, Stand With Us and Jewish Community Relations Council (JCRC), all Zionist organizations mobilizing their base to flood inboxes of state lawmakers, join bus rides to lobby Sacramento and scream “antisemitism” on the regular. BetTzedek Legal Services, Jewish Center for Justice and the Jewish Labor Committee are also members of Jewish CA.

AB 2664: Shielding sales of Occupied Palestine

As if written by Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu, AB 2664 (Bauer-Kahan-Orinda) virtually bans protests at places of worship promoting illegal sales of occupied Palestinian land or platforming Israeli weapons contractors or showcasing IDF soldiers boasting of their shameful slaughter in Gaza. The bill, sailing through committees and expected to pass the legislature, would declare a 100-foot buffer zone around a synagogue or mosque to prohibit anyone inside the buffer zone from approaching within eight feet of a congregant to hold an “educational” sign or distribute an “educational” flyer. A first offense carries a $10,000 fine and up to six months in jail; a second offense $25,00,000 fine and up to a year in jail. Introduction of AB 2664 follows protests in 2024 outside two Los Angeles synagogues–Shaarey Zedek Congregation in Valley Village and Adas Torah synagogue in Pico-Robertson–for hosting promotional My Home in Israel events featuring real estate in occupied Palestine.

Bonta, Murphy and Borvick

Despite complaints to the office of California Attorney General Rob Bonta, the synagogue real estate events were not investigated for allegedly violating international law that protects civilians in occupied territories or domestic fair housing law that bars discrimination in real estate. In contrast, Attorney General of New Jersey Phil Murray pursued an investigation–not prosecution–following similar real estate sales in his state.

My Home in Israel was founded in 2010 by Gedaliah Borvick, an investment broker who left New Jersey to emigrate or make “aliyah” to Israel, where Jewish immigrants are automatically granted citizenship while exiled Palestinian and descendants are barred from returning to their homeland. 

Elephant in the room

Although Jewish California describes AB 2664 as an effort to “protect safe access to religious institutions while respecting constitutional protest rights,” the ACLU of California told the Assembly Judiciary Committee AB 2664 “would create a new crime, interfere with peaceful protests and violate the First Amendment.” Similarly, the American Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee submitted an opposition letter noting the bill extends leafleting bans into driveways and parking lot entrances, thus expanding “criminal exposure into transitional spaces where ordinary civic interaction occurs …”

David Mandel, member of Jewish Voice for Peace-Sacramento, testified in opposition to the bill on 4/21/26 at the committee hearing. Mandel told the 12-member committee the motivation for introducing the bill was to “suppress a wave of peaceful protests” over synagogue and church sales “in clear violation of the 4th Geneva Convention- supreme law of the land, according to Article VI of our Constitution.”

The committee passed AB 2664 10-0, even though Chair Ash Kalra (D-San Jose) declined to recommend AB 2664, essentially abstaining, along with Assemblymember Isaac Bryan (D-Culver City). Kalra questioned the need for AB 2664 when the federal FACE Act already prohibits intimidation and harassment at places of worship.

AB 2664 has a long list of LJC sponsors; Dawn Addis (Morro Bay), Ben Allen (South Bay), Josh Becker (Atherton), Marc Berman (San Mateo), Jesse Gabriel (Encino), Matt Haney (San Francisco), Josh Lowenthal (Long Beach), Gail Pellerin (Santa Cruz), Susan Rubio (West Covina), and Rick Chavez Zbur (Santa Monica).

The full state assembly is expected to vote on AB 2664 by May 24, 2026.

At the assembly hearing the bill’s principal author Bauer-Kahan invoked the Holocaust to denounce “antisemitic” incidents outside LA’s Wilshire Blvd Temple, not once mentioning the December 2025 incidents involved protests over the synagogue hosting Israeli military contractor Elbit Systems, producer of Israel’s drones for surveillance and guided missiles. The same month synagogue protesters unfurled banners “Genociders Not Welcome,” the Palestinian Ministry of Health reported Israel had killed over 70,000 Palestinians, while the UN reported 38,000 were women and girls. Backing up Bauer-Kahan, Zbur said it was unrealistic for California’s 42,000 religious institutions to seek individual restraining orders ahead of a protest. Zbur’s critics argue AB 2664, instead, serves as a permanent injunction or prior restraint against lawful speech and assembly at all places of worship.

Under previous state appropriations, places of worship can tap into $160 million of state taxpayer dollars to hire private security to circle the perimeter of a religious institution and enforce, if signed into law, AB 2664’s ban on leafleting and sign-holding. Once the state legislature approves Jewish California’s request for another $100 million plus, there will be that much more in the kitty to hire MAGEN AM, an armed “security” company that recruits former IDF soldiers and boasts that “12 IDF and US veterans (are) employed as guards.”

Restricting dissent at CSU

This is not the first time members of the LJC have sponsored a bill to clamp down on dissent during Israel’s genocide in Gaza. Following the 2024 pro-Palestine encampments, the caucus sponsored SB 1287 (Glazer), a bill signed by Governor Newsom to pile on more time, place and manner restrictions on protests at Cal State University and California Community College campuses and to require training on violations of civility, lest a college demonstrator shout “From the River to the Sea.” The new restrictions at Cal State University campuses prohibit overnight camping, masking to conceal one’s identity and disrupting a speech with a “heckler’s veto.” Protests and rallies must have prior approval from the administration. Shout “Genocide!” too often and too loudly and get expelled.

AB 2615: Faux Clean-Up for Reviled AB 715

Assemblymembers Zbur and Addis, authors of the widely-opposed education bill AB 715, introduced AB 2615 after Governor Newson signed AB 715 into law with an attached signing statement to remind the bill’s authors of their commitment “to address urgent concerns about unintended consequences” expressed by “teachers, administrators, school board members, school staff, students and civil rights organizations.”

AB 715–oppposed by the 310,000 member California Teachers Association– establishes a governor-appointed antisemitism coordinator to monitor instruction, beginning with four-year olds in pre-Kindergarten and continuing through 12th grade, and to oversee teacher training on antisemitism. Here’s the catch: the instruction and training rely on the guidelines in the U.S.National Strategy to Counter Antisemitism that conflate criticism of Israel as a Jewish state with antisemitism.

“You all have blood on your hands.”

When the state senate passed AB 715 after midnight on September 13, 2025, the vote was 35-0, with five “not voting” (Choi, Cortese, Gonzalez, Valladares, Wahab). Minutes later, a grassroots activist shouted from the balcony to the lawmakers down below, “This is an Israeli genocide censorship bill and you all know it … so this bill is aiding and abetting a genocide that is ongoing. You all have blood on your hands.”

Rivas, Zbur, Addis vs. Garcia, teachers and school boards

Much to the dismay of rank and file teachers, Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas (D-Salinas) has refused to green-light the filing of a substantive clean up bill, AB 2159, authored by Assemblymember Robert Garcia (D-San Bernardino) that would remove all references to the misguided U.S. National Strategy to Counter Antisemitism and ensure the antisemitism coordinator is hired as a merit-based civil servant, not a governor appointee vulnerable to political influence from the California Israel lobby.

In contrast, there is little difference between the Zbur/Addis AB 715 and their faux clean-up AB 2615, except the latter requires a school district or county office of education to issue an investigation report within 20 days of a filed complaint before the state superintendent intervenes, whereas the earlier AB 715 allowed the state superintendent to intervene immediately. Additionally, AB 2615 requires the Superintendent “to ensure that local educational agencies omit all portions of the instructional materials found to have resulted in unlawful discrimination.” The meaning of “found to have resulted in” is so vague–absent a court ruling or even a state board of education decision–as to censor lessons on a live-streamed genocide.

References to the U.S. National Strategy to Counter Antisemitism that conflate criticism of Israel with antisemitism remain in Zbur’s AB 2615, along with the political appointment of the antisemitism coordinator in a new Office of Civil Rights. This office is conveniently housed in the California Government Operations Agency, the Governor’s pet department charged with deployment of AI throughout the state apparatus.

Those testifying in opposition to AB 2615 at the Assembly Education Committee hearing included representatives for the California Teachers Association, California Faculty Association, SEIU, ACLU, Jewish Voice for Peace-Sacramento and California School Board Association.

Assembly member Garcia, author of AB 2159, the real clean up bill–the one Rivas held back—told Education Chair Patel and committee colleagues, “There is mass murder taking place right now and what was frustrating about this process last year is that it was wrapped around protecting Jewish students when the real intent, and I’m stating the elephant in the room right here, was to silence folks … I know my colleagues go along to get along, but that’s not what I am here for. This does need to be amended and I urge a true fix.”

In 2025, the Arab American Anti-Discrimination Committee sought a preliminary injunction to block implementation of AB 715 on the basis of viewpoint discrimination and infringement on teacher and student academic freedom. Though a federal judge denied the injunction, saying only students–not K-12 teachers, enjoyed academic freedom, the case could still go forward as teachers face administrative harassment for lessons on Israel’s genocide and Palestinian demands for decolonization.

In the aftermath of AB 715’s passage and a complaint filed by Emet Legal Services, LAUSD has brought the hammer down on pro-Palestinian Ethnic Studies and history teachers at LAUSD’s business magnet. The District has ordered department-wide professional development on “access to instruction on fair, equitable, and neutral terms on the basis of protected characteristics.” In plain English, this translates to bland lessons devoid of terms such as “ethnic cleansing” “occupied territories” and “genocide” to instead use euphemisms like “war” “conflict” and “military activity” to describe Israel’s horror show in Gaza, where Israel’s blockade of sanitation equipment has led to a rat invasion.

SB 1387: Data on Jews as Ethnic Group

SB 1387 (Stern-Calabasas) would amend the state’s education, penal and unemployment codes to create a separate category, other than religious, for people of Jewish ethnicity or ancestry to check off on data collection forms. The data, offered on a voluntary basis, would be published on state agency portals without revealing the names of those who had identified as Jews of ethnic origin.

Privacy concerns in the age of MAGA

Joshua Goldstein, UC Berkeley professor of demography, told JWeekly one must consider if the benefits–collecting data to improve inequities–outweighs the risks of storing data identifying Jews.

Rob Lipton, a member of Jewish Voice for Peace-Bay Area, also expressed concern. “It's like slapping a yellow star on your shoulder,” he said, adding SB 1387 “is an example of Jewish people ghettoizing themselves.”

The danger in turning over the names and contact information of Jewish people may be reminiscent of Nazi Germany–but the peril is not confined to the Holocaust.

A federal judge recently ordered the University of Pennsylvania to cooperate with a Trump administration subpoena for the names of all Jewish university students and staff members. The Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) insisted it needs the names in order to investigate claims of antisemitism, but the university–heeding loud objections on campus– filed legal papers to oppose the order. For now, the judge has delayed compliance.

No leap of faith is necessary to imagine that a federal antisemitism investigation of Jews at the university could quickly go south with Jews punished for refusal to participate in a witch hunt or for participation in Palestinian-rights protests.

In the face of Trump’s masked ICE raids and transgender persecution, Stern’s SB 1387, co-sponsored by outgoing senator Ben Allen–candidate for state insurance commissioner and assemblymembers Berman and Lowenthal–raises questions about the motivation behind such fraught legislation. Perhaps the answer lies in the not-so-hidden agenda of Jewish California, whose members argue that Jews are a peoplehood or ethnic group with shared ancestry rooted in a biblical connection to the land of Israel. Consequently, it’s plausible that SB 1387 has little to do with addressing inequities and everything to do with conflating criticism of Israel with antisemitism to stifle outrage over Israel’s bombardment and starvation of Gaza, occupation of the West Bank and mass murder in Lebanon and Iran.

Ashkenazi, Sephardic, Mizrahi–not the same

Privacy concerns aside, not everyone agrees that Jews are all part of the same ethnic group.

Stern’s skeptics argue that Ashkenazi Jews from Eastern Europe are not of the same ethnicity as Sephardic Jews from Spain and Portugal or Mizrahi Jews from Africa or West Asia. Not if shared language and traditions define ethnicity. Ashkenazim speak Yiddish, a Germanic language written in Hebrew, while Sephardim speak Ladino or Judaeo-Spanish popularized by Jews expelled from Spain in 1492, and Mizrahi, who historically spoke in Arabic dialects, now communicate in modern Hebrew. During the Passover seder to mark the liberation of Jewish slaves from Egypt, Ashkenazi enjoy Gefilte fish; Sephardim savor spicy tomato-based fish; Mizrahim relish legumes and lamb.

Beyond differences in language and food, there are unique marriage customs, with a fasting Ashkenazi bride circling her groom seven times to symbolize protection of the family, while the Sephardic and Mizrahi brides celebrate henna parties promising good reproductive luck and protection from the envy of others. Finally, rejection of Zionism is most common among Ashkenazi Jews.
