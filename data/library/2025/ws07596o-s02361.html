California to Ban Ultra-Processed Foods in School Meals by 2035 - LAmag

Tara Nguyen

The bill is working to define UPFs and effectively remove them from schools.

Governor Newsom signed a bill Wednesday to phase out Ultra-Processed Foods (UPF) from school meals by July of 2035. The bill is first of its kind in the nation, requiring K-12 schools to ban unhealthy foods that serve over one billion California Children annually. 

The bill, first introduced by Jesse Gabriel (D-San Fernando Valley) AB1264, also known as the “Real Food, Healthy Kids Act,” builds on previous bills to ban artificial colorings and additives in California. The bill signed today asks the California Department of Public Health to identify foods and beverages that are high in saturated fat, sodium, and sugar. Along with any unnecessary or harmful food colorings, additives, and flavor enhancers, that may be linked to cancer, obesity, cardiovascular diseases, and behavioral issues. Once recognized as an “ultraprocessed food of concern,” they will be banned in school lunches within the next 10 years. 

Working on a timeline, the California Department of Public Health must identify the UPF’s and “restricted school foods” by June 1, 2025. 

“With Governor Newsom’s signature on AB 1264, California has taken a historic step toward protecting our children from harmful ultra-processed foods and chemical additives,” said Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel. “While Washington DC is paralyzed by inaction, California is once again leading the nation with a bipartisan, commonsense, science-based approach.” 

According to data by the CDC, Adults and Children over the age of 1 in the U.S. consumed more than half of their daily caloric intake from ultra-processed foods in 2021-2023. In the same article, “Ultra-processed foods can be very tasty and convenient, but high in calories and often lack important nutrients and fiber. They often contain little to no whole foods and are characterized using ingredients typically not found in home kitchens.” 

Those who opposed the bill argued that defining UPF is going to be difficult and may ban harmless ingredients with nutritional value, like canned fruits and vegetables. In the past, bills similar to this would be ridiculed by Republicans as government overreach. 

California has now beaten the “Make America Healthy Again” movement in efforts to define Ultra-Processed Foods. 

MAHA, spearheaded by United States Secretary of Health and Human Services, Robert F. Kennedy Jr., put out a report pledging to define Ultra-Processed foods but ultimately promised that the government would “continue efforts.” 

In a post on X, Governor Gavin Newsom said, “DC politicians can talk all day about ‘Making America Healthy Again,’ but we’ve been walking the walk on boosting nutrition and removing toxic additives and dyes for decades.”
