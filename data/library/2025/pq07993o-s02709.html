L.A. fires live updates: Winds to strengthen as blaze heads toward major highway; at least 16 dead Kelly Kasulis Cho; Pannett, Rachel . Kelly Kasulis Cho; Pannett, Rachel.

At least 16 people have been killed as roaring flames continue to ravage Southern California, with strong Santa Ana winds expected to flare-up through the middle of next week. But the winds are not expected to be as powerful as those that fueled the current destructive fires, meteorologist Rich Thompson said at a Palisades briefing Saturday evening. Four active fires in the Los Angeles region have burned more than 38,000 acres — an area bigger than San Francisco — according to Cal Fire, with flames claiming more than 12,000 structures and displacing tens of thousands. Malibu has been devastated: some areas have simultaneously gone without electricity, gas, phone service and cable, assembly member Jacqui Irwin (D-Thousand Oaks) said.

More than 14,000 personnel, including those from Canada and Mexico, are fighting the Palisades Fire, San Fernando Valley representative Jesse Gabriel said Saturday evening. Some have been working for 48 hours straight, officials said, with states such as Texas and Montana also pitching in.

The Palisades Fire was about 11 percent contained as of 8:30 p.m. local time and was burning near the Getty Museum and a mountain pass known for spectacular views. It is threatening to jump over a major highway, Interstate 405, into a more heavily populated area as firefighters struggle to contain the blaze. Meanwhile, the Eaton Fire near Pasadena was about 15 percent contained as of 8:30 p.m. local time.

"Due to the scope and magnitude of this incident, there's nothing I can say that is going to provide relief and comfort for those that have been impacted," Jim Hudson of Cal Fire said Saturday evening. "We have three priorities: Life, your property, and permanent control" of the flames, he added.
