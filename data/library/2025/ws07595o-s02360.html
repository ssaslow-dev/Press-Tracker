California phasing out UPFs in school meals

By Jeff Gelski

LOS ANGELES — California Governor Gavin Newsom on Oct. 8 signed Assembly Bill No. 1264 into a state law called the “Real Foods, Healthy Kids Act.” The law will define “ultra-processed foods” and “ultra-processed foods of concern” and phase them out of public schools for grades kindergarten through 12th grade over a 10-year period.

“California has never waited for Washington or anyone else to lead on kids’ health,” Newsom said at a signing ceremony in Los Angeles. “We’ve been out front for years, removing harmful additives and improving school nutrition. This first-in-the-nation law builds on that work to make sure every California student has access to healthy, delicious meals that help them thrive.”

Jesse Gabriel, a member of the California assembly, introduced the bill. It passed by a vote of 40-0 on Sept. 11 in the California state senate and by a vote of 79-1 on Sept. 12 in the California state assembly.

The 10-year timeline

The law defines “ultra-processed foods” for certain school-related purposes and requires California’s Department of Public Health to define “ultra-processed foods of concern” and “restricted school foods” on or before June 1, 2028. Schools no later than July 1, 2029, must begin phasing out restricted school foods and ultra-processed foods of concern.

Beginning July 1, 2032, the law prohibits a vendor from offering restricted school foods and ultra-processed foods of concern to schools. Beginning July 1, 2035, the law prohibits a school district, county superintendent of schools or charter school maintaining kindergarten or any of grades 1 to 12 from offering a nutritionally adequate breakfast or lunch that includes restricted school foods or ultra-processed foods of concern.

The Real Foods, Healthy Kid Act does not apply to the sale of school fund-raising events that take place away from school premises and that take place on school premises at least a half hour after the end of the school day.

“As California’s largest school meal vendor, Revolution Foods strongly supports the goal of this legislation to ensure healthier meals for children across the state, and we’d be happy to provide input on its implementation,” Revolution Foods said. “Since our founding, Revolution Foods’ mission as a B Corporation has been to craft meals with high-quality ingredients and avoid the additives this bill seeks to phase out, including artificial colors and sweeteners, flour improvers, and certain preservatives.”

The mean percentage of total calories consumed from ultra-processed foods among Americans age 1 and over was 55% from August 2021 to August 2023, according to a report issued in August 2025 by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Atlanta. The percentage, at 61.9%, was higher for Americans of the ages of 1 to 18 than the percentage, at 53%, for Americans age 19 and older. The CDC used the Nova classification system to define ultra-processed foods.

What are UPFs?

The Real Foods, Healthy Kids Act defines ultra-processed foods (UPFs) as any food or beverage that contains certain substances, with examples being surface-active agents; stabilizers and thickeners; propellants, aerating agents and gases; colors and coloring adjuncts; emulsifiers and emulsifier salts; flavoring agents and adjuvants; flavor enhancers; and non-nutritive sweeteners. The law also defines UPFs as foods and beverages that contain high amounts of saturated fat, sodium or added sugar.

The law states that the California Department of Public Health, when determining if items are ultra-processed foods of concern, should consider whether the substance or group of substances are banned or restricted in other state, federal or international jurisdictions; whether the products include or require a warning label in other state, federal or international jurisdictions; and whether a substance or group of substances, based on peer-reviewed scientific evidence, are linked to health harms or adverse health consequences, including cancer, cardiovascular disease, metabolic disease, development or behavioral issues, reproductive harm, obesity or type 2 diabetes.

Newsom in 2024 signed the School Food Safety Act, which prohibits the sale in California of school food containing petroleum-based synthetic dyes beginning Dec. 31, 2027. The dyes are Blue No. 1 and No. 2, Green No. 3, Red No. 40, and Yellow No. 5 and No. 6.

Some other states that have passed laws targeting food sold at schools include Louisiana, Arizona, West Virginia, Virginia and Utah. The US Department of Health and Human Services and the US Department of Agriculture are seeking input to establish a uniform definition of ultra-processed foods.
