The shadow campaign for attorney general kicks into gear

California Playbook (with help from Tyler Katzenberger)

[Excerpt saved: the newsletter's lead item on the attorney general race, which profiles Gabriel among potential candidates. The rest of the California Playbook covers unrelated items.]

FLYING TO THE RACES — Rob Bonta is inching closer to running for governor, and the shadow campaign to succeed him as California attorney general is in full swing.

Bonta has told supporters in recent days he’s strongly considering entering the governor’s contest. If he pivots, it would create a highly-competitive race to become the state’s top prosecutor — a coveted role held previously by the likes of Kamala Harris and Jerry Brown, especially as California helps lead the blue-state resistance to President Donald Trump.

What to know about the potential contenders for attorney general, provided Bonta aims higher:

Rick Zbur: Out of the gate, the assemblymember has been among the most aggressive in forming his campaign-in-waiting. Zbur has called supporters in recent days, including labor unions and LGBTQ+ and Jewish leaders, to ask if he would have their support. An ally granted anonymity to discuss Zbur’s efforts said he is “actively planning” a potential campaign. Zbur would likely be the most progressive major contender in the race, at least among the current hypothetical field. Similar to Bonta, Zbur has close ties to unions and other progressive groups. He would, if elected, be California’s first openly LGBTQ+ attorney general — a factor that could help him tap into a national fundraising network. Before Zbur joined the Legislature, he led Equality California and worked as an environmental attorney.

Jesse Gabriel: The assemblymember’s name has been popping up increasingly in conversations about the race, and his entrance could make for an intrahouse fight with Zbur. Both are allies of Speaker Robert Rivas and Democrats from the Los Angeles area, so there’s a risk of splitting an overlapping base if both get in. Gabriel could benefit from his chairship of the Assembly Budget Committee, a powerful panel that is historically helpful for fundraising. An ally for the attorney said he has more than $2.9 million in his legislative campaign account, which is more than any other state legislator.

Brooke Jenkins: Another contender generating buzz is San Francisco’s district attorney. Jenkins, if she runs, would be one of the most moderate major Democrats in the race. She took the helm of the DA’s office after voters in the city ousted progressive Chesa Boudin in 2022, largely over frustrations with the city’s problems with drug addiction and property crime. Jenkins has charted a more pro-police direction and has aggressively targeted drug dealers. Like many cities, San Francisco has seen its crime rate plummet in recent years. That could offer Jenkins a compelling resume to run on, and wealthy tech donors from the Bay Area would likely spend heavily to advance her tougher approach to crime. At the same time, Jenkins has been outspoken in recent months about her support for San Francisco’s large immigrant communities and opposition to Trump’s immigration raids. An ally said Jenkins is looking at the potential race and receiving calls from supporters nudging her in that direction.

Tom Umberg: The term-limited state senator recently announced a run for the Board of Equalization, but a chance to be the state’s top cop could be too attractive to pass up. Umberg has focused much of his legislative career on criminal justice issues, and was earlier in his career a federal prosecutor. His moderate profile sets him up to run to the right of his fellow Democrats, and with three stints in the Legislature stretching back to the 1990s, he could tap into deep Sacramento roots.

Nathan Hochman: The Los Angeles district attorney came up short when he challenged Bonta as a Republican in 2022. But since then, he has shed his GOP affiliation and ousted progressive District Attorney George Gascón in a blue county. Hochman is probably the only would-be candidate on this list who could convincingly out-mod Umberg, and his running would provide a test of whether voters have moved ideologically since passing the tough-on-crime Prop 36.

Lorena Gonzalez: Her name has been in the rumor mill and Gonzalez, head of the state Labor Federation and a lawyer, would likely be a formidable contender. But Gonzalez told Playbook that she is “absolutely not” considering the AG’s race. Her not running would free up labor unions to gravitate toward another potential candidate, possibly Zbur or Gabriel.
