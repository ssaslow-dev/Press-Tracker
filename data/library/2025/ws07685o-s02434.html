Growers, groups skeptical of ‘ultra-processed foods’ bill

Tim Hearden

Tim Hearden,Senior Editor, Western Farm Press

August 27, 2025

Clovis, Calif., farmer Pat Ricchiuti, who grows oil olives and other commodities, is among those who are concerned about a bill in the California Legislature that would task scientists with defining "ultra-processed foods" and banning them from school menus.Tim Hearden

California state lawmakers are taking aim at harmful food ingredients on school menus, but the state’s producers of extra-virgin olive oil are casting a wary eye at the legislation.

Assembly Bill 1265 by Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, would task state scientists – working in cooperation with experts from the University of California – with identifying “particularly harmful” ultra-processed foods and would phase these foods out from school meals by 2032, the author explains.

The bill seeks to target food ingredients that researchers have linked to cancer, cardiovascular disease, metabolic disorders, neurological or behavioral issues, and other health harms, Gabriel’s office noted in a release. AB 1264 is coauthored by a diverse group of legislators, including Assembly Republican leader James Gallagher (R-East Nicolaus) and Progressive Caucus chair Alex Lee (D-San Jose).

But one group raising red flags is the American Olive Oil Producers Association, whose president and CEO, Kimberly Houlding, said the legislation’s original language would have categorized extra-virgin olive oil as an ultra-processed food.

“Clearly we’re not,” Houlding told Farm Press. “We’re very much a minimally processed food, so that was concerning,” she added, particularly considering “all of the health benefits of extra-virgin olive oil.”

Clovis, Calif., farmer Pat Ricchiuti, who produces oil olives as well as other commodities, echoes Houlding’s sentiments. “It’s not processed any differently than mama used to do it in the kitchen,” he told Farm Press.

The bill, which passed the Assembly in June and is now in the Senate Appropriations Committee, has been amended several times as it has advanced. For example, language was added requiring a petition process for exemption of a food or ingredient from the designation.

I think there has definitely been some improvements in the language,” Houlding said. “There have been some updates to the language I think that have hopefully addressed some of the concerns we had.”

For instance, the use of nitrogen gas to take air out of tanks, preventing oxidation and contamination of stored oil, would have caused the oil to be classified as ultra-processed, she said.

Federal definition

The state bill comes as three federal agencies – the USDA, Food and Drug Administration and Health and Human Services – are requesting information to establish a federally recognized, uniform definition for ultra-processed foods. The agencies call the effort a critical step in providing increased transparency to consumers about the foods they eat. Comments will be taken through Sept. 23.

“It’s something that we’re actively working on right now with our member companies and our technical advisers, and we’ll be putting together some comments,” Houlding said. “If it was similar to what they’ve talked about before, I think that extra-virgin olive oil would safely not be categorized as an ultra-processed food.”

California’s bill is the latest by Gabriel to promote food safety. He has authored past legislation to restrict the sale of certain food dyes and vegetable oils in schools. His current bill faced early opposition from the Consumer Brands Association, which argued it would place significant financial and administrative burdens on school districts and the state Department of Education and result in fewer products available, increasing costs and reducing choices.

Houlding’s olive oil organization is working with the California League of Food Producers and other groups to soften the bill’s impact on producers.

“I know California has a desire to move on this and perhaps even move ahead of the federal government,” Houlding said.
