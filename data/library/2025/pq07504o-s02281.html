Mayor Karen Bass Launches Reelection Campaign with Broad Coalition Rally at LATTC

By Lila Brown

Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass officially launched her reelection campaign on December 13th with a rally that brought together Angelenos from across the city, alongside leaders from labor, business, faith, and government, signaling the breadth of her political coalition as she seeks a second term.

Held at Los Angeles Trade-Technical College's Culinary Arts Event Space, the rally drew hundreds of supporters. The atmosphere was celebratory but resolute, underscoring what speakers described as a pivotal moment for Los Angeles amid national political headwinds.

Mayor Bass framed her reelection bid around what she called tangible progress during her first term, emphasizing public safety, housing, affordability, climate action, and the protection of immigrant communities.

"We are told all the time that labor can't work with business, that community can't work with government," Bass said. "But what you see here today is Los Angeles. We come together because we believe in justice."

Central to the mayor's remarks was what her administration describes as the first sustained reduction in street homelessness in decades-an achievement that runs counter to national trends. Bass highlighted the city's emergency declaration on homelessness, the Inside Safe initiative, and the streamlining of more than 40,000 housing units to address affordability.

She also pointed to the creation of the Office of Community Safety, noting that homicide totals in 2025 are trending toward a decades-long low, and emphasized historic climate reforms, including the official elimination of coal usage in the City of Los Angeles.

"This city has had a tough year," Bass said. "But we stood together in tragedy, we stood together to protect our communities, and we are not done."

The rally featured a notable show of unity across sectors.

Los Angeles County Federation of Labor President Yvonne Wheeler formally announced the federation's endorsement of Mayor Bass, representing more than 800,000 union members across the county. Joined by SEIU 721 President David Green, labor leaders praised the mayor's efforts to protect city jobs, expand affordable housing, and champion living wages. "Karen Bass has always been a fierce advocate for working people," Wheeler said. "She understands a simple but powerful truth - that a job must be a good job, one that delivers wages and benefits so families don't just survive, but thrive. That's why the Los Angeles labor movement is proud to stand united in endorsing Mayor Karen Bass for reelection." "One layoff is one too many," Green said, crediting the mayor's hands-on leadership with reducing the threat of 1,600 layoffs to zero. "Now it's our turn to stand up for her."

From the business community, Stuart Waldman, President of the Valley Industry & Commerce Association (VICA), spoke alongside entrepreneur Karim Webb, CityView CEO Sean Burton, and business leaders George Pla, Annika Yeo, and Priscilla Chavez, highlighting the mayor's engagement with job creation and economic recovery across Los Angeles. Immigrant rights were front and center as Angelica Salas, President of CHIRLA Action Fund, addressed the crowd alongside leaders from diverse immigrant communities, commending Bass for what she described as decisive action in resisting federal policies that target immigrants.

Mayor Bass was joined on stage by a wide array of elected officials, including Congresswoman Sydney Kamlager-Dove, State Senators Maria Elena Durazo and Lola SmallwoodCuevas, Assemblymembers Jesse Gabriel, Mike Fong, and Isaac Bryan, and Los Angeles City Council President Marqueece Harris-Dawson, along with several councilmembers and school board leaders. Former state and city officials were also in attendance.

Councilmember Hugo Soto-Martinez pointed to Inside Safe operations in Hollywood, citing an independent RAND Corporation study that found homelessness reduced by 50% in the area. "That's leadership," he said. "That's the mayor's leadership."

Faith leaders representing multiple traditions led interfaith prayers, including Rev. Dr. Timothy Tyler of First AME Church and Rabbi Tamara Eskenazi of Hebrew Union College, reinforcing the moral framing of the mayor's campaign message. The program was moderated by Emmy-nominated actress, writer, and producer Yvette Nicole Brown, who described Mayor Bass as a "humble servant leader" whose career-from Speaker of the California Assembly to the U.S. Congress and now City Hall-has been defined by service.

"This 15 the kickoff of her campaign to serve a second term as Mayor of Los Angeles," Brown told the crowd. "And we're here at the very beginning."

Following the rally, SEIU organizer Maurice Littles shared his perspective on the mayor's leadership. "I've seen the difference," Littles said. "She's been on the front lines for years, even before she was mayor. Through all the attacks-from Washington, [D.C.] and elsewhere she's held her ground and delivered."

Littles also emphasized the significance of Bass's leadership as a Black woman. "She stands tall," he said. "People know her not just by her words, but by her deeds."

Jordan "TJ" Karter, a sound engineer who plans to enroll next semester in Los Angeles Trade-Technical College's Culinary Arts program, attended the rally at the invitation of his friend Rosco Hines, a colleague of Mayor Bass. Karter said he was encouraged by the mayor's remarks and her focus on the city's most pressing challenges.

"I really enjoyed the mayor's speech and think she's doing a great job overall," Karter said. "I'd love to see her continue working with Governor Gavin Newsom on immigration and homelessness."

In closing, Mayor Bass acknowledged the political fight ahead, characterizing the election as a choice between unity and division, and between working people and what she repeatedly referred to as the "billionaire class."

"Los Angeles is not for sale," she told supporters. "And I'm not finished."

As the 2026 mayoral race officially begins, Saturday's rally made clear that Mayor Karen Bass is entering the campaign with a coalition that spans labor halls, boardrooms, community organizations, and faith institutions-an alliance she will rely on as she seeks to extend her tenure at City Hall.
