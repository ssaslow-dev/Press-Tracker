Legislative Delta Caucus Urges CA Legislature to Reject Newsom Proposal to Fast-Track Delta Tunnel

Governor Gavin Newsom has really amped up his attack on Tribes, fishing communities, Delta residents, the Bay-Delta ecosystem, Southern California water ratepayers and the people of California by trying to fast-track his plan to build the salmon-killing Delta Tunnel through the State Legislature.

On Thursday, the Legislative Delta Caucus urged the leaders of the California Senate and Assembly to reject Gov. Gavin Newsom’s proposal to fast-track the Delta Tunnel Project in the 2025-26 state budget.

Today, May 20, the Assembly Budget Subcommittee 4 on Climate Crisis, Resources, Energy and Transportation will meet at 9:30am in State Capitol Room 437.

In a letter to Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas, Senate President Pro-Tempore Mike McGuire, Assembly Budget Chair Jesse Gabriel, and Senate Budget Chair Scott Wiener, along with Gov. Newsom, all of the Delta Caucus’ 15 members noted that the costly and destructive Delta Tunnel Project “is opposed by every city and county affected by it.”

“The bipartisan Delta Caucus is unanimous in strong opposition to the governor’s proposal to fast-track the Delta Tunnel, a $20 billion unaffordable project to be paid by ratepayers who are already struggling with the high cost of living in California. This project will destroy prime farmland, wreak havoc on the Delta region for a generation, and fail to solve the state’s water issues,” said Delta Caucus co-chairs, Senator Jerry McNerney, D-Pleasanton, and Assemblywoman Lori D. Wilson, D-Suisun City. “We call on the Legislature to quickly reject the governor’s proposal. California should instead pursue less costly and destructive alternatives, including fortifying Delta levees and increasing water recycling and groundwater storage.”

A copy of the Delta Caucus’ letter is here.

The Delta Caucus is a bipartisan group of legislators who represent counties of the Sacramento-San Joaquin River Delta. The caucus members are Senator McNerney (D) (co-chair), Assemblywoman Wilson (D) (co-chair), Senator Roger Niello (R) (co-vice chair), Assemblymember Heath Flora (R) (co-vice chair), Assembly Majority Leader Cecilia Aguiar-Curry (D), Senator Jesse Arreguín (D), Senator Angelique Ashby (D), Assemblymember Anamarie Ávila Farías (D), Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D), Senator Christopher Cabaldon (D), Senator Tim Grayson (D), Assemblymember Josh Hoover (R), Assemblymember Maggy Krell (D), Assemblymember Stephanie Nguyen (D), and Assemblymember Rhodesia Ransom (D).

We need your presence and voice to help stop this dangerous initiative.

Now more than ever, it is crucial for anyone connected to the Bay-Delta estuary to speak out. The Governor is attempting to advance the Delta Tunnel under the guise of the budget process, ignoring legal requirements and disregarding public input. We must stand together to protect our ecosystems and communities.

Meanwhile, yesterday dozens of Delta residents, Tribal members, recreational and commercial fishermen, environmental justice advocates, conservations testified against the Delta Tunnel before the State Water Resources Control Board. I listened to the hearing for two hours and was planning to testify before the board, but I had to leave to do a medical procedure (a bone scan). Unfortunately, when they called my name, I wasn’t there. Anyway, here is the written testimony that I am submitting:

My name is Dan Bacher. I’m an independent journalist and researcher focusing on fish, environmental and social justice and water equity. I strongly urge the State Water Resources Control Board to vote against the water rights permit filed by the Department of Water Resources to build the Delta Tunnel.

We are now witnessing the worst-ever collapse of Central Valley salmon and Bay-Delta fish populations in the history of the state, as I have documented in article after article. Commercial salmon fishing is closed for the third year in a row, due to the collapse of Sacramento River and Klamath River fall-run Chinook populations.

The winter and spring Chinook runs of the Sacramento River have also been hammered by massive water diversions and water exports from the Delta to corporate agribusiness.

Between 1996-2005 the average return for fall-run Chinook on the mainstem Sacramento River was 79,841 spawning salmon. In 2023 that number fell drastically to only 3,560 salmon – a 95% decline, according to an analysis by the Golden State Salmon Association.

Spring-run Chinook salmon have also experienced a dramatic 95% decline due to a lack of cold water flows in Central Valley salmon rivers. The average wild and hatchery spring-run return plummeted from 28,238 fish in 2021 to just 1,231 salmon in 2023.

The winter run Chinook salmon have declined from 117,000 spawners returning to the Sacramento River in 1969, even after Shasta Dam was built, to only 789 adults and 578 jacks (two-year-olds) in 2024.

And the Delta’s once abundant pelagic (open water) fish species have declined by over 95 percent. The Delta smelt. once the most abundant fish on the entire Delta, has not been found in the last seven CDFW fall surveys of the Delta – while the longfin smelt and other pelagic fish species continue in free fall.

But rather than do the right thing and correct the untenable situation with salmon and other fish, the Governor is embarked on a mad plan to make things even worse. He is pushing forward with the Delta Tunnel and Sites Reservoir projects, along with the voluntary agreements, that will seal the doom of salmon populations and the Bay-Delta Ecosystem.

Despite the Department of Water Resources claim that the Delta Tunnel will not increase water exports from the Delta, testimony of DWR engineer Amardeep Singh reveals that the Delta Conveyance Project (DCP) will increase water deliveries from the Delta by 22%.

“DCP operation will not decrease water supply for Central Valley Project (CVP) contractors and will increase water supply for SWP Table A contractors by 22 percent,” he states on page 2 of his testimony.

Moreover, during drought periods when fish are already strained by low flows and high temperatures, the DCP would increase deliveries by 24%: static1.squarespace.com/…

The Delta Tunnel would be used in conjunction with the proposed Sites Reservoir to export even more water out of the Sacramento River when what the ecosystem needs is more water for salmon and fish — and not more water diverted to agribusiness and Southern California water agencies.

The State Water Board must show the courage to stop, once and for all, the Delta Tunnel Project — and force state officials to obey a plethora of state and federal laws protecting the ecosystem by actually providing the long-needed water for the salmon and other fish.
