In Focus SoCal: California budget; HHAP funding

In Focus Staff

LOS ANGELES — The new California budget is in play, and this year’s $321 billion spending plan scales back a number of progressive priorities, including a landmark health care expansion for low-income undocumented people in order to close a $12 billion deficit.  

On this week’s “In Focus SoCal,” host Tanya McRae takes a closer look at the new California budget.

McRae sits down with Assemblyman Jesse Gabriel, chair of the Assembly Budget Committee, about criticism that the budget doesn’t consider future deficits. 

“My response is that I think we took that issue very seriously. We said throughout the budget process that we had two goals,” Gabriel said. "It was to create a budget that was rooted in compassion and fiscal responsibility. And so we understood that this is a very challenging moment for a lot of folks in the state of California.”

Riverside Mayor Patricia Lock Dawson discusses the elimination of HHAP funding in this year’s budget and how it will impact efforts around California to get more homeless people into permanent housing. 

“When you look at California versus the rest of the United States, our homeless population growth slowed over the last year at only 3%, which is a lot less than it was. And it is a lot less than what the growth is nationwide,” Lock Dawson said. “So I think that speaks to the fact that these funds have been working. Our strategies have been working. And we really feel that the time to not fund is not now because it will slow progress.”
