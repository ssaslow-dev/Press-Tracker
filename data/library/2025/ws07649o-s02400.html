Newsom signs bill expanding CA grant program to outdoor LGBTQ events

Governor Gavin Newsom signed Assembly Bill 1836 on September 25, which allows nonprofits, including LGBTQ organizations, to apply for security funds for outdoor offsite events like Pride festivals.

Photo: Courtesy Governor’s Office

Under legislation Governor Gavin Newsom signed Friday, outdoor LGBTQ community events like Pride festivals will now have access to a state program that provides funding to help pay for security measures. Safety concerns for such public gatherings have increased over the years due to the rise seen in anti-LGBTQ hate.

 Gay Assemblymember Chris Ward (D-San Diego) and Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Encino) co-authored Assembly Bill 1836 to allow nonprofits that host outdoor events for their local LGBTQ community to seek upward of $500,000 for security measures via the state’s Nonprofit Security Grant Program. While it has been covering events held at nonprofit facilities, the program did not accept applications for offsite events put on by such entities.

 That will change due to Newsom’s enacting AB 1836. Gabriel, who chairs the Assembly Budget Committee, was on hand for the September 25 signing ceremony with Newsom.

 “California has built the most robust state-level Nonprofit Security Grant Program in the nation, providing critical security funding to more than 1,600 houses of worship, LGBTQ+ community centers, reproductive health clinics, and other nonprofits at risk of hate-motivated violence. This year, a diverse interfaith coalition came together to secure $80 million in ongoing annual funding to permanently sustain this lifesaving program,” stated Gabriel, who also chairs the California Legislative Jewish Caucus. “AB 1836 builds on that historic milestone by ensuring that security funding is also available for offsite events like Pride celebrations, marches, and cultural and religious festivals. It sends a clear message that Californians of all faiths and backgrounds should be able to gather, worship, and celebrate without fear.”

 He added that, “Under Governor Newsom’s leadership, California has been at the forefront of the fight against hate, and we are deeply grateful to him for signing this important new law.”

 Newsom signed AB 1836 along with AB 2664 authored by Assemblymember Rebecca Bauer-Kahan (D-Orinda) at Wilshire Boulevard Temple in Los Angeles’ Koreatown. The governor’s office noted it was vandalized last December by protesters who had entered the building and disrupted “a peaceful gathering by shouting antisemitic insults and damaging the interior of the temple.” According to local media reports, police arrested two people on vandalism and battery charges.

 AB 2664 makes it unlawful for someone within a 100 foot radius of an access point to a place of religious worship to “intentionally approach another person seeking to enter or exit a place of religious worship within 8 feet of that person to either pass a leaflet or handbill to, display a sign to, or engage in oral protest or education or to harass, obstruct, threaten, or intimidate the person or occupant.”

 “Every Californian should be able to walk into a synagogue, church, mosque, or other house of worship without fear,” stated Newsom. “California is continuing to protect these organizations and the people they serve, and we’re strengthening that work from the front door to expanding security resources to community events statewide.”

 Noting she is a Jewish woman and a mother, and that one in four Jewish Americans don’t feel safe attending Jewish institutions, Bauer-Kahan stated, “For too long, too many people have been asked to accept fear as part of practicing their faith.” She thanked Newsom “for signing AB 2664, which says we cannot accept that as normal, and it should never become the norm. This bill creates a safe bubble zone around houses of worship so people of all faiths can worship with the dignity, peace, and security they deserve."

 In May of this year state leaders announced that $80 million had been awarded to 343 nonprofit organizations, 228 of which are faith-based groups, through the California Nonprofit Security Grant Program. Among the fiscal year 2025 grantees was San Francisco-based Parivar Bay Area, the nation’s first and only Hijra, Kinnar and Global South transgender immigrant-led organization, which received a $60,800 grant.

The San Francisco Lesbian Gay Bisexual Transgender Community Center received $208,253 under the program last fiscal year, as did the San Diego Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, and Transgender Community Center, which was awarded $250,000. In the nearby city of Oceanside, the North County LGBTQ Resource Center received a $215,985 grant.

 Told of the expansion of the security grant program by the Bay Area Reporter, San Francisco LGBT Pride Celebration Committee Executive Director Suzanne Ford said she would work with the offices of gay state Senator Scott Wiener (D-San Francisco) and Assemblymember Matt Haney (D-San Francisco) on applying for funding via the state program. She noted that the security for the city’s two-day festival and parade held on the last weekend of June, said to attract 1 million attendees and is the largest Pride event on the West Coast, cost $350,000.

 “I am just elated they changed the rules and made us eligible. I will be reaching out to state Senator Wiener’s office and state Assemblyman Haney’s office to help us secure some funds,” said Ford, a transgender woman who has overseen the Pride celebration since 2022. “The security costs coming back from the (COVID) pandemic have just been astronomical.”

 Newsom has until September 30 to either sign into law or veto legislation sent to him this legislative session. Still before him are a number of bills related to LGBTQ health issues and youth.

 Over the summer he signed into law legislation to make permanent an online suicide prevention training program the state had piloted for middle through high school students, as the B.A.R. had reported. Last Sunday, he vetoed Bauer-Kahan’s bill aimed at protecting providers of gender-affirming care from being extradited to face charges for doing so by another state, citing concerns over executive authority for jettisoning it.
