California moves to ban ultra-processed foods in school lunches

“We don’t love our kids any less than they do in Europe, or Asia, or Latin America,” said Asm. Jesse Gabriel at a bill signing event.

By Jackson Ellison
Published 2:55 PM PDT October 9, 2025

SACRAMENTO, Calif. — California is phasing out ultra-processed foods in school lunches at public schools.

Assembly Bill 1264, authored by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel (D-Los Angeles), defines “particularly harmful ultra-processed foods” as those determined by experts based on scientific research, as they have been linked to causing adverse health effects.

Foods containing fruits, vegetables, dairy, protein or whole grains are all allowed.

"This is California, a third of the nation's vegetables come from our state, three-quarters of fruits and nuts come from the state of California, we're so proximate to this bounty," Newsom said.

Schools would need to begin phasing out the offering of particularly harmful ultra-processed foods by 2028, with a complete ban on vendors providing the foods to schools by 2032. Any producer of products that are deemed "particularly harmful" would be able to petition the state to remain in schools.

Gabriel, who has made it a mission to phase out harmful foods in schools, says California is finally catching up to the rest of the world.

“We don’t love our kids any less than they do in Europe, or Asia, or Latin America,” Gabriel said at a bill signing event. “So why would we offer them less protection from harmful products linked to cancer, cardiovascular disease and reproductive harm?”

The bill has received support from medical professionals, such as those from the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), who say ultra-processed foods sold in schools have contributed to chronic health conditions like obesity and type 2 diabetes in children.

”These aren’t just distant threats, they are showing up in exam rooms, they’re showing up in my exam room," said Dr. Ravinder Khaira with the AAP, told ABC10 in September.
