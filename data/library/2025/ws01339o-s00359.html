EL CENTRO- Jewish American Heritage Month, celebrated each May, honors the contributions and culture of Jewish Americans throughout history. It was first proclaimed by President George W. Bush in 2006, following resolutions passed by Congress.

The month offers an opportunity to celebrate Jewish achievements in areas like arts, politics, civil rights, and business, and educate others about Jewish culture, history, and traditions, and reflect on challenges Jewish communities have faced, including antisemitism, and affirm a commitment to tolerance and diversity.

Nowhere are those contributions more deeply etched than in the Golden State, where Jewish pioneers, activists, artists, and leaders have helped shape California’s cultural, political, and economic landscape.

From the earliest days of the Gold Rush to today’s innovation hubs, Jewish Californians have played a pivotal role in building and enriching the state. Some arrived as immigrants fleeing persecution in Europe; others were born into American families rooted in Jewish traditions. All brought with them a sense of resilience, community, and purpose that left a lasting imprint.

Gold Rush Roots and Civic Leadership

The Jewish presence in California began to grow significantly during the Gold Rush of the 1850s. Like many others, Jews were drawn to California with the promise of wealth and the chance to build a new life. San Francisco became a central hub, with Jewish-owned businesses springing up and synagogues like Congregation Emanu-El being founded—one of the oldest in the American West. The temple was founded in 1850 by a small group of mostly German Jews, many from Bavaria, who were traders and merchants.

Levi Strauss, a Bavarian Jewish immigrant, forever changed American fashion and frontier life by patenting blue jeans in San Francisco in 1873. Strauss immigrated to San Francisco in 1853 during the Gold Rush. Today, Levi’s remains a global brand and symbol of California’s entrepreneurial spirit.

Advocates for Justice

California’s Jewish community has also been at the forefront of civil rights and social justice. Legendary attorney and Supreme Court Justice Louis Brandeis inspired generations of Jewish legal minds, many of whom made their mark in California courts. Raised in Kentucky by Jewish immigrant parents, Brandeis was appointed to the Unites States Supreme Court in 1916. Activists such as Rabbi Abraham Joshua Heschel, who marched with Martin Luther King Jr., was instrumental in fostering interfaith dialogues. During the Vatican II council, he worked towards reforming anti-Semitic church doctrine.

Innovation and Philanthropy

In Silicon Valley and across the tech industry, Jewish entrepreneurs have helped drive California’s innovation economy. From Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison to Google’s Sergey Brin, these leaders embody a spirit of curiosity and creativity.

Meanwhile, Jewish philanthropic institutions based in California support causes ranging from Holocaust education to hunger relief, often rooted in the Jewish concept of tikkun olam—repairing the world.

Preserving and Celebrating Culture

Institutions like the Skirball Cultural Center in Los Angeles and the Contemporary Jewish Museum in San Francisco preserve and showcase Jewish heritage for future generations. These centers not only explore Jewish history but also highlight the intersections of Jewish identity with American pluralism.

The Mobile Museums of Tolerance recently visited El Centro, bringing high school students an immersive, interactive educational experience on civil rights issues.

Hollywood’s Jewish Architects and Cultural Guardians

Hollywood would not be what it is today without the vision of Jewish immigrants who turned California’s citrus groves into the movie capital of the world. In the early 20th century, many Eastern European Jews, often excluded from other industries due to antisemitism, found opportunity in the then-nascent film business. They built not only studios but a distinctly American art form that shaped global culture.

Carl Laemmle, the German-Jewish founder of Universal Pictures, was known not only for bringing monsters like Dracula and Frankenstein to the big screen but for personally helping hundreds of Jews escape Nazi Germany. The Warner brothers, Polish-Jewish immigrants, used their studio to expose Nazi atrocities in films like Confessions of a Nazi Spy (1939), well before American intervention in WWII. Russian born Louis B. Mayer, the powerful head of MGM, helped turn Hollywood into a myth-making machine that reflected—and created—American ideals. Mayer helped create the “Hollywood star” system at MGM, launching some of the biggest careers on the silver screen like Greta Garbo, Rudolph Valentino, and Clark Gable.

These moguls didn’t just create films; they crafted narratives of hope, heroism, and justice that resonated with both Jewish values and universal themes. Many of them used their wealth and influence to support Jewish causes, combat antisemitism, and contribute to war relief efforts.

Today, their legacy continues through modern Jewish filmmakers like Steven Spielberg, whose USC Shoah Foundation, headquartered in Los Angeles, preserves Holocaust survivor testimonies and educates future generations.

Local Leaders and Political Trailblazers

Jewish Californians have long played an influential role in state and local politics, from city halls to the U.S. Capitol.

Imperial Valley’s Assistant Public Defender Daniel Rosenberg spoke to Imperial Valley Press about the importance of preserving Jewish History.

“The story of Jewish survival is so extraordinary and unpredictable that it challenges the imagination. It defies logic and statistical probability. Over three thousand years ago we became a nation. We have been dispersed across the world and have survived millennia of attacks against both our physical and spiritual existence. Some believe that our historical and national homeland of Israel is a key element to our survival. But while we will never relinquish our eternal claim to it, the reality is we were exiled from our land thousands of years ago and there are more Jews scattered across the world than there are currently living in Israel.”

Rosenberg went on to emphasize the cultural richness and resilience of Jewish communities across generations.

“ We are diverse in culture, environmental factors, and language. While all my grandparents were born here in the United States, in the past 500 years my ancestors have lived in Russia, Spain, Belarus, Turkey, Poland, and Lithuania. The one and only feature entirely common for our people and the key to our survival is our Torah. The Torah is a clearly defined value system that has been transmitted from generation to generation.”

“I, like the last three generations of my family, are not only Jewish but American. Numerous members of my family have served in the United States military and photographs of them in their uniforms are displayed prominently in our home. The only flag that has ever flown in front of our house is “the Star-Spangled Banner”. Being a Jewish American is symbiotic, and we are the beneficiaries of the freedoms that make this not only the country that I live in but the country I love.”

Rosenberg’s personal story reflects a broader truth about Jewish contributions throughout history, helping shape policies, foster community, and uphold democratic values.

One of the most prominent Jewish political figures in California was Dianne Feinstein, born in San Francisco. She made history as the first Jewish woman elected to the U.S. Senate. Before that, she became mayor of San Francisco in 1978 after the assassination of Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk. Feinstein's political career was marked by efforts in gun control, environmental protection, and national security, and she was often seen as a centrist voice during turbulent times.

Meanwhile, in Southern California, Jewish leadership has also been visible. Los Angeles, home to one of the largest Jewish populations outside of Israel, saw Eric Garcetti, the first elected Jewish mayor who served from 2013 to 2022. Garcetti’s paternal grandfather was a Mexican immigrant and maternal grandparents were Russian-Jews.

Other notable figures include Rep. Adam Schiff (D-Burbank), who gained national attention during impeachment proceedings and has long advocated for a strong U.S.-Israel relationship, pledging his support for Israel following October 7 attacks by Hamas.

California State Senator Ben Allen, representing parts of Los Angeles, is another rising Jewish political leader focusing on education, sustainability, and civic engagement. Allen is a member of the California Legislative Jewish Caucus.

The California Legislative Jewish Caucus is a group within the California State Legislature composed of Jewish members (and some associate members) who work to advance the Jewish community's priorities and advocate for vulnerable Californians of all backgrounds. The caucus aims to promote justice, equality, and progress within the state legislature. One of the goals and missions of the caucus is to advocate for the Jewish community’s concerns and safety. The current co-chairs are Scott Wiener and Jesse Gabriel.

Beyond elected office, Jewish leaders have been deeply involved in community organizing and interfaith coalitions across the state. Organizations like the Jewish Public Affairs Committee of California (JPAC) represent Jewish interests in Sacramento, advocating for issues ranging from antisemitism prevention to affordable housing.

Harvey Milk: A Jewish Trailblazer for LGBTQ+ Rights

A pivotal public servant, Harvey Milk was both proudly gay and proudly Jewish. Born in New York to Lithuanian Jewish immigrants, In 1977, Milk made history when he was elected to the San Francisco Board of Supervisors, becoming one of the first openly gay elected officials in the United States. His campaign and tenure emphasized coalition-building, minority rights, and inclusive governance. Milk’s Jewish identity, though sometimes overlooked, informed his moral framework and sense of justice. He often drew parallels between Jewish suffering and the struggles of LGBTQ+ people.

Milk's assassination in 1978, alongside Mayor George Moscone, shocked the nation and galvanized a movement. His legacy continues through public memorials, biographies, and films—including the Academy Award-winning Milk (2008). In 2009, President Barack Obama posthumously awarded him the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Today, Harvey Milk’s name adorns schools, public offices, and streets throughout California. His story serves as a powerful reminder of the role Jewish Californians have played in the fight for equality—not just for themselves, but for all marginalized communities.
