The lawmakers bunking together in Sacramento

By LINDSEY HOLDEN

Programming note: We’ll be off Monday, back in your inboxes on Tuesday.

BETTER TOGETHER: Some California lawmakers in search of capital city accommodations may end up feeling like they’re back in college.

Since most legislators’ districts aren’t close to Sacramento, they need someplace to crash when they’re in session. Although there’s a sizable crew of hotel dwellers — dubbed by some the “Sheraton caucus” — a handful of lawmakers opt to rent housing with their colleagues in lieu of living out of suitcases.

Driven by this newsletter writer’s curiosity about the phenomenon of powerful state figures living in collegiate-like settings, Playbook today brings you a semi-comprehensive investigation into the practice. We uncovered one lawmaker’s infatuation with vegetables, another’s penchant for hosting parties and the truth about what it’s like to live with a “neat freak.”

It’s been nearly a decade since lawmakers got a very public lesson in being careful who you room with. But the nine legislators Playbook interviewed all said having roommates made an isolating and challenging job more bearable and gave them a built-in sounding board, in addition to serving more practical economic purposes.

“Having someone who’s going through it with you that you also are living with, so that you could commiserate and compare notes and sort of go through that crucible together, I think is really important,” said Pasadena Assemblymember John Harabedian, who rooms with Bay Area Assemblymember Patrick Ahrens. “I think it makes it a lot more tolerable.”

THE FRESHMEN: Harabedian and Ahrens, who met at the legislative freshman orientation, aren’t the only freshmen pairing up: Assemblymember Sade Elhawary and state Sen. Sasha Renée Pérez, both from the Los Angeles area, bunk together while in Sacramento.

“I had an idea of who I wanted to be roommates with, and she was, like, No. 1 on my list,” Pérez said of Elhawary, whose family members are also her constituents.

When it appeared Elhawary would win her election, Pérez invited her to an Anderson .Paak concert at the Hollywood Bowl so the two could get to know each other as friends.

Pérez did the groundwork to find their apartment, which the pair took over from former Berkeley state Sen. Nancy Skinner. Elhawary and her mom served as the interior decorators.

Harabedian was initially going to live in a hotel until Ahrens invited him to move in.

“He asked if I wanted to, and he’s just a really easy person to get along with. I mean, he’s, like, the most likable guy in Sacramento,” Harabedian said. “He and I just clicked really quickly, and we had similar outgoing personalities.”

Although Ahrens is more of an early riser than Harabedian, the two go to an early-morning Orange Theory workout class with other lawmakers, which sometimes includes Elhawary.

The two have developed such a close friendship that Ahrens spent Father’s Day with Harabedian’s family.

Ahrens called them “the best roommate pair this Legislature has ever seen.”

“Much better than Mr. Berman and Mr. Gabriel,” he quipped.

THE BROMANCE: Assemblymembers Marc Berman and Jesse Gabriel are longtime roommates AND seatmates in the Assembly chamber. They’re perhaps the best-known legislative roommates — at least to members of the press corps, whose desks are directly behind theirs on the green carpet.

Friends encouraged the two to meet when Gabriel ran for his Los Angeles-area seat in a special election. He ended up sitting next to Berman, who represents a Bay Area-district.

“That was the beginning of the end,” Berman said. “The rest is history.”

Gabriel said there’s usually a night during the week when they’re working together at their apartment with sports on in the background. Having Berman as his roommate “has been one of the biggest blessings of my service in the Legislature,” Gabriel said, even though he’s a “neat freak.”

“If you think he gets fired up in committee or on the floor, you should see what happens when you leave a spoon on the counter.”

Berman confirmed this perception, saying Gabriel “does not do the dishes” and “eats all the food that he bans,” referring to the assemblymember’s legislation prohibiting the use of certain ingredients.

“He acts like he doesn’t know where the trash bins are, even though he parks next to them every day,” Berman said. “He pays his rent like three times a year, if I’m lucky. But my experience in Sacramento and as a legislator would not be nearly as great an experience as it’s been if he and I weren’t roommates.”

THE HOSTS: Central Coast Assemblymembers Dawn Addis and Gail Pellerin are known for hosting celebrations and gatherings for their colleagues at their Land Park house. Pellerin wanted the space for her goldendoodle, Darwin.

The two met when Addis began running for the Assembly in a newly-drawn district that includes parts of Santa Cruz. Pellerin succeeded former Assemblymember Mark Stone in a neighboring district.

“It fell in my lap in so many ways,” Addis said. “Friendship, housemate, great place to live. It’s made a very easy landing in Sacramento.”

The pair talked about Pellerin’s love of hosting everything from book clubs to baby showers. Assemblymember Tina McKinnor said she and her roommate, state Sen. Lola Smallwood-Cuevas, sometimes stop by for a glass of wine and the occasional dance party.

“It’s not uncommon to come home and there’s various legislators that are hanging out or popping by,” Addis said.

THE ODD COUPLE: McKinnor and Smallwood-Cuevas, who both represent the Los Angeles area, met on the campaign trail and got an apartment together after an unsatisfying year of hotel living.

They call themselves the “odd couple” in reference to a 1970s TV show about two people with opposite dispositions who end up living together. McKinnor is the more social one, with Smallwood-Cuevas describing herself as “a little grouchier and less talkative.”

“I have friends and people that stop by and Lola goes up into her room and goes to bed,” McKinnor said. “But sometimes they’re calling for, ‘Lola, come down!’”

Smallwood-Cuevas said McKinnor is the “Energizer bunny” who’s always cooking vegetables to encourage healthy eating.

“She cooks like, beets, and there are five Tupperwares of the beets in the fridge,” Smallwood-Cuevas said, “And then I say, well, let’s just order Fire Wings. And then the good thing is, she’s always down for that.”

SENDING IN REINFORCEMENTS: Gavin Newsom announced today he’s sending new teams of state police to major California cities — contrasting the effort with Donald Trump’s unilateral deployment of National Guard troops in Los Angeles and Washington, our Blake Jones reports.

The California Highway Patrol troopers will target crimes like auto theft and drug dealing in Los Angeles, San Diego, inland regions and the San Francisco Bay Area. Trump recently threatened to send Guard members to San Francisco, saying he would “clean up” the city.

Newsom insisted he was “not reacting and responding to anything” relating to Trump and emphasized the state’s deployment, unlike the president’s, was not being done over the objections of local governments.

“As it relates to the president in particular, he’s doing things to people, not with people, and it’s a point of profound, consequential contrast. He’s de facto militarizing American cities,” the governor told reporters.

HOMAN HITS BACK: Meanwhile, White House border czar Tom Homan bashed Newsom after the governor said that Immigration and Customs Enforcement act as President Donald Trump’s “private police force,” our Nicole Markus reports.

“Newsom is an embarrassment to the position he holds. Hard stop,” Homan told reporters. “If he had an ounce of integrity he’d be calling President Trump and thanking him for making LA safer. He’d be calling the head of ICE and thanking them for the thousands of criminals, public safety threats they’ve taken off the streets of California.”

Newsom’s comments during POLITICO’s “The California Agenda: Sacramento Summit” were the latest in a series of attacks he’s leveled recently against the president.

“When they’re done with this — all that funding and that ‘big beautiful betrayal’ allows more resources for this private police force that increasingly is showing a tendency not to swear an oath to the Constitution, but to the president of the United States,” Newsom said.

HITTING THE AIRWAVES: California voters will soon see a flood of television ads from those on both sides of the state’s redistricting ballot measure.

Ad-tracking firm AdImpact reported ’No on Prop 50 - Protect Voters First’ has spent $2.6 million on TV reservations.

The Prop 50 ballot measure committee, ‘Election Rigging Response Act,’ has bought $957,000 in reservations, according to the firm.
