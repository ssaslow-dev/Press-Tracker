Newsom signs California law banning 'ultra-processed foods' from school lunches

A law signed by California Gov. Gavin Newsom on Wednesday will change what food items are available in schools as part of an effort to increase nutrition standards in the state.

(Previous coverage in the video player above)

According to Newsom's office, he signed AB 1264, which provides a statutory definition of "ultra-processed foods" and will phase many of these foods out of school lunches by 2032. The governors office said the law, proposed by Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, D-Encino, is the first of its kind in the U.S.

The law will task state scientists with determining whether a product qualifies as an ultra-processed food (UPF) based on these factors:

    • Does the product includes additives that are banned, restricted or subject to warnings in other jurisdictions
    • Based upon scientific research, is the product or some of its ingredients linked to cancer, cardiovascular disease, metabolic disease, developmental harm, reproductive harm, obesity, type 2 diabetes etc.
    • Does the product contribute to food addiction
    • Is the product high in fat, sugar, or salt

Gabriel said in a statement that with Newsom's signature on his bill, "California has taken a historic step" toward protecting children.

“While Washington, DC, is paralyzed by inaction, California is once again leading the nation with a bipartisan, commonsense, science-based approach," Gabriel said
," Gabriel said. "Here in the Golden State, Democrats and Republicans are joining forces to ensure that students are receiving healthy, nutritious meals that are made from real foods, not harmful chemical additives."

In January, Newsom issued an executive order that involved investigation into how those ultra-processed foods put public health at risk.
