Getting Assemblymember Gabriel covered: a guide

What the coverage record shows works  ·  Data as of Nov 5, 2026  ·  Ratings show how sure each finding is

Eight things to do

  1. Send a release at every bill introduction and every signing, small bills included. Introductions with a release were covered 88% of the time, against 5% without one. Signings with a release were always covered (median 5 stories); without one, 58% were covered (median 1). Medium-high
  2. Stop sending releases that don't earn coverage. Honors and awards: 1 of 7 got any story. "Passes the Assembly" outside food safety: median 1 story. Housing: 24 releases, median 1. District and community: 18 releases, 18 stories in total. Make floor votes a social post instead. Medium
  3. Put the news in the title. Releases with "first", "landmark" or "nation" in the title were picked up 97% of the time (median 13 related stories), against 72% (median 2) for the rest. Releases that name a bill: 81% against 59%. Part of this is the subject itself. Medium
  4. Issue on Monday or Tuesday; avoid Wednesday and Thursday. Monday releases were picked up 95% of the time, Wednesday 59% and Thursday 71% (ordinary releases, 2021 on, food safety left out). Friday releases get picked up but the stories land about 3 days later. Avoid the end-of-session crush in the second week of September. Low-medium
  5. Give AP and CalMatters first looks on big news. Their stories travel: each AP story ran in about 20 other outlets, and each CalMatters story in about 7. One placement there is worth many single-outlet stories. High
  6. Make the Budget Chair role a story about him. 358 budget stories since 2024, but only 12% feature him and 5% are positive; there were 17 budget releases against 393 budget stories in all. Offer background briefings before the January proposal, the May Revise and the June deal, and one budget op-ed per cycle. Medium-high
  7. Keep a 60–120-word Gabriel quote in every release. Stories tied to a release quote him 69% of the time, against 35% for other stories. Quotes of that length get picked up most and are reused most. The 15 releases with no quote were picked up 47% of the time, but they were mostly weak kinds anyway. Medium
  8. Give the district and in-language press something they can use. District and Valley outlets carry 4% of his stories (157). Non-English outlets carry 11% (Spanish 179, Chinese 113, Korean 49), and 74% of those stories follow an English story rather than a release, because releases go out only in English. Add a translated quote on relevant bills for Sing Tao (rising: 10 stories in 2026), La Opinión, Telemundo 52, Univision 34 and Korea Daily, and a local angle for the LA Daily News and SFV Sun. Medium

Where the tracker undercounts

  • LA Times: 28 → 17 → 2 stories (2024 → 2026). It blocks the web search, so this is mostly missed stories, not lost coverage. Low
  • TV: 84 → 36 stories a year (2023 → 2026). Partly real (LA stations: KTLA 1, ABC7 LA 0 in 2026), partly segments the search can't see. Check against the office's booking log before setting goals. Low-medium
  • Spanish local press (Adelante Valle, La Opinión) looks quieter, but it came mostly through NewsBank, which has thinned. Don't present it as a real decline.
  • The newest weeks are about 80–85% complete until the monthly NewsBank download.

Track each quarter

  • Bills with a release at introduction and at signing (goal: all of his bills).
  • Releases picked up (now 75%; goal 80%+).
  • Budget stories that feature him (now 12%; goal 20%+).
  • District and Valley stories (now 4%; goal: a clear rise).
  • Stories that quote him (now 41%; goal: back above 50%).
  • LA TV and radio stories, compared with the office's own bookings.

How sure: High the pattern is clear and would hold with complete data. Medium the direction is right but the size may be off. Low worth testing, not a rule. The figures show what goes together, not proof of cause: big bills get both more releases and more coverage.

Sources: Coverage, Press releases and Bills tabs of the office tracker (fingerprint 3a98feb1), Nov 5, 2026. "Stories" counts originals only, leaving out verbatim release copies; reprints are counted separately. Tone is rated by Claude (the AI the tracker uses) with a written rubric.
