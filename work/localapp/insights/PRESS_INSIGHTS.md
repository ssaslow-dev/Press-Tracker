# Press coverage insights: Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel

Workbook: `AJG Coverage - NewsBank.xlsx`, saved 2026-11-05. Read-only analysis; nothing in the workbook was changed.

"Story" below means an Original row (one piece of reporting). "Reprint" means the same piece run again elsewhere (a wire story in another paper, a chain's sister papers, a Nexstar clip on another station). Verbatim reposts of his own releases are not counted as coverage. Counts for October–November 2026 are not final (see Caveats).

---

## 1. Summary: the eight most useful takeaways

1. **Food safety is the franchise.** One topic produces 23% of all stories (891 of 3,832) and 47% of all release-driven coverage. Every one of the 24 food-safety releases got coverage (median 20 stories each); everything else combined gets a median of 2 stories per release. AB 418 alone: 470 stories plus 609 reprints.
2. **Three in four releases get at least one story, but most get only one to three.** 236 official releases since 2018: 178 (75%) have at least one related story; 40 (17%) produced nothing at all. The median release earns 2 stories and 1 reprint.
3. **Releases tied to a bill milestone work; ceremonial and "passed the Assembly" releases don't.** Signing releases average 9 stories, introduction releases 9, "passes the Assembly/Senate" 6 (median 2; outside food safety, median 1). Honors and award releases: 1 of 7 got any story. District/community releases: 18 issued, 1 story each on average.
4. **A bill milestone with a release gets roughly 5 to 10 times the coverage of the same milestone without one.** Signed with a release: every one covered, median 5 stories. Signed without a release: 58% covered, median 1. Introduced with a release: 88% covered; without: 5%.
5. **The statewide political press is the core audience and it reports independently.** Politico (183 stories), Sacramento Bee (153), CalMatters (106), LA Times (105) lead all-time; only 10% of Politico's and 11% of CalMatters' stories trace back to a release. The Jewish press (Jewish Journal 104, J. 57, San Diego Jewish World 44, Jewish Insider 40) is the second pillar and the most favorable (about 40% positive).
6. **Coverage is mostly neutral and the negatives are concentrated.** 76% of rated stories are Neutral, 16% Positive, 4.5% Negative. Negative stories cluster on guns (18% of gun stories) and in a handful of outlets (California Globe 16, OC Register 9, gun-rights sites). 2026 has the highest positive share in years (23%), driven by national food-policy profiles.
7. **Reach and quoting are slipping even as volume holds.** Stories per year: 764 (2023), 714, 642, 607 (2026 to date). The share with a Gabriel quote fell from 52% (2023) to 41% (2026), and the national/international share from 27% to 18%, as more stories mention him only as Budget chair.
8. **Timing: September and October carry a third of the year.** Stories per month-of-year (2019–2026): September 586, October 540, March 430; November to February 150–250 each. Monday and Tuesday releases do best; Wednesday and Thursday releases do worst (competing Capitol news); Friday releases are picked up but the stories land later.

## 2. Press release performance

### How pickup was counted

- **Releases:** the Press releases tab, in-scope rows only (236 of 244): 199 from gabriel.asmdc.org (June 2018 – October 2026) and 37 Legislative Jewish Caucus releases from his chair tenure. 201 are press releases, 31 statements, 4 other (a letter, a constituent update, an event notice, a "What they're saying" roundup).
- **Related stories:** the tracker's release matcher (`code/release_related.json`, the same data behind the tab's "Related count"). A story counts as related to a release when it (a) ran or quoted the release text (a run of 25+ identical words), or (b) covered the same announcement in the release's coverage window (same bill and subject within about a week, same names and figures, or a roundup mention). Later coverage weeks afterwards and "possible" matches are not counted.
- **Pickup rate** = share of releases with at least one related story. **Stories** = related Originals; **reprints** = their reprints.
- **Copies** = verbatim reposts of his release (130 rows: Targeted News Service, US Fed News, San Diego Jewish World, Sierra Sun Times, PublicNow and similar). They are hidden in the tracker and counted separately, not as coverage.

**Reliability of the links.** The "ran or quoted the text" links are mechanical and reliable (High). The "same announcement" links were hand-checked at 95%+ on samples when built (Medium: a few stories about a bill will be credited to a release that merely coincided with the news). Only stories with saved text can be text-tested, so a few no-copy stories (LA Times, Politico, AP) that used release wording are under-counted. A release's count cannot exceed the tracker's own coverage, so the gaps described in Caveats (print, TV, newest weeks) apply here too.

### Other patterns

- **A bill in the release matters.** Releases naming a bill: 81% pickup vs 59% without (outside food safety 78% vs 54%; this difference is unlikely to be chance).
- **Joint releases with another legislator: no measurable gain.** 99 joint vs 137 solo: 76% vs 75% pickup; outside food safety joint releases average 3.7 stories vs 2.8 (median 2 vs 1), a difference well within chance. Releases with three or more legislators (caucus group releases) do worse: 58% pickup, 2 stories. Senator Wiener is the co-issuer on 34 (caucus releases).
- **Length:** releases over 700 words get coverage 85% of the time vs about 73% for shorter ones, a weak pattern that mostly reflects bigger announcements being longer.
- **Office vs caucus:** office releases 76% pickup, mean 5.9 stories; Jewish Caucus 73%, mean 2.5.
- **Who runs releases verbatim:** Targeted News Service (161 copies, all releases), US Fed News (41), San Diego Jewish World (16), PublicNow (16), Lassen County Times (13), Sierra Sun Times (12), Contra Costa News (8), East County Today (8), Van Nuys News Press (6). These are reach, not reporting.

### Patterns worth acting on

1. Put the release effort on signings and introductions, where a release roughly quintuples coverage (section 7). Treat "passed the Assembly" as a social-media post unless the vote itself is news.
2. Honors, ceremonial and bundle releases ("14 bills advance", "four bills signed") earn nothing in the press; if they must go out, pair them with a pitch to one Valley outlet rather than a general send.
3. Housing releases are frequent (24) and weak (median 1 story). Either attach them to a hard milestone or fold them into fewer, larger packages.
4. Avoid the second week of September for anything that can wait: the 2025-09-09 pair of "Legislature passes" releases sank in the end-of-session flood, while the same bills got coverage at signing a month later.

## 3. Best day of the week to issue a release

**What the data can and cannot say.** The tab records dates, not times, so "Friday afternoon vs Monday morning" cannot be tested. The weekday is also largely dictated by events: Saturday and Sunday releases (12 in total) are almost all governor's signings and legislative passage, and they are the biggest of all (mean 17 stories) for that reason, not because of the day. The comparison below therefore uses ordinary press releases from 2021 on, excluding food safety, so the big-event days don't swamp it (about 150 releases).

| Day issued | Releases | Got coverage | Stories per release (median / mean) | Reprints (mean) | Share of stories within 2 days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 20 | 95% | 3 / 5.0 | 3.9 | 72% |
| Tuesday | 42 | 76% | 2 / 4.0 | 5.0 | 68% |
| Wednesday | 29 | 59% | 2 / 2.4 | 2.5 | 67% |
| Thursday | 28 | 71% | 1.5 / 2.5 | 1.5 | 54% |
| Friday | 27 | 89% | 3 / 4.3 | 6.5 | 32% |

- Wednesday and Thursday releases do measurably worse (fewer stories; p = 0.03 against the other days). These are the Capitol's busiest days for competing news.
- Monday is the strongest day for pickup rate and speed; Friday matches it on pickup and stories but the coverage arrives later (median 3 days after the release vs 1 day for Monday/Tuesday; only a third of Friday-release stories appear within two days). Friday's reprint count is high because weekend editions re-run stories.
- Within single issues the ordering holds loosely (antisemitism: Friday 88% vs Mon–Thu 81%; guns: Friday 100% vs 75%), but the samples are 3–8 releases each.

**Strength of evidence:** Medium-Low. The Wednesday/Thursday dip is the only difference unlikely to be chance; the Monday and Friday advantages are suggestive (p = 0.16 and 0.39), there is no time-of-day data, and the weekday is confounded with what kind of news it was.

**Plain recommendation:** when the day is yours to choose, issue on Monday or Tuesday morning; avoid Wednesday and Thursday; use Friday only for news that benefits from a slower weekend build (features, local follow-ups), and expect the stories on Monday.

## 4. Releases with a Gabriel quote vs without

**How quotes were identified.** The tab's "Gabriel quoted" column (Yes on 221 of 236 in-scope releases) was checked against the release texts in `code/release_texts.json` by looking for a quoted passage with "Gabriel" within a sentence of it. The two agree on 213 of 236; the 10 "Yes" rows my search missed are caucus statements where the quote is attributed to "the Caucus" or to "Gabriel and Wiener" jointly, and the tab is right. Whether outlets used his words comes from the Coverage tab's Quoted column (Yes only when his own words appear in quotation marks) on the related stories.

Only 15 in-scope releases have no quote from him: the Giffords visit (2019), two COVID notices, four caucus statements, the diversity-caucuses statement on the LA City Council tapes, the Morgan Freeman school-food endorsement list, two "advances N bills" bundles, the private-judges bill passing, the hostages letter, and the 2026 "What they're saying" roundup.

| | No Gabriel quote | With a Gabriel quote |
|---|---|---|
| Releases | 15 | 221 |
| Got coverage | 47% | 77% |
| Stories per release (median / mean) | 0 / 1.5 | 2 / 5.6 |
| Same, excluding food safety | 0 / 1.6 (14 releases) | 2 / 3.3 (198) |
| Reprints (mean) | 1.6 | 6.3 |
| Statewide or national stories per release | 0.8 | 1.9 |
| First story the same day | 57% | 57% |
| Related stories that quote him (Quoted = Yes) | 42% | 66% |
| Related stories that reuse release wording | 44% | 45% |

- Excluding food safety, the pickup gap (43% vs 75%) is unlikely to be chance (p = 0.02); the story-count gap is suggestive (p = 0.16). Solo releases only: 38% vs 72%. Jewish Caucus releases only: 57% vs 77%.
- Across all coverage since 2021, release-linked stories quote him 69% of the time; stories with no release behind them 35%. The release quote is what gets him quoted.
- Quote length (among quoted releases, outside food safety): 1–60 words of his quote, 62% pickup; 61–120 words, 75%; over 120 words, 80%. A long quote slightly helps pickup but the stories reuse less of it (38% of related stories reuse release wording vs 74% for short quotes), so a punchy 60–120-word quote is the sweet spot.

**Confidence:** Medium. The direction is consistent across every cut, but there are only 15 unquoted releases and they are also disproportionately the weak kinds (bundles, letters, ceremonial), so the quote is partly a marker of a real announcement rather than the cause.

**Recommendation:** never send a release without a quotable 2–3 sentence line from him; keep the quote to about 60–120 words with one sentence that can stand alone, because that is the sentence the Bee, KCRA and the Jewish press lift.

## 9. Caveats: what the data cannot tell, and how to read the newest months

- **Newest weeks are incomplete.** The web search misses roughly 1 in 6 recent stories (print-only, wires, paywalled papers); those arrive with the monthly NewsBank download. Treat October 2026 as 80–85% complete and November as a placeholder (5 stories so far). About a quarter of 2026 stories came only from NewsBank, so any "this month vs last month" comparison before the refresh will understate the newest month.
- **TV and radio are undercounted,** especially LA stations and untitled segments; podcasts were only searched from 2026. The TV decline is real in direction but its size is uncertain.
- **Before 2018 the tracker is thin** (about 100 stories in 25 years) and 2018–2022 was reconstructed from archives, so earlier years' counts are floors.
- **Non-English coverage** was captured systematically only recently (25 stories added on 2026-11-03). Earlier non-English counts are floors, and Korean/Chinese dates can trail US dates by a day.
- **Dates:** 4% of stories carry an estimated or unknown date; 36 are undated and left out of the time charts. About 585 Page cells hold NewsBank scan positions rather than printed page numbers (irrelevant to these counts).
- **Reprint grouping** is text-based (25-word runs covering 90% of both texts). Wire stories in outlets whose text could not be saved (LA Times, AP, Politico) can appear as separate stories, which slightly inflates story counts and deflates reprint counts for those outlets.
- **Tone** is Claude's rating against a rubric, on Originals only, with straight news always Neutral and praise of the bill (not of him) counted as Positive. Read tone as "how the piece frames him," not as sentiment about the policy.
- **Release links** can only be made to stories in the tracker, use rule-based windows (text within 30 days, same-news within about a week), and credit a release when the story coincides with it. The matcher does not know whether a reporter actually read the release.
- **Releases** are only what is posted on gabriel.asmdc.org and the Jewish Caucus site. Media advisories, pitches, social posts and the Speaker's or Governor's releases quoting him are not in the tab, so "coverage without a release" sometimes had another trigger.
- **No times of day,** no reporter emails or pitch records, and no readership figures: "reach" here is a category label per outlet, not an audience number.
- Seventy-two stories have no Topic and 128 no Tone (no saved text); nine have no Reach label.

## Method (brief)

- **Tabs and columns used.** Coverage (Story ID, Original or reprint, Date, Outlet, Medium, Format, Reach, Language, Author 1–9, Topic, Bills, Tone, Role, Mention depth, Quoted, Press release, Data Source, Date source); Press releases (Release ID, Date, Title, Source, Release type, In scope, Issue, Gabriel quoted, Legislator 1–6, Other official 1, Bill 1–6, Word count, Copies (rows), Related count); Bills (Bill, Session, Date, Milestone); `code/release_related.json` for the per-story release links and lags; `code/release_texts.json` for the release bodies (quote check). Removed and Unlinked print pages were read only to confirm they hold nothing countable.
- **Coverage universe.** Coverage rows minus the 399 rows marked Press release = Yes (verbatim release copies, hidden in the tracker) and the 2 rows marked In Tracker? = No: 7,423 rows = 3,832 Originals (stories) + 3,591 Reprints. No "Reprint (unverified)" rows remain in this workbook. Reprints were counted by Story ID; reprint lag is the reprint's date minus the Original's.
- **Release analysis.** In-scope releases only. Related stories = release_related.json entries of the tier-1 types (text, nearcopy, quote, bill, topic, anchor, roundup, pre), which is what the tab's Related count holds; "later" and "possible" entries excluded; stories that are themselves release copies excluded (2 entries). Lags come from the file (story date minus release date). Pickup rate, stories, reprints, outlets, reach (Reach contains "Statewide" or "National"), TV/radio and language were aggregated per release and then by type, issue, year, weekday, joint (any Legislator column filled), bill (Bill 1 filled), word count and quote. Weekday, joint and quote comparisons were re-run excluding food safety; "p" values are two-sided permutation tests on mean stories per release (20,000 shuffles).
- **Op-eds:** Role = "Op-ed by Gabriel" plus bylines containing "Jesse Gabriel", minus release reposts bylined to him (Sierra Sun Times, Del Norte Triplicate). Follow-on: same-Topic Originals in the 14 days after vs before.
- **Milestones:** Bills tab rows from 2021 for Introduced (Assembly), Floor vote (Assembly/Senate), Passed both houses, Signed, Vetoed; stories = Originals whose Bills column names the bill, dated −1 to +7 days; "with a release" = an in-scope release naming the bill in Bill 1–6 dated −1 to +2 days.
- **Quotes in releases:** the tab's "Gabriel quoted" column, checked against release bodies with a regular expression for a quoted passage within 160 characters of "Gabriel"; quote length is the word count of such passages.
- **Checks made on surprising numbers:** the Saturday/Sunday release spike (all signing/passage days, so reported as event-driven); the LA Times 2026 drop (flagged as a possible capture gap rather than reported as fact); the 2023 outlet-count peak (204 first-time outlets, mostly one-off syndication of the Skittles story); the Sierra Sun Times "Jesse Gabriel" bylines (release copies, excluded from op-eds); the "Related count" definition (confirmed in `code/reltab.py` as tier-1 only).
