Anti-Israel Jewish protesters disrupt the start of California's legislative session

[Caption: Assemblymembers Heath Flora, left, and Josh Lowenthal, right, leave the Assembly chamber as pro-Palestinian, anti-Israel protesters calling for a ceasefire in Gaza disrupt the first day of the California legislative session in Sacramento, California, Jan. 3, 2024. (Credit: Rich Pedroncelli/AP)]

SACRAMENTO, California – Hundreds of American protesters calling for a ceasefire in the war between Israel and Hamas disrupted the first California legislative session of the year on Wednesday, forcing the state Assembly to adjourn just moments after it opened.

As lawmakers had just heard the opening prayer and had barely recited the Pledge of Allegiance, pro-Palestinian, anti-Israel Jewish protesters all wearing the same black T-shirt rose from their seats and began chanting "Ceasefire now" and "Let Gaza live." Several people unfurled banners from the chamber gallery that read: "Jews say 'never again for anyone.'"

At first, Jim Wood, a Democratic Assemblymember from Healdsburg who was presiding over the session, tried to continue despite the chanting. But after a while, he called a recess and adjourned the session a few minutes later. Nearly all lawmakers left the chamber.

The protesters cheered when state officials turned off the chamber lights, switching on their phone flashlights while continuing to chant their slogans, including a long call-and-response from the gallery. "We are Jewish and Californian; we invite you, members of the Assembly, to join us in demanding an immediate ceasefire," they shouted.

[Caption: Protesters reveal T-shirts with messages calling for a ceasefire in Gaza on the first day of the California legislative session in Sacramento, California, Jan. 3, 2024. (Credit: Rich Pedroncelli/AP)]

The office of Democratic Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas declined to comment.

James Gallagher, Assembly Republican leader, said the protesters had obstructed their work.

"We are trying to open our session. It's true that we probably didn't have much to do today. But if their goal is to disrupt the business of government, I don't think that's a good way to get your message across," he said. "We can't let them disrupt our business. We have work to do. We have urgent issues to deal with this year."

Wednesday's protest was organized by far-left and radical anti-Israel groups, including Jewish Voice for Peace, IfNotNow and the International Jewish Anti-Zionist Network.

Binya Kóatz, a Jewish teacher and artist who lives in the San Francisco area, said that "as long as the United States gives Israel a blank check," Americans have "not only the right but also the responsibility to stop carrying on as if nothing were happening."

Kóatz explained that the groups chose to protest at the California State Capitol because, although these lawmakers do not control the federal funds sent to Israel, "we know that state legislatures get the attention of their national counterparts in California, and getting this body to call for a ceasefire now can put California at the forefront of the national movement."

This is not the first time protesters calling for a ceasefire have disrupted events in California's capital. In November, protesters forced their way into a Sacramento convention hall, forcing the California Democratic Party to cancel some events at its endorsing convention. Last month, Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom canceled a Christmas tree lighting ceremony he was to attend in person after being warned that protesters planned to stage an action at the event.

The protesters did not disrupt the state Senate, which held its session as planned, during which lawmakers gave speeches in memory of former U.S. Sen. Dianne Feinstein, who died in September.

[Caption: After the lights were turned off, protesters calling for a ceasefire in Gaza use cellphone lights to demonstrate on the first day of the California legislative session in Sacramento, California, Jan. 3, 2024. (Credit: Rich Pedroncelli/AP)]

The war broke out on Oct. 7 when Hamas attacked Israel, invading the south of the country from the Gaza Strip with thousands of terrorists. The attack killed nearly 1,200 people, mostly civilians massacred by the terrorists who rampaged through communities in the south. More than 360 people were murdered at an outdoor rave, and more than 240 people were taken hostage and brought to Gaza. Israel responded by launching a military campaign aimed at destroying Hamas, removing it from power in Gaza and freeing the hostages.

In the United States, Wednesday was marked by disruptions at several state capitols. An emailed bomb threat sent to officials in several states led to the evacuation of offices or buildings in Connecticut, Georgia, Hawaii, Kentucky, Maine, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi and Montana. Other states, including Missouri, Maryland, Nebraska, New Hampshire, Oklahoma and Wyoming, received threats but did not evacuate.

The California legislative session, which began Wednesday and ends Aug. 31, is expected to focus on decisions about artificial intelligence (AI) and the huge budget deficit. But, as Wednesday's protest shows, the repercussions of the war between Israel and Hamas are bound to have an impact.

[Caption: Protesters calling for a ceasefire in Gaza gather in the Capitol rotunda on the first day of the California legislative session in Sacramento, California, Jan. 3, 2024. (Credit: Rich Pedroncelli/AP)]

The California Legislative Jewish Caucus sent a letter to state lawmakers on Wednesday asking for the creation of a committee to find policy solutions to protect the Jewish community.

"We have our own criticisms of Israel. We want the war to end," said Democratic state Sen. Scott Wiener, a member of the Jewish caucus. "We also know that the ceasefire resolutions we are seeing at the local level have sometimes veered into stoking anti-Jewish hatred, which is causing a great deal of fear in our community."

Assemblymember Jesse Gabriel, a Democrat from Los Angeles, said his 3-year-old child now has to pass through metal detectors to enter preschool, which is located in a local synagogue.

"The level of fear, anxiety and tension is unlike anything I've seen in my entire life," Gabriel said.
