This decision would leave California migrants without free health care starting in 2026

[Caption: Gavin Newsom opted to stop enrolling low-income migrants and will begin charging a monthly premium starting in 2027. (REUTERS/Jose Luis Gonzalez/File Photo)]

California Gov. Gavin Newsom said Wednesday that the state will stop enrolling new low-income migrants without legal status in the government-funded health care program starting in 2026. Beginning in 2027, those already enrolled will start paying a monthly premium.

The decision responds to higher-than-expected program costs and to economic uncertainty caused by federal tariff policies, The Associated Press (AP) reported.

The Medi-Cal program, which offers free health care to low-income adults regardless of immigration status, was implemented thanks to Newsom's push for universal health care in the nation's most populous state. However, the program's costs exceeded initial projections by $2.7 billion.

Which migrants will be affected by this new decision?

[Caption: Officials specified that children will not be affected. (AP Photo/Eric Gay, file)]

The freeze on new Medi-Cal enrollment will not affect minors, but starting in 2027, adults without legal status, or those who have it but are ineligible for federal Medicaid, will have to pay a $100 monthly premium. These measures are estimated to save California $5.4 billion for the 2028-2029 period.

"The state must take difficult but necessary steps to ensure fiscal stability and preserve the long-term viability of Medi-Cal for all Californians," the governor's office said in an announcement cited by AP.

The governor's administration stresses the need for these measures to maintain fiscal viability and ensure the sustainability of the Medi-Cal program for all Californians.

The governor's office emphasizes that the program's expansion, together with other factors such as rising pharmaceutical costs and the enrollment of older adults, generated a multibillion-dollar shortfall earlier this year, prompting the state to seek loans and authorize new funding.

AP reported that the state provides free health care to more than a third of its 39 million residents.

Fiscal pressure on California's budget has increased due to the combination of wildfires, tariff policies and the expansion of health care. Lawmakers anticipate a financial decline that could last for the next few years.

Newsom, who has blamed part of the deficit on President Donald Trump's tariff policies, estimates they have cost the state $16 billion in tax revenue.

The reasons behind this decision

[Caption: The decision seeks to cut the administration's spending. (AP Photo/Noah Berger)]

According to AP, the California governor will begin budget negotiations with lawmakers; however, it is not entirely clear where the Democrats who control the Legislature stand on the plan to halt enrollment of certain migrants in the free health care program.

Jesse Gabriel, chair of the budget committee, told the news agency before Newsom's proposal was announced that this would be "a very complex budget," saying they would have to make "difficult decisions."

In 2024, the governor and the Legislature reached an agreement to tap the state's rainy-day fund, cut spending and temporarily raise taxes on certain businesses. These plans sought to close a budget deficit of roughly $46.8 billion, according to AP.

Amid budget negotiations with the Democratic-majority Legislature, it remains to be seen how lawmakers will react to the governor's plan to freeze new enrollment in Medi-Cal. The outcome of these negotiations must be reflected in the final budget, which has to be approved before June.
