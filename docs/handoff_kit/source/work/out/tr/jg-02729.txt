Gavin Newsom announces measures to shield California from Donald Trump: what his plan is

The Golden State's governor signaled that he will defend its residents against the president-elect's promises; the Democratic leader made his position clear on certain controversies

December 12, 2024 · LA NACION

[Caption: Gavin Newsom and Donald Trump hold different positions on immigration, border security, the economy and more.]

California Gov. Gavin Newsom announced that he will do what is necessary to protect the state from possible policies of the incoming federal administration led by Donald Trump. In a special session convened in Sacramento, the governor and the state Legislature proposed creating a legal fund to safeguard fundamental rights, the environment, the state economy and programs that benefit millions of residents.

[Caption: The special session was held in the California Legislature.]

The summit, formally convened by Newsom, aimed to establish a litigation fund to strengthen the Golden State's legal resources against potential federal actions deemed harmful, the official statement said. Priority issues include protecting civil rights, reproductive freedom, access to clean water and clean air, and defending working families, including immigrant families.

The Democratic leader explained that the initiative seeks to ensure California's resilience against federal policies that could undermine the progress made in the state.

"California is a fundamental pillar of the country, from the economy to innovation to the protection of rights and freedoms. We are prepared to work with the incoming administration, but when there are excesses that threaten lives or rights, we will act," the governor said.

[Caption: Gov. Gavin Newsom seeks Joe Biden's support to secure key climate waivers before Donald Trump's return.]

Creation of a litigation fund for California

As the centerpiece of the strategy, Newsom and lawmakers proposed creating a fund of up to $25 million for the California Department of Justice and other state agencies. The money will be used to confront actions that may be considered unconstitutional, challenge illegal decisions in court and mitigate administrative harm.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta backed the proposal and said his office will be ready to act from day one of the new federal administration. "With billions of dollars at stake and our state's values at risk, we must be prepared," said Bonta, who said he will work closely with the governor and the Legislature.

Background from Trump's first term

Between 2017 and 2021, the first Trump administration tried several times to withdraw federal funds meant for California and to promote policies contrary to the state's values. During that period, the California Department of Justice filed 122 lawsuits against the federal government, amounting to roughly $42 million in litigation.

These legal actions not only protected key rights and programs but also generated tangible economic benefits for the state, Newsom's office noted.

[Caption: Donald Trump is known for his hardline policies against illegal immigration.]

For example, in one successful case, California recovered nearly $60 million in federal public safety grants. In another win, the defense of energy efficiency standards translated into estimated savings of more than $8 billion for consumers over the next three decades.

Working with the new Republican administration

Despite historical tensions, Newsom has stressed the importance of working with Trump where there is common ground. The governor recently traveled to Washington, D.C., to discuss with congressional and White House leaders key initiatives such as improving access to health care, mental health, fighting climate change and approving disaster funding.

"Where we can collaborate, we will. But where Californians' rights, safety and economic stability are in danger, we will be ready to respond," said state Senate President pro Tem Mike McGuire.

For his part, Assembly Budget Committee Chair Jesse Gabriel said that "we cannot afford to be caught unprepared."
