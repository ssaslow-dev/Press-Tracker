Newsom takes on ICE with a new law that protects California's immigrant families

The governor signed AB 1655; it changes CalWORKs rules so that households' assistance is not automatically reduced

October 5, 2026 · LA NACION

California has added a new measure in response to federal immigration policies. AB 1655 changes CalWORKs rules so that an immigration detention does not reduce the cash aid going to a household, with the goal of keeping families from losing part of their assistance when one of their members is taken into custody.

California changes CalWORKs rules in response to ICE detentions

Gov. Gavin Newsom on Sept. 29 signed AB 1655, authored by Assemblymember Isaac Bryan, which changes the rules of the California Work Opportunity and Responsibility to Kids program, known as CalWORKs.

The legislation provides that a child or other member of the family unit who is detained in a federal immigration detention facility may be considered temporarily absent from the home for the entire period of detention.

CalWORKs provides cash aid and other services to low-income families through a combination of federal, state and county funds. Until now, the rules covered situations of temporary absence, including a child's hospitalization, but the new legislation expressly adds federal immigration detention to those exceptions.

The key points of AB 1655:

- A child or any other member of the assistance unit who remains detained in a federal immigration detention facility will be considered temporarily absent from the home for as long as the detention lasts.
- The family may be eligible to receive CalWORKs assistance and services, provided it meets the conditions set by law.
- The other members of the family unit must meet the applicable eligibility requirements.
- If the detained person is outside California, they must intend to return to the state once released.
- The detained person must be legally eligible for CalWORKs benefits under all other criteria, regardless of their detention status.
- A written, sworn statement submitted by the applicant or recipient may be sufficient to establish that a member of the family unit is being held in an immigration facility.
- The state Department of Social Services may begin implementing these provisions through all-county letters or other written instructions while the corresponding regulations are adopted.

In this way, the detention of a family member will not automatically mean that their share of the cash assistance disappears while they remain in federal custody.

California sets when the new protection will take effect

AB 1655 does not take effect immediately. The new provisions will begin to apply on July 1, 2027, or on the date the Department of Social Services reports that the state system can implement the necessary automation, whichever is later.

The reference to the automated system is important because applying the new rule requires changing the way CalWORKs assistance is determined. For that reason, the legislation ties its effective date to the state system's technical capacity to process the new status of detained members.

While the final regulations are being adopted, the Department of Social Services may administer and implement the rule through written instructions to the counties. These will have the same force and effect as regulations until the corresponding rules are approved.
